File no. 0065October 11, 2026Money and power

High active share is linked to outperformance.

While many active funds underperform, those with the highest active share significantly outperform their benchmarks both before and after expenses. The evidence is mixed because results vary depending on the specific group of funds studied.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The results do not support the existence of skilled or informed mutual fund portfolio managers.

    “The results do not support the existence of skilled or informed mutual fund portfolio managers.”

    Study, 1997. Carhart, The Journal of Finance.

  2. Funds with the highest Active Share significantly outperform their benchmarks, both before and after expenses.

    “Active Share predicts fund performance: funds with the highest Active Share significantly outperform their benchmarks, both before and after expenses, and they exhibit strong performance persistence.”

    Study, 2009. Cremers & Petäjistö, Review of Financial Studies.

  3. Persistence is higher in sectors where concentration of assets under management is higher.

    “We find robust evidence that persistence is higher in sectors where concentration of assets under management is higher.”

    Study, 2006. Keswani & Stolin, The Journal of Financial Research.

  4. Nonindex funds with the lowest Active Share underperform their benchmarks.

    “Nonindex funds with the lowest Active Share underperform their benchmarks.”

    Study, 2009. Cremers & Petäjistö, Review of Financial Studies.

  5. Most active funds underperform passive benchmarks during the 2020 COVID-19 crisis.

    “We find that most active funds underperform passive benchmarks during the crisis, contradicting a popular hypothesis.”

    Study, 2020. Pástor & Vorsatz, The Review of Asset Pricing Studies.

The evidence is mixed.

Most active funds underperformed passive benchmarks during the 2020 covid-19 crisis. Nonindex funds with the lowest active share also underperform. However, specific characteristics like high active share are linked to outperformance.

Trust is not a strategy.

That may be why simply hiring a manager is not enough. The data suggests that specific fund characteristics matter more than the mere existence of a professional.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence is mixed, showing that while many active funds underperform, specific characteristics like high Active Share are linked to outperformance.

The findings vary significantly depending on the specific group of funds studied, such as those with high Active Share versus those with low Active Share.
