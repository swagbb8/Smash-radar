Who decides if you get the job?

File no. 0028October 8, 2026Modern society

Discrimination against the elderly and disabled is as severe as racial bias.

Research shows that hiring discrimination against candidates with disabilities, older candidates, and less physically attractive candidates is as severe as treatment based on racial or ethnic characteristics.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Selection bias in favor of male over female candidates was eliminated and, if anything, slightly reversed in sign starting in 2009 for mixed-gender and male-stereotypical jobs in our sample.

    “Instead, selection bias in favor of male over female candidates was eliminated and, if anything, slightly reversed in sign starting in 2009 for mixed-gender and male-stereotypical jobs in our sample.”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Schaerer et al., Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes.

  2. Hiring discrimination against candidates with disabilities, older candidates, and less physically attractive candidates seems equally severe as the unequal treatment of candidates with salient racial or ethnic characteristics.

    “Our meta-analysis shows that hiring discrimination against candidates with disabilities, older candidates, and less physically attractive candidates seems equally severe as the unequal treatment of candidates with salient racial or ethnic characteristics.”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Lippens, European Economic Review.

  3. In high-discrimination countries, white natives receive nearly twice the callbacks of nonwhites; in low-discrimination countries, white natives receive about 25 percent more.

    “In high-discrimination countries, white natives receive nearly twice the callbacks of nonwhites; in low-discrimination countries, white natives receive about 25 percent more.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Quillian et al., Sociological Science.

  4. Since 1989, whites receive on average 36% more callbacks than African Americans, and 24% more callbacks than Latinos.

    “Since 1989, whites receive on average 36% more callbacks than African Americans, and 24% more callbacks than Latinos.”

    Meta-analysis, 2017. Quillian et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America.

  5. Discrimination is significantly greater in the selection process for low-skilled than for high-skilled jobs.

    “Discrimination is significantly greater in the selection process for low-skilled than for high-skilled jobs.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Flage, International Journal of Manpower.

Your name and traits determine your callback rate.

Evidence confirms that names and characteristics associated with race, age, and disability result in fewer callbacks. This discrimination is significantly greater for low-skilled jobs.

The gatekeepers see you as a category.

That may be why your resume feels like a lottery. Your identity might be filtering you out before a human ever sees your skills.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A screen glows in a dark room.

You sit at your desk at 10 pm. You click send on an application. You wait for a notification that may never come because of a name on a page. You are a data point in a filter you cannot see.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that names and characteristics associated with race, age, and disability continue to result in fewer callbacks for many applicants.

The studies show that the level of discrimination varies significantly depending on the specific country, job type, and the amount of positive information provided in the application.
