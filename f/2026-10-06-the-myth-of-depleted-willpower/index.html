Does your willpower actually run out?

File no. 0014October 6, 2026Psychology

The ego depletion effect may be indistinguishable from zero.

When researchers correct for publication bias and small-study effects, the evidence for willpower running out disappears.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. When accounting for small-studies effects using the PET-PEESE method, the ego depletion effect was indistinguishable from zero.

    “A recent meta-analysis showed that after accounting for small-studies effects by using a newly developed method called PET-PEESE, the ego depletion effect was indistinguishable from zero.”

    Meta-analysis, 2017. Dang, Psychological Research.

  2. The evidence for the depletion effect is actually no different from zero when correcting for publication bias.

    “we found very strong signals of publication bias, along with an indication that the depletion effect is actually no different from zero.”

    Meta-analysis, 2014. Carter & McCullough, Frontiers in Psychology.

  3. Preregistered analyses did not find evidence for a depletion effect.

    “Confirmatory Bayesian meta-analyses using an informed-prior hypothesis (δ = 0. 30, SD = 0. 15) found that the data were 4 times more likely under the null than the alternative hypothesis.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Vohs et al., Psychological science.

  4. The overall pooled effect of cognitive load and ego depletion on moral judgments did not reach statistical significance.

    “The overall pooled effect was in the direction predicted by the mDPM, but did not reach statistical significance.”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Rehren, Frontiers in Psychology.

  5. Findings provide preliminary support for the ego-depletion effect and strength model hypotheses.

    “Findings provide preliminary support for the ego-depletion effect and strength model hypotheses.”

    Meta-analysis, 2010. Hagger et al., Psychological bulletin.

The evidence is highly conflicted.

While some older studies show support for the depletion effect, large-scale preregistered tests and bias corrections often fail to find it. Different methods produce different outcomes.

Your willpower might not be a finite resource.

That may be why you feel capable of making decisions even after a long day. The idea of a depleted battery might be a narrative rather than a biological reality.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence is highly conflicted, with large-scale preregistered tests and bias corrections often failing to find a significant depletion effect.

The results are complicated by the fact that different meta-analysis methods and the specific types of depleting tasks used can produce different outcomes.
