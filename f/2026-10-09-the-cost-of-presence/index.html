Your brain is being hijacked

Does your phone drain your brain when you are not using it?

File no. 0057October 9, 2026Technology

Your phone drains your brain even when it is silent.

The mere presence of a smartphone reduces available cognitive capacity. This effect is most significant for those with high smartphone dependence.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The mere presence of a smartphone reduces available cognitive capacity, even when people successfully avoid the temptation to check them.

    “Results from two experiments indicate that even when people are successful at maintaining sustained attention—as when avoiding the temptation to check their phones—the mere presence of these devices reduces available cognitive capacity.”

    Experiment, 2017. Ward et al., Journal of the Association for Consumer Research.

  2. A meta-analysis found that the mere presence of a smartphone negatively impacts working memory capacity, but has no significant effect on other cognitive functions.

    “Working memory capacity was negatively impacted by the mere presence of a smartphone, while results for the remaining cognitive functions produced null summary effects.”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Parry, Media Psychology.

  3. Participants with higher positive urgency showed increased cognitive interference in a silent-mode smartphone condition compared to a turned-off smartphone condition.

    “The results showed that participants with higher positive urgency presented increased cognitive interference (reflected by poorer task performance) in the "silent-mode smartphone" condition compared with participants in the "turned-off smartphone" condition.”

    Experiment, 2019. Canale et al., Scientific Reports.

  4. The cognitive costs of a smartphone's presence are highest for people who have a high level of smartphone dependence.

    “Moreover, these cognitive costs are highest for those highest in smartphone dependence.”

    Experiment, 2017. Ward et al., Journal of the Association for Consumer Research.

  5. An experiment on participants aged 20-34 showed that the mere presence of a smartphone results in lower cognitive performance.

    “The results of the conducted experiment imply that the mere presence of a smartphone results in lower cognitive performance, which supports the hypothesis of the smartphone presence using limited cognitive resources.”

    Experiment, 2023. Skowronek et al., Scientific Reports.

The evidence on this effect is mixed.

While some studies show reduced cognitive capacity, a meta-analysis found no significant effect on many other cognitive functions. Research in this area has poor statistical power.

The honest summary

The evidence is mixed, with some studies showing reduced cognitive capacity and others finding no significant impact on performance.

The research is characterized by substantial methodological heterogeneity and generally poor statistical power.
