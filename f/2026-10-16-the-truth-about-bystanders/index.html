Will you stay still or step in?

File no. 0098October 16, 2026Psychology

Danger makes people act.

Bystanders were 19 times more likely to intervene in real-life conflicts when danger was present than when it was not. The evidence is mixed.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. In real-life conflicts recorded by cameras, bystanders were 19 times more likely to intervene when danger was present than when it was not.

    “In the presence of danger, bystanders were 19 times more likely to intervene than in the absence of danger.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Lindegaard et al., Social Psychological and Personality Science.

  2. The bystander effect was attenuated when situations were perceived as dangerous, perpetrators were present, and the costs of intervention were physical.

    “The bystander effect was attenuated when situations were perceived as dangerous (compared with non-dangerous), perpetrators were present (compared with non-present), and the costs of intervention were physical (compared with non-physical).”

    Meta-analysis, 2011. Fischer et al., Psychological Bulletin.

  3. The presence of other people in a critical situation reduces the likelihood that an individual will help.

    “Research on bystander intervention has produced a great number of studies showing that the presence of other people in a critical situation reduces the likelihood that an individual will help.”

    Meta-analysis, 2011. Fischer et al., Psychological Bulletin.

  4. School-based programs increased bystander intervention both on a practical and statistically significant level.

    “Nevertheless, this meta-analysis indicated that programs increased bystander intervention both on a practical and statistically significant level.”

    Meta-analysis, 2012. Polanin et al., School Psychology Review.

  5. Bystander naloxone administration and overdose education programs are associated with increased odds of recovery.

    “Empirical evidence in the research literature suggests that bystander naloxone administration and overdose education programs are associated with increased odds of recovery”

    Meta-analysis, 2015. Giglio et al., Injury Epidemiology.

  6. Bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation increases the survival of out-of-hospital cardiac arrests.

    “Based on currently available evidence, the findings of this meta-analysis suggest that BCPR increases the survival of OHCAs”

    Meta-analysis, 2018. Song et al., Scandinavian Journal of Trauma Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine.

The bystander effect is not absolute.

While more people usually reduce the chance of help, this effect is smaller in dangerous situations. It weakens when the risk of physical harm is clear and the perpetrator is visible.

Your instinct is tied to risk.

That may be why you feel paralyzed in a crowd of strangers, but your body might react differently if the threat becomes immediate and physical.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while the 'bystander effect' exists, it is significantly mitigated by the presence of danger and specific situational factors.

The finding that danger increases intervention is based on a specific analysis of 80 interpersonal conflicts recorded by public surveillance cameras.
