Does your multitasking actually cost you more than you think?

File no. 0177October 26, 2026The future

The number of tasks you supervise does not increase switch costs.

Research shows that the quantity of tasks being supervised does not influence the cost of switching between them. This evidence is mixed regarding the link between subjective cognitive failure and performance.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. There was not definitive evidence regarding switching, with 34 effect sizes and a BF10 of .50, which is two times greater evidence for no relationship.

    “whereas there was not definitive evidence regarding switching (34 effect sizes, μ = −. 06, BF10 = . 50, i.e. , two times greater evidence for no relationship).”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Goodhew & Edwards, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review.

  2. Task load, as manipulated by the number of robots to be supervised, did not influence switch costs.

    “Task load, as manipulated by the number of robots to be supervised, did not influence switch costs.”

    Experiment, 2010. Squire & Parasuraman, Ergonomics.

  3. Children with ASD do not show difficulties on an ecological valid switch task, but have difficulty disengaging from an emotional task set.

    “Children with ASD do not show difficulties on an ecological valid switch task, but have difficulty disengaging from an emotional task set.”

    Study, 2012. Vries & Geurts, Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders.

  4. Cognitive load makes it harder for people to predictively control their movements, with 48% of participants able to do so under a cognitively loading task compared to 70% in the no-load condition.

    “In Experiment 2, 48% of the participants were able to predictively control their movements with a cognitively loading task, compared with 70% in the no-load condition.”

    Experiment, 2017. Levy‐Tzedek, Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience.

  5. Switch cost may last for between 20 s to 5 min.

    “switch cost may last for between 20 s to 5 min;”

    Study, 2024. Xu et al., Transportation Research Part F Traffic Psychology and Behaviour.

  6. Participants were faster to perform the same task than when they chose to switch between different task actions.

    “Participants were faster to perform the same task than when they chose to switch between different task actions.”

    Experiment, 2010. Squire & Parasuraman, Ergonomics.

Cognitive load affects movement, not just thought.

High cognitive load makes it harder to predictively control your movements. When you switch between different task actions, you are slower than when performing the same task.

The cost of switching is not immediate.

A switch cost may last for between 20 seconds and 5 minutes. That may be why you feel a lingering lag after moving between different types of work.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A quiet office at 3 p.m.

You close a spreadsheet and open a video call. You sit for a few moments, staring at the screen before your hands move to type again.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that cognitive load and specific task environments impact switching and movement control, but the relationship between subjective cognitive failure and switching is not established.

The research is limited by a lack of definitive evidence connecting subjective cognitive failure to objective switching performance and a lack of consensus on respiratory variability as a reliable index of cognitive load.
