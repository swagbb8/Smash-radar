Is your kindness a biological defense?

File no. 0342November 28, 2026Psychology

Social threat responses can increase your risk of disease.

A multilevel biological threat response to social situations can increase risk for viral infections and inflammation-related disease conditions when sustained.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. When sustained, a multilevel biological threat response to social situations can increase an individual's risk for viral infections and several inflammation-related disease conditions.

    “When sustained, however, this multilevel biological threat response can increase individuals' risk for viral infections and several inflammation-related disease conditions that dominate present-day morbidity and mortality.”

    Review, 2020. Slavich, Annual Review of Clinical Psychology.

  2. Women may show heightened amygdala reactivity to threatening stimuli and may consciously register fear more strongly via anterior cingulate activity.

    “Neuropsychological evidence is not yet conclusive but suggests that women show heightened amygdala reactivity to threatening stimuli, may be better able to exert prefrontal cortical control over emotional behaviour and may consciously register fear more strongly via anterior cingulate activity.”

    Review, 2013. Campbell, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B Biological Sciences.

  3. Parents who declined to consent to clinical research had more anxiety about their decision and found it harder to make than those who consented.

    “Parents who declined consent had a relatively higher socioeconomic status, had more anxiety about their decision, and found it harder to make their decision compared with consenting parents”

    Randomized trial, 2013. Hoberman et al., Archives of Pediatrics and Adolescent Medicine.

  4. Humans have a neurocognitive and immunologic ability to symbolically represent and respond to potentially dangerous social situations, which is critical for survival.

    “This neurocognitive and immunologic ability for humans to symbolically represent and respond to potentially dangerous social situations is ultimately critical for survival.”

    Review, 2020. Slavich, Annual Review of Clinical Psychology.

  5. Consenting parents had higher levels of trust and altruism and exhibited low decisional uncertainty.

    “consenting parents, who had higher levels of trust and altruism, perceived the potential for enhanced care, reflected better understanding of randomization, and exhibited low decisional uncertainty.”

    Randomized trial, 2013. Hoberman et al., Archives of Pediatrics and Adolescent Medicine.

Your body reacts to social situations as physical threats.

Humans have a biological system to symbolically represent and respond to dangerous social situations. This system is critical for survival but has physical health consequences.

Parental altruism may be linked to lower anxiety.

Parents who show higher levels of trust and altruism exhibit low decisional uncertainty. This suggests that what looks like humility might be a biological byproduct of lower anxiety.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that parental altruism is associated with lower anxiety and higher trust, but it also establishes that humans have a biological system for responding to social threats that can have physical health consequences.

The sources do not provide a direct causal link between anxiety and the specific personality trait of 'humility'.
