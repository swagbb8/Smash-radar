The future is closer than you think

Can a machine hear your thoughts before you speak?

File no. 0030October 8, 2026The future

A brain implant decodes attempted speech into 78 words per minute.

Cortical activity from a person with paralysis is translated into text or audio at various speeds. Systems range from 15.2 to 78 words per minute.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. A neuroprosthesis achieved 90.2% accuracy using a 125,000-word vocabulary after 1.4 additional hours of system training on the second day.

    “On the second day, after 1. 4 additional hours of system training, the neuroprosthesis achieved 90. 2% accuracy using a 125,000-word vocabulary.”

    Study, 2024. Card et al., New England Journal of Medicine.

  2. A person with ALS and severe dysarthria used a speech neuroprosthesis to communicate in self-paced conversations at a rate of approximately 32 words per minute for more than 248 cumulative hours.

    “the participant used it to communicate in self-paced conversations at a rate of approximately 32 words per minute for more than 248 cumulative hours.”

    Study, 2024. Card et al., New England Journal of Medicine.

  3. A participant's attempted speech was decoded at 62 words per minute, which is 3.4 times as fast as the previous record.

    “Our participant's attempted speech was decoded at 62 words per minute, which is 3. 4 times as fast as the previous record”

    Study, 2023. Willett et al., Nature.

  4. A multimodal speech-neuroprosthetic approach demonstrated accurate and rapid large-vocabulary decoding with a median rate of 78 words per minute and median word error rate of 25%.

    “For text, we demonstrate accurate and rapid large-vocabulary decoding with a median rate of 78 words per minute and median word error rate of 25%.”

    Study, 2023. Metzger et al., Nature.

  5. A person with anarthria and spastic quadriparesis caused by a brain-stem stroke had sentences decoded from cortical activity at a median rate of 15.2 words per minute with a median word error rate of 25.6%.

    “We decoded sentences from the participant's cortical activity in real time at a median rate of 15. 2 words per minute, with a median word error rate of 25. 6%.”

    Study, 2021. Moses et al., New England Journal of Medicine. This shows a link, not a cause.

  6. A high-performance intracortical BCI was tested by three clinical trial participants with paralysis and exceeded previous iBCIs by a factor of 1.4-4.2 in typing rate.

    “For all three participants, performance exceeded previous iBCIs (Bacher et al. , 2015; Jarosiewicz et al. , 2015) as measured by typing rate (by a factor of 1. 4-4. 2)”

    Clinical trial, 2017. Pandarinath et al., eLife.

Cortical activity becomes text.

Neuroprostheses decode the brain's signals. One system achieved 90.2% accuracy with a 125,000-word vocabulary. Another system reached a median rate of 15.2 words per minute.

The boundary between thought and speech is thinning.

That may be why the distinction between internal thought and external communication is becoming a technical variable. Your private internal monologue might soon have a direct digital output.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A silent room at night.

You sit in your chair and think of a word. In the past, that word stayed in your head. Now, a machine can catch it before you speak it aloud.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that speech neuroprostheses can decode cortical activity into words and sentences for people with paralysis, with some systems reaching high accuracy and conversational speeds.

The performance of these systems varies significantly across different studies, with word rates ranging from 15.2 to 78 words per minute.
