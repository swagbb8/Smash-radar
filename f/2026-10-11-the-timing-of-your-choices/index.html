Do you choose your actions or just witness them?

File no. 0070October 11, 2026The human brain

Your brain starts movements before you feel the choice.

Brain activity can be decoded several seconds before you become consciously aware of your decision.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The outcome of free decisions can be decoded from brain activity several seconds before reaching conscious awareness.

    “we demonstrated using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) that the outcome of free decisions can be decoded from brain activity several seconds before reaching conscious awareness.”

    Study, 2011. Bode et al., PLoS ONE.

  2. Free will can exist in the form of a veto of voluntary movements.

    “Notwithstanding this, free will is not impossible; it can exist in the form of free that is, of a veto of voluntary movements.”

    Experiment, 2011. Seifert, Americanae (AECID Library).

  3. Predictive activity patterns recorded before a decision became more stable as they got closer to the time of the conscious decision.

    “we show that predictive activity patterns recorded before a decision was made became increasingly stable with increasing temporal proximity to the time point of the conscious decision.”

    Study, 2011. Bode et al., PLoS ONE.

  4. The claim that the timing of readiness potential results tips the scales toward either denying or affirming free will cannot be justified from a neurological or philosophical perspective.

    “it is shown that the claim that the results on the timing of readiness potential tip the scales in favour of one or the other view cannot be justified - neither from a neurological, nor from a philosophical perspective.”

    Experiment, 2009. Batthyány, PhilPapers (PhilPapers Foundation).

  5. The link between the Libet paradigm and free will or moral responsibility is not clear.

    “While the Libet paradigm may be useful for examining how stimuli affect temporal judgments, the link between this and free will or moral responsibility is not clear.”

    Experiment, 2018. Saigle et al., AJOB Neuroscience.

  6. The readiness potential is a slow buildup of electrical potential at the scalp that is linked to neural activity involved in movement preparation.

    “The readiness potential (RP), a slow buildup of electrical potential recorded at the scalp using electroencephalography, has been associated with neural activity involved in movement preparation.”

    Review, 2021. Schurger et al., Trends in Cognitive Sciences.

The signals build before the thought.

Electrical potential builds at the scalp as your brain prepares for movement. These patterns become more stable as the time of your conscious decision nears.

Agency remains a subject of debate.

Whether this timing denies free will is not settled. It may be compatible with your sense of agency or a conscious veto of voluntary movements.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that brain activity precedes conscious decisions, but researchers disagree on whether this implies a lack of free will or if it can be reconciled with conscious agency.

The link between neural timing and abstract concepts like free will or moral responsibility remains unclear and is subject to ongoing critique regarding methodology and interpretation.
