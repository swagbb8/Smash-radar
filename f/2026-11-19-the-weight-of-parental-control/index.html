Is strict parenting the only path to behavior problems?

File no. 0304November 19, 2026Psychology

Strict parenting is linked to internalizing symptoms.

Harsh control and psychological control are positively associated with adolescent anxiety, depression, and suicidal ideation. These findings are mixed and come from different theoretical backgrounds.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Permissive parenting style is linked to internalizing behavior problems with a correlation of r= . 12, p<. 001.

    “Permissive and internalizing (r= . 12, p<. 001), externalizing (r= . 12, p<. 001)”

    Study, 2011. Alizadeh et al., Asian Social Science.

  2. Children have more internalizing and externalizing problems when they perceive their parents as more dissimilar in parenting styles.

    “To conclude, children have more internalizing and externalizing problems when they perceive their parents as more dissimilar in parenting styles.”

    Study, 2012. Berkien et al., European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry.

  3. Authoritarian parenting style of mothers and fathers is a significant predictor of internalizing and externalizing behavior problems in children.

    “Furthermore, results demonstrated only authoritarian parenting style of mothers and fathers as a significant predictor of internalizing and externalizing behavior problems in children.”

    Study, 2011. Akhter et al., Pakistan Journal of Psychological Research.

  4. Harsh control and psychological control by parents are positively associated with adolescent anxiety, depression, and suicidal ideation.

    “Conversely, psychological control and harsh control by parents are positively associated with adolescent anxiety, depression, and suicidal ideation.”

    Systematic review, 2019. Gorostiaga et al., International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

The source of the problem is complex.

Authoritarian parenting predicts behavior problems. However, permissive parenting is also linked to internalizing issues. Children also show more problems when they perceive parents have dissimilar styles.

Consistency matters more than intensity.

That may be why the perception of a united front is significant. When parents have dissimilar styles, children show increased problems. The pressure of harsh control adds another layer of internal distress.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that strict and harsh parenting styles are linked to internalizing symptoms, but it also highlights that permissive parenting and parental dissimilarity contribute to these problems.

The reported findings are inconsistent and the studies in question have been conducted from different theoretical backgrounds.
