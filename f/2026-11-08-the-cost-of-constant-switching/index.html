Your brain is being hijacked

Does multitasking actually lower your IQ?

File no. 0252November 8, 2026Health and environment

Multitasking may not lower your functional IQ.

The evidence on whether these habits consistently reduce functional IQ is mixed and inconsistent.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Bilinguals did not show a reduced n-1 shift cost and did not show an enhanced n-2 repetition cost compared to monolinguals in one task-switching experiment.

    “However, we did not observe such results. Our findings suggest that previous results cannot be replicated and that the n-2 repetition cost is another index that shows no reliable bilingualism effect.”

    Experiment, 2016. Branzi et al., Bilingualism Language and Cognition.

  2. The prefrontal cortex (PFC) can be used to quantify and classify mental workload with up to 78% accuracy for single-trial discrimination of three levels of workload.

    “The results show up to 78% accuracy for single-trial discrimination of three levels of workload from each other.”

    Experiment, 2014. Herff et al., Frontiers in Human Neuroscience.

  3. A higher rate of everyday language switches was related to a smaller mixing cost in errors in a set shifting task.

    “Most consistent effects were found for the set shifting task where a higher rate of everyday language switches was related to a smaller mixing cost in errors.”

    Study, 2011. Soveri et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

  4. Bilinguals demonstrated a smaller mixing effect (global switch cost) than monolinguals, indicating better ability to reconfigure stimulus-response associations.

    “Monolinguals and bilinguals performed similarly when switching between tasks (local switch cost) in a mixed-task block, but bilinguals demonstrated a smaller mixing effect (global switch cost) than monolinguals”

    Study, 2014. Wiseheart et al., Bilingualism Language and Cognition.

  5. Participants in a high load condition walked more slowly and performed the counting task more slowly and with more errors than those in the low load condition.

    “Participants in the high load condition walked more slowly and performed the counting task more slowly and with more errors than those in the low load condition.”

    Experiment, 2013. Hill et al., Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting.

  6. Concussed adolescents exhibited a significantly greater switch cost on the Task-Switching Test (P = 0.038, mean difference value = 38 ms) compared to control subjects.

    “Compared with control subjects, the concussed group exhibited a significantly greater switch cost on the Task-Switching Test (P = 0.038, mean difference value = 38 ms)”

    Observational study, 2013. Howell et al., Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise.

Your prefrontal cortex tracks the workload.

High mental workload can be classified with up to 78% accuracy using the prefrontal cortex. Task switching increases workload and can lead to more errors.

Efficiency comes with a cost.

That may be why you feel slower or make more mistakes when handling multiple tasks. Your brain is actively reconfiguring associations to keep up.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A phone sits on your desk.

You type a single email while listening to a podcast and checking a news feed. You feel productive, but your pace slows down.

The honest summary

The sources show that multitasking and task-switching increase prefrontal cortex workload and can lead to more errors, but they do not provide a definitive measure of a functional IQ drop.

The studies vary significantly in methodology, ranging from small-scale fNIRS experiments to observational studies on head trauma and bilingualism.
