Is your phone making you less present?

File no. 0043October 8, 2026Human relationships

Phubbing harms relationships through exclusion and less intimacy.

The damage to your connection with a partner is not primarily about conflict or jealousy. It is driven by feelings of exclusion and a lack of responsiveness.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Conflict and jealousy are not the primary mechanism through which partner phubbing results in reduced relationship satisfaction.

    “In contrast to previous work, this suggests that conflict and jealousy are not the primary mechanism through which pphubbing results in reduced relationship satisfaction.”

    Observational study, 2021. Beukeboom & Pollmann, Computers in Human Behavior. This shows a link, not a cause.

  2. The conditional effect of parental phubbing on adolescents' life satisfaction was significant among the preoccupied teens and the fearful teens but not significant among the secure teens and the dismissing teens.

    “Overall, the conditional effect of parental phubbing on adolescents' life satisfaction was significant among the preoccupied teens and the fearful teens but not significant among the secure teens and the dismissing teens.”

    Study, 2020. Liu et al., Child Care Health and Development.

  3. Partner phubbing is linked to lower well-being through romantic jealousy, but only among anxiously attached individuals.

    “Study 3 employs a survey and finds that partner phubbing has downstream negative effects on well-being through romantic jealousy, but only among anxiously attached individuals.”

    Experiment, 2021. David & Roberts, Journal of Social and Personal Relationships.

  4. By involving and informing a partner about one's phone activities, it is possible to reduce feelings of exclusion, maintain more responsiveness and intimacy in the conversation, and consequently reduce detrimental relationship effects.

    “This means that by involving and informing a partner about one's phone activities, it is possible to reduce feelings of exclusion, maintain more responsiveness and intimacy in the conversation, and consequently reduce detrimental relationship effects.”

    Observational study, 2021. Beukeboom & Pollmann, Computers in Human Behavior.

  5. Partner phubbing and attachment anxiety have an interactive effect on jealousy, which then leads to relationship satisfaction.

    “Study 2 finds that partner phubbing and attachment anxiety have an interactive effect on jealousy, which then leads to relationship satisfaction.”

    Experiment, 2021. David & Roberts, Journal of Social and Personal Relationships.

  6. The link between partner phubbing and relationship satisfaction is mediated by feelings of exclusion, less perceived partner responsiveness, and less intimacy.

    “Results of both studies demonstrate that the link between partner phubbing and relationship satisfaction was mediated by feelings of exclusion, less perceived partner responsiveness, and less intimacy.”

    Observational study, 2021. Beukeboom & Pollmann, Computers in Human Behavior.

Attachment styles change how you feel.

The negative effects of phubbing on well-being are significant for individuals with preoccupied or fearful attachment styles. It may not affect those with secure or dismissing styles.

Transparency changes the dynamic.

That may be why involving your partner in your phone activities reduces feelings of exclusion. Sharing the screen can maintain intimacy and reduce detrimental effects on your bond.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that partner phubbing consistently harms relationship quality, primarily through feelings of exclusion and reduced intimacy rather than just conflict.

The effects of phubbing are often moderated by individual psychological factors like attachment styles and specific behaviors like shared phone use.
