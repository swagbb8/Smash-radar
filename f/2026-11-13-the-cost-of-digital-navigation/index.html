Where does your internal map go?

File no. 0278November 13, 2026Technology

GPS use is linked to lower hippocampal connectivity.

Using GPS navigation in AR glasses for three months showed a decrease in functional coupling between the hippocampus and associated brain regions. The evidence on this is emerging.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The experimental group showed a decrease in functional coupling between the hippocampus and associated brain regions after three months of using GPS navigation in AR glasses, while the control group showed an increase.

    “the experimental group showed FC decrease after three months of using GPS navigation implemented in AR glasses in contrast to FC increase in the control group without such intervention.”

    Experiment, 2018. Fajnerová et al., BioMed Research International.

  2. No effect of using GPS navigation in AR glasses was observed on navigational abilities.

    “Importantly, no effect of intervention on navigational abilities was observed.”

    Experiment, 2018. Fajnerová et al., BioMed Research International.

  3. No significant differences emerged between experts and novices, nor females and males, for the retrieval of spatial information presented on 2D maps with a simple design and content.

    “no significant differences emerged between neither experts and novices, nor females and males for the retrieval of spatial information presented on 2D maps with a simple design and content.”

    Experiment, 2018. Keskin et al., Journal of Eye Movement Research.

  4. Analysis shows that paths tend to be drawn more frequently at first but soon decrease in frequency in favour of landmarks.

    “Analysis shows that paths tend to be drawn more frequently at first but soon decrease in frequency in favour of landmarks.”

    Study, 2007. Huynh & Doherty, Cartographica The International Journal for Geographic Information and Geovisualization.

  5. Nodes, boundaries, and districts tend to be drawn throughout the drawing process but are much less frequent.

    “Nodes, boundaries, and districts tend to be drawn throughout the drawing process but are much less frequent.”

    Study, 2007. Huynh & Doherty, Cartographica The International Journal for Geographic Information and Geovisualization.

Brain changes do not equal lost skills.

While brain connectivity changes, researchers observed no effect on actual navigational abilities. The study on connectivity was limited to three months and did not observe any impact on cognitive skills.

Your brain maps are changing.

That may be why your internal sense of place feels different. Your brain might be prioritizing external data over internal structural connections.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The familiar drive home.

You sit in the passenger seat. You watch the blue line move on the screen. You do not look at the landmarks outside the window. You do not think about the turns. You just follow the line to your front door.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that technology-assisted navigation is associated with measurable changes in brain connectivity but does not currently show a measurable degradation in spatial navigation skills.

The study on hippocampal connectivity was limited to a three-month period and did not observe any impact on actual cognitive navigational abilities.
