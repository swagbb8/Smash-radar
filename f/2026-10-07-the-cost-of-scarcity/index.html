Does lacking money change how you think?

File no. 0020October 7, 2026Money and power

Poverty may reduce your cognitive capacity.

Concerns about lack of money consume mental resources, which can lead to suboptimal decisions and behaviors.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Experimental results do not find that mental and financial scarcities significantly impact test performance.

    “Experimental results do not find that mental and financial scarcities significantly impact test performance.”

    Experiment, 2015. Graves, USF Scholarship Repository (University of San Francisco).

  2. Poverty itself reduces cognitive capacity because poverty-related concerns consume mental resources.

    “Instead, it appears that poverty itself reduces cognitive capacity. We suggest that this is because poverty-related concerns consume mental resources, leaving less for other tasks.”

    Experiment, 2013. Mani et al., Science (New York, N.Y.).

  3. Farmers show diminished cognitive performance before harvest, when poor, as compared with after harvest, when rich.

    “We found that the same farmer shows diminished cognitive performance before harvest, when poor, as compared with after harvest, when rich.”

    Experiment, 2013. Mani et al., Science (New York, N.Y.).

  4. The current literature predominantly confirms that poverty induces a scarcity mindset, which subsequently forces the poor into suboptimal decisions and behaviors.

    “Our findings indicate that the current literature predominantly confirms the first and second proposition”

    Review, 2021. Bruijn & Antonides, Theory and Decision.

  5. Cognitive load could decrease self-control capacity needed for healthy dietary behavior because it consumes mental bandwidth.

    “Cognitive load could decrease self-control capacity needed for healthy dietary behavior because it consumes mental bandwidth.”

    Review, 2024. Veer et al., International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity.

  6. The theory does not fully accord with the data and lacks some precision.

    “the theory does not fully accord with the data and lacks some precision.”

    Review, 2021. Bruijn & Antonides, Theory and Decision.

The data is mixed and contested.

While some studies show poverty reduces cognitive capacity, other experiments find that financial scarcity does not significantly impact test performance. Current literature lacks precision.

The mental weight of scarcity.

That may be why the stress of missing funds feels like a physical drain on your ability to plan or maintain self-control for healthy choices.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The grocery store aisle.

You stand before a shelf of food, trying to calculate the cost of every item while your mind weighs the bills you still have to pay by evening.

The honest summary

The evidence suggests a link between poverty and reduced cognitive capacity, but results are inconsistent across different experimental settings.

Methodological issues and a lack of precision in the current literature prevent a firm conclusion on the extent of scarcity theory.
