Algorithmic bias persists in neural networks.

Studies of convolutional neural networks for medical imaging show that bias is a recurring limitation. This occurs across multiple fields.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. There were concerns in all studies regarding the risk of bias in the index test and the reference standards.

    “There were concerns in all studies regarding the risk of bias in the index test and the reference standards.”

    Systematic review, 2022. Mathew et al., The Angle Orthodontist.

  2. The SL-LSTM method achieves a better long-term prediction accuracy and stability compared with the WNN.

    “Compared with the WNN, the SL-LSTM method has no obvious advantages in the prediction accuracy and stability in short-term prediction but achieves a better long-term prediction accuracy and stability.”

    Experiment, 2021. Huang et al., GPS Solutions.

  3. The accuracy of neural networks on test data for classifying cervical vertebrae maturation ranged from 50% to more than 90%.

    “The accuracy of the models on the test data ranged from 50% to more than 90%.”

    Systematic review, 2022. Mathew et al., The Angle Orthodontist.

  4. Algorithmic bias persisted across studies of convolutional neural networks for medical imaging.

    “However, implementation challenges including computational limitations, poor connectivity, dataset scarcity, and algorithmic bias persisted across studies.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Innocent et al., Frontiers in artificial intelligence.

  5. The review of neural networks for epilepsy detection includes a discussion on dataset bias.

    “The review concludes with a comprehensive discussion and summary of the technical characteristics, research directions, and limitations of current methods, including patient-to-patient identification, explainable AI, dataset bias, and zone location.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Wu et al., Artificial Intelligence Review.

The data is flawed.

Research on epilepsy detection includes discussions on dataset bias. Concerns also exist regarding bias in index tests and reference standards. The evidence is mixed on whether this mirrors human cognitive heuristics.

The system reflects its input.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The sources confirm that bias is a recurring limitation in neural network applications across various fields, though they do not explicitly detail the mechanism of mirroring human cognitive heuristics.

The sources identify the existence of bias but do not provide specific data on whether this bias originates from human cognitive heuristics versus other data flaws.
