Does shared trauma actually create a bond?

File no. 0110October 17, 2026Human relationships

Depression, not trauma, is the primary driver of bonding failure.

Postpartum depression at 2 months after delivery is a more important factor affecting bonding failure than posttraumatic stress symptoms after childbirth. The evidence is mixed and shows associations rather than direct causation.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. Higher severity of maternal physical neglect in childhood was significantly associated with less postpartum mother-infant bonding impairment.

    “In contrast, higher severity of maternal physical neglect was significantly associated with less postpartum MIB impairment.”

    Observational study, 2019. Lehnig et al., BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth.

  2. Higher severity of maternal emotional neglect in childhood is significantly related to more postpartum mother-infant bonding impairment.

    “higher severity of maternal emotional neglect in childhood, higher levels of postpartum depression and higher education were significantly related to more postpartum MIB impairment.”

    Observational study, 2019. Lehnig et al., BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth.

  3. Both mother-infant bonding and trauma symptoms improved over time across the intervention and control groups in studies of birth trauma.

    “Both mother-infant bonding and trauma symptoms improved over time across the intervention and control groups.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Odgers et al., Australian occupational therapy journal.

  4. Postpartum depression at 2 months after delivery is a more important factor affecting bonding failure than posttraumatic stress symptoms after childbirth.

    “However, the important factor affecting bonding failure was not PTSS after childbirth, but depression at T2.”

    Observational study, 2020. Suetsugu et al., BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth.

  5. Military sexual trauma is linked to poorer mother-infant bonding indirectly through maternal depression.

    “MST was associated with higher maternal depression, and higher maternal depression was associated with poorer mother-infant bonding. The effect of MST on bonding was indirect through depression.”

    Observational study, 2021. Creech et al., Depression and Anxiety.

The mechanism is indirect.

Military sexual trauma is linked to poorer mother-infant bonding indirectly through maternal depression. Bonding impairment is more consistently associated with depression and specific childhood neglect than with the direct experience of trauma.

The source of the bond is obscured.

That may be why shared trauma is often used to explain distance. The actual barrier might be a clinical condition like depression, rather than the memory of the event itself.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The quiet room.

You sit in a nursery at 3 am. You look at the sleeping infant. You feel a heavy distance between your body and theirs, even though you both survived the same event.

The honest summary

The sources indicate that bonding impairment is more consistently associated with maternal depression and specific types of childhood neglect than with the direct experience of trauma.

All studies provided are observational and show associations rather than direct causation.

Is it the memory that separates you, or something else?
