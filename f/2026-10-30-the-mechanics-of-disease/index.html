Was public health a tool for control?

File no. 0204October 30, 2026History

The evidence does not support a history of social control.

The provided data focuses on technical and socioeconomic factors of disease transmission. There is no evidence here regarding laws used for racial or class-based segregation.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. Shared sanitation facilities have historically been excluded from international sanitation targets because of concerns about acceptability, hygiene and access.

    “More than 761 million people rely on shared sanitation facilities. These have historically been excluded from international sanitation targets, regardless of the service level, due to concerns about acceptability, hygiene and access.”

    Systematic review, 2014. Heijnen et al., PLoS ONE. This shows a link, not a cause.

  2. Long-range human movement is a fundamental factor in the transport of V. cholerae and the formation of regional patterns of cholera epidemics.

    “We show that long-range human movement is fundamental in quantifying otherwise unexplained inter-catchment transport of V. cholerae, thus playing a key role in the formation of regional patterns of cholera epidemics.”

    Study, 2011. Mari et al., Journal of The Royal Society Interface.

  3. A meta-analysis of 12 studies found that relying on shared sanitation is linked to increased odds of disease with an odds ratio (OR) 1.44, 95% CI: 1.18-1.76.

    “A meta-analysis of 12 studies reporting on diarrhoea found increased odds of disease associated with reliance on shared sanitation (odds ratio (OR) 1. 44, 95% CI: 1. 18-1. 76).”

    Systematic review, 2014. Heijnen et al., PLoS ONE.

  4. Poor water, sanitation and hygiene conditions are linked to child stunting through sustained exposure to enteric pathogens and wider social and economic mechanisms.

    “The evidence reviewed suggests that poor WASH conditions have a significant detrimental effect on child growth and development resulting from sustained exposure to enteric pathogens but also due to wider social and economic mechanisms.”

    Review, 2016. Cumming & Cairncross, Maternal and Child Nutrition.

  5. Heterogeneously distributed drinking water supplies and sanitation conditions may affect large-scale cholera transmission.

    “We also show quantitatively how heterogeneously distributed drinking water supplies and sanitation conditions may affect large-scale cholera transmission, and analyse the effects of different sanitation policies.”

    Study, 2011. Mari et al., Journal of The Royal Society Interface.

Disease follows movement and infrastructure.

Long-range human movement transports bacteria across regions. Shared sanitation is linked to increased disease odds. Water and hygiene conditions influence child stunting through sustained pathogen exposure.

Infrastructure determines your health.

That may be why your physical environment dictates your physical well-being. Your health is a byproduct of how water and waste systems are distributed around you.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A shared bathroom in a crowded building.

The honest summary

The provided sources focus on the technical, epidemiological, and socioeconomic factors of disease transmission and sanitation rather than the history of social control.

The sources do not contain any historical data or specific mentions of racial or class-based segregation laws.
