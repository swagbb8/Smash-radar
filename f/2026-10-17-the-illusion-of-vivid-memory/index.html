Is your memory lying to you?

File no. 0114October 17, 2026Psychology

Vivid memories are not more accurate.

The clarity of a memory does not correlate with its factual consistency. Your most intense recollections are just as prone to decay as your mundane ones.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Flashbulb memories are not special in their accuracy, as previously claimed, but only in their perceived accuracy.

    “Flashbulb memories are not special in their accuracy, as previously claimed, but only in their perceived accuracy.”

    Randomized trial, 2003. Talarico & Rubin, Psychological science.

  2. Consistency for flashbulb and everyday memories did not differ, in both cases declining over time.

    “Consistency for the flashbulb and everyday memories did not differ, in both cases declining over time.”

    Randomized trial, 2003. Talarico & Rubin, Psychological science.

  3. Participants' bond with Michael Jackson predicted their confidence but not the consistency of their flashbulb memories 18 months later.

    “Participants' bond with Michael Jackson predicted their confidence but not the consistency of their flashbulb memories 18 months later.”

    Study, 2013. Day & Ross, Memory.

  4. No effects of retention interval length or rehearsal were found for flashbulb memory, which contained details at each assessment.

    “No effects of retention interval length or rehearsal were found for flashbulb memory, which contained details at each assessment.”

    Experiment, 2005. Shapiro, Memory.

  5. Ratings of surprise and consequentiality were unrelated to the accuracy and clarity of the memories.

    “Ratings of surprise and consequentiality were unrelated to the accuracy and clarity of the memories.”

    Study, 2005. Berntsen & Thomsen, Journal of Experimental Psychology General.

  6. Ratings of recollection, vividness and other phenomenological properties were consistently higher for FBMs than for EDMs across time.

    “However, ratings of recollection, vividness and other phenomenological properties were consistently higher for FBMs than for EDMs across time.”

    Study, 2006. Talarico & Rubin, Applied Cognitive Psychology.

Confidence is a feeling, not a fact.

Flashbulb memories feel more vivid and clear than everyday memories. However, their consistency declines over time at the same rate as ordinary memories.

Your feelings are not a record.

That may be why you feel certain about your past even when you are wrong. The intensity of a moment can mask the erosion of the details.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A kitchen at midnight.

You stand in the dark kitchen. You remember the exact light on the counter. You are certain of every detail. Time has likely altered most of them.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while flashbulb memories feel more accurate and vivid than everyday memories, they do not actually maintain higher levels of consistency over time.

The studies often rely on self-reported ratings of confidence and vividness rather than objective measures of factual accuracy.
