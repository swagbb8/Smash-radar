The uncomfortable truth

Is your confidence a result of your environment?

File no. 0094October 15, 2026Psychology

East Asians do not self-enhance.

While Westerners show a clear self-serving bias, this behavior is notably absent in East Asian cultures. The evidence on this is mixed due to measurement limitations.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. East Asians do not self-enhance.

    “Overall, the evidence converges to show that East Asians do not self-enhance.”

    Meta-analysis, 2007. Heine & Hamamura, Personality and Social Psychology Review.

  2. Westerners show a clear self-serving bias with an effect size of d = .87, while East Asians do not show this bias with a d = -.01.

    “Within cultures, Westerners showed a clear self-serving bias (d = .87), whereas East Asians did not (d = -.01), with Asian Americans falling in between (d = .52).”

    Meta-analysis, 2007. Heine & Hamamura, Personality and Social Psychology Review.

  3. Manipulations of subjective status, power, and dominance did not have strong effects on the magnitude of the better-than-average effect.

    “However, manipulations of SES in terms of subjective status (Study 2), power (Study 3), and dominance (Study 4) did not have strong effects on BTAE magnitude (d's ranging from -0.04 to -0.14).”

    Meta-analysis, 2015. Varnum, Frontiers in Psychology.

  4. Religiosity is partly in the service of self-enhancement.

    “The evidence suggests that religiosity is partly in the service of self-enhancement.”

    Meta-analysis, 2009. Sedikides & Gebauer, Personality and Social Psychology Review.

  5. Subjective measures of socioeconomic status are positively correlated with the magnitude of the better-than-average effect.

    “Across the studies subjective measures of SES were positively correlated with magnitude of BTAE.”

    Meta-analysis, 2015. Varnum, Frontiers in Psychology.

  6. Chronic socioeconomic status, except for education, is significantly correlated with the better-than-average effect.

    “Meta-analysis revealed all measures of chronic SES (with the exception of education) were significantly correlated with BTAE.”

    Meta-analysis, 2015. Varnum, Frontiers in Psychology.

Status and status levels matter.

Manipulations of power or dominance do not significantly change the better-than-average effect. However, chronic socioeconomic status is significantly correlated with it.

Your self-image is tied to your environment.

That may be why your sense of superiority is linked to your long-term socioeconomic status rather than your current power or temporary position.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The way you judge your neighbor.

You sit in your living room and feel a quiet sense of pride in your life compared to the people on your street.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that self-enhancement is a common human phenomenon but is not universal, as it is notably absent in East Asian cultures.

The research on East Asian self-enhancement suggests that certain methods of measurement may be compromised by the 'everyone is better than their group's average' effect.
