The world you don’t see

Who knows your daily routine better than you?

File no. 0313November 21, 2026The future

Your home is no longer a private space.

Smart home technology can effortlessly collect data on your presence and the time you spend on specific activities. The evidence on this is mixed regarding long-term clinical effects.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Smart meters can predict residents' daily behavioral patterns by analyzing past trends of electrical home appliances.

    “The presented neurocomputing-based time-series load modeling and forecasting mechanism facilitated by energy decomposition is capable of predicting residents’ daily behavioral patterns by nonintrusively analyzing and modeling relevant electrical home appliances based on their past trends”

    Study, 2022. Lin et al., IEEE Access.

  2. Users' knowledge of their smart home does not strongly influence their threat models and protection behaviors.

    “Our results reveal that while people are uncertain about manufacturers' data practices, users' knowledge of their smart home does not strongly influence their threat models and protection behaviors.”

    Study, 2019. Tabassum et al., Chalmers Research (Chalmers University of Technology).

  3. Behavioral data such as presence (n=38) and time spent on activities (n=18) can be acquired effortlessly in private spaces.

    “While behavioral data (e.g. , presence (n=38), time spent on activities (n=18)) can be acquired effortlessly, physiological parameters (e.g. , heart rate, respiratory rate) are measurable on a limited scale (n=5).”

    Review, 2021. Wang et al., Sensors.

  4. There is no convincing proof of a clear effect of monitoring functions on clinical outcome with a large sample size and long-term monitoring.

    “In summary, convincing proof of a clear effect of these monitoring functions on clinical outcome with a large sample size and long-term monitoring is still lacking.”

    Review, 2021. Wang et al., Sensors.

  5. The home was never an inviolable site of total privacy.

    “The home was never an inviolable site of total privacy.”

    Study, 2017. Silverman, Social research.

Predictable patterns emerge from your appliances.

Smart meters analyze past trends of your electrical home appliances to predict your daily behavioral patterns. This data collection happens in your private spaces.

Knowledge does not equal protection.

Knowing how your smart home works does not strongly influence how you protect your data. This may be why surveillance remains a constant in modern living.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A quiet evening in your living room.

You sit on the couch as the lights dim. The smart meter records the shift in power from your television and your presence in the room.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that smart home technology enables granular behavioral tracking and prediction, though its direct impact on clinical outcomes remains unproven and user awareness is not a strong driver of security behavior.

The sources do not provide a large sample size or long-term monitoring to prove the clinical effects of these monitoring functions.
