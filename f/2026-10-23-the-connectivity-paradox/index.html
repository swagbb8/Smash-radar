Is your social media making you feel alone?

File no. 0158October 23, 2026Social media

Active social media use is linked to greater online support.

While problematic use correlates with loneliness, active engagement is linked to better wellbeing and support.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Active social media use is linked to greater online support (r = .34), wellbeing (r = .15), positive affect (r = .11), and symptoms of anxiety (r = .12).

    “between-person associations for active SMU and greater online support (r = .34), wellbeing (r = .15), positive affect (r = .11), and symptoms of anxiety (r = .12)”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Godard & Holtzman, Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication.

  2. Passive social media use is linked to greater online support (r = .15).

    “passive SMU and greater online support (r = .15)”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Godard & Holtzman, Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication.

  3. Limiting social media use to approximately 30 minutes per day may lead to significant improvement in well-being.

    “Our findings strongly suggest that limiting social media use to approximately 30 minutes per day may lead to significant improvement in well-being.”

    Experiment, 2018. Hunt et al., Journal of Social and Clinical Psychology.

  4. Problematic social media use is linked to moderate correlations with depression and loneliness.

    “The mean correlations of problematic SM use with life satisfaction and self-esteem are small, whereas those of problematic SM use with depression and loneliness are moderate.”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Huang, International Journal of Social Psychiatry.

The evidence on social media is mixed.

Problematic use correlates with depression and loneliness. However, active use is linked to positive affect and support. Most findings are correlational.

The way you use the screen matters.

That may be why some people find community while others feel isolated. It is not just the time spent, but the nature of the interaction.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while problematic and addictive social media use correlates with loneliness and distress, active engagement can be linked to positive outcomes like online support.

Most findings are correlational, with the exception of one experiment suggesting that limiting use may reduce loneliness.
