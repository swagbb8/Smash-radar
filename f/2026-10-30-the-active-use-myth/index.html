Does active posting make you more depressed?

File no. 0203October 30, 2026Social media

Active use is linked to positive affect and wellbeing.

Research shows that active social media use correlates with higher wellbeing and online support. The evidence does not support the idea that active posting correlates more strongly with negative affect than passive scrolling.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. In an experiment, the type of social media use (active or passive) did not explain the effects on depressive symptoms.

    “Structural equation modeling analysis showed that while the type of use didn't explain the effects, depressive symptoms, and self-assessment motivation did.”

    Experiment, 2023. Aubry et al., Personality and Individual Differences.

  2. Active social media use is linked to greater online support (r = .34), wellbeing (r = .15), positive affect (r = .11), and symptoms of anxiety (r = .12).

    “between-person associations for active SMU and greater online support (r = .34), wellbeing (r = .15), positive affect (r = .11), and symptoms of anxiety (r = .12)”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Godard & Holtzman, Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication.

  3. Passive use was linked to worse emotional outcomes in general social media contexts, but not in the context of social media groups.

    “Moderator analyses revealed that passive use was associated with worse emotional outcomes in general SM contexts, but not in the context of SM groups.”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Godard & Holtzman, Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication.

  4. Passive social media use is linked to greater online support (r = .15).

    “passive SMU and greater online support (r = .15)”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Godard & Holtzman, Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication.

  5. Social comparisons on social media use lead to more depressive symptoms.

    “On the one hand, social comparisons on social media use lead to more depressive symptoms.”

    Experiment, 2023. Aubry et al., Personality and Individual Differences.

The type of use does not explain depressive symptoms.

Experiments show that whether you use social media actively or passively does not explain the effects on depressive symptoms. The evidence on the link between social media and mental health is mixed or contested.

The way you interact matters more than the time spent.

That may be why your online interactions feel different depending on your goals. Social comparisons on these platforms lead to more depressive symptoms, regardless of how active you are.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

You scroll through a group chat at night.

You see friends talking and feel a sense of connection. You move from reading to typing your own thoughts into the thread.

The honest summary

The evidence does not support the idea that active posting correlates more strongly with negative affect than passive scrolling; in fact, active use is linked to positive outcomes like wellbeing and online support.

Many of the findings regarding the relationship between social media and mental health are based on studies of poor quality with small effect sizes.
