Are you really only using half of your brain?

File no. 0125October 19, 2026The human brain

There is no global left-brained or right-brained phenotype.

Data do not support a whole-brain phenotype of greater strength in one hemisphere over another across individuals.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Data are not consistent with a whole-brain phenotype of greater "left-brained" or greater "right-brained" network strength across individuals.

    “our data are not consistent with a whole-brain phenotype of greater "left-brained" or greater "right-brained" network strength across individuals.”

    Study, 2013. Nielsen et al., PLoS ONE.

  2. Left-handers tended to have more aroused right than left hemispheres, whereas the distribution of right-handers was centered around 0 arousal bias.

    “Left-handers tended to have more aroused right than left hemispheres, whereas the distribution of right-handers was centered around 0 arousal bias.”

    Experiment, 1997. Eviatar et al., Neuropsychology.

  3. Left-handers demonstrated a more variable distribution across both hemispheres, supporting a less focal profile of functional brain organization.

    “Furthermore, left-handers demonstrated a more variable distribution across both hemispheres, supporting a less focal profile of functional brain organization.”

    Study, 2018. O’Regan & Serrien, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience.

  4. The meta-analysis revealed a significant reduction in hemispheric lateralization in individuals with ASD compared to NT controls.

    “The meta-analysis revealed a significant reduction in hemispheric lateralization in individuals with ASD compared to NT controls.”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Céspedes, Publication Server of Kaiserslautern University of Technology (Kaiserslautern University of Technology).

  5. The results, although not significant, suggested a slight tendency toward left lateralization in the depression group.

    “The results, although not significant, suggested a slight tendency toward left lateralization in the depression group.”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Horato et al., Translational Psychiatry.

  6. Right-handers benefitted more from left-hemispheric lateralization for language comprehension and right-hemispheric lateralization for spatial attention than left-handers.

    “The data showed that right-handers benefitted more from left-hemispheric lateralization for language comprehension and right-hemispheric lateralization for spatial attention than left-handers.”

    Study, 2018. O’Regan & Serrien, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience.

Lateralization is a local property.

Specific brain regions are lateralized for tasks like language or spatial attention. However, these are local features of brain networks, not a global characteristic of your brain.

Your brain is not a binary.

That may be why you do not fit into a single category of personality. Your brain functions through localized networks rather than a total hemispheric split.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A split screen on a monitor.

You watch a video while typing a message. Your brain manages these tasks through specific networks, not by switching between two different sides of your head.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while specific tasks and conditions (like handedness or neurodevelopmental disorders) influence hemispheric asymmetry, there is no evidence for a whole-brain phenotype of greater left or right-brain strength.

The studies show that lateralization is often a local property of brain networks rather than a global characteristic.
