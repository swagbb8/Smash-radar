What is currently inside your brain cells?

File no. 0277November 13, 2026Science and reality

Nanoplastics are found in human brains with dementia.

Higher concentrations are observed in cerebrovascular walls and immune cells. Evidence regarding the pathways of exposure and clearance is still limited.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Microplastics were not biomagnified in the edible parts of the marine food web of the Persian Gulf; instead, trophic dilution occurs.

    “the results of the trophic magnification factor (TMF) and biomagnification factor (BMF) calculation indicated that MPs were not biomagnified in edible parts of the marine food web of the Persian Gulf.”

    Study, 2019. Akhbarizadeh et al., Food Additives & Contaminants Part A.

  2. Microplastic concentrations in human liver and brain samples increased over time between 2016 and 2024.

    “the time of death (2016 versus 2024) was a significant factor, with increasing MNP concentrations over time in both liver and brain samples (P = 0. 01).”

    Observational study, 2025. Nihart et al., Nature medicine.

  3. Greater accumulation of nanoplastics was observed in human brains with a documented dementia diagnosis, specifically in cerebrovascular walls and immune cells.

    “even greater accumulation of MNPs was observed in a cohort of decedent brains with documented dementia diagnosis, with notable deposition in cerebrovascular walls and immune cells.”

    Observational study, 2025. Nihart et al., Nature medicine.

  4. No clear sign of microplastic biomagnification in situ was observed at the higher trophic levels.

    “Combined, these findings indicate that, although bioaccumulation of MPs occurs within trophic levels, no clear sign of MP biomagnification in situ was observed at the higher trophic levels.”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Miller et al., PLoS ONE.

  5. Microplastics were found in human brain tissues, with higher proportions of polyethylene than in the liver or kidney.

    “Brain tissues harbor higher proportions of polyethylene compared to the composition of the plastics in liver or kidney”

    Observational study, 2025. Nihart et al., Nature medicine.

  6. Studies have confirmed that nano- and microplastics can induce apoptosis in cells and have genotoxic and cytotoxic effects.

    “Most studies to date have confirmed that nano- and microplastics can induce apoptosis in cells and have genotoxic and cytotoxic effects.”

    Review, 2024. Winiarska et al., Environmental Research.

Microplastics are moving through your body.

Concentrations in the human liver and brain have increased between 2016 and 2024. These particles can induce apoptosis in cells and have genotoxic effects.

The food chain may not be the only source.

While microplastics do not consistently biomagnify in marine food webs, they are still accumulating in human tissues. This may be why internal exposure is a primary concern.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

You drink from a plastic bottle.

You notice the faint texture of the container against your lips. You do not see the particles entering your system.

The honest summary

The sources confirm that microplastics and nanoplastics are present in human tissues and can cause cellular damage, but they do not consistently show biomagnification in marine food webs.

Many studies lack sufficient data to evaluate specific human health effects or the pathways of exposure and clearance.
