Why do you choose pain over silence?

File no. 0154October 23, 2026Psychology

You may choose pain over silence.

Some people choose to administer strong electric shocks to themselves even when a mild or no-shock option is available.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Participants chose to administer strong electric shocks to themselves even when an innocuous response option generating no or a mild shock was available.

    “However, they administered strong electric shocks to themselves even when an innocuous response option generating no or a mild shock was available.”

    Experiment, 2022. Eder et al., Scientific reports.

  2. The self-infliction of pain was attractive for many participants because they were curious about the shocks, their intensities, and the effects they would have on them.

    “Instead, it seems that the self-infliction of pain was attractive for many participants, because they were curious about the shocks, their intensities, and the effects they would have on them.”

    Experiment, 2022. Eder et al., Scientific reports.

  3. Many people, especially men, chose to give themselves a mild electric shock rather than be deprived of external sensory stimuli.

    “In fact, many of the people studied, particularly the men, chose to give themselves a mild electric shock rather than be deprived of external sensory stimuli.”

    Study, 2014. Wilson et al., Science.

  4. Wilson et al.’s results provide little support for the conclusion that people find being alone with their own thoughts unpleasant or aversive.

    “Wilson et al.’s results provide little support for the conclusion that people find being alone with their own thoughts unpleasant or aversive.”

    Preprint, not peer reviewed, 2017. Fox et al..

  5. The results from many prior studies of the affective qualities of self-generated thought show “just thinking” to be on average somewhat enjoyable.

    “Instead, their results—like the results from many prior studies of the affective qualities of self-generated thought—show “just thinking” to be on average some what enjoyable.”

    Preprint, not peer reviewed, 2017. Fox et al..

The evidence is mixed.

While some avoid solitude through self-inflicted pain, other research shows that thinking is often enjoyable. Pain-seeking behavior may be driven by curiosity about intensity and effects rather than a desire to avoid thought.

The source of your distraction.

That may be why you reach for constant stimulation. It might not be an inability to think, but a curiosity about external sensations or a preference for active engagement over internal reflection.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The scroll before sleep.

You lie in bed in the dark. You feel the weight of the silence. You pick up your phone to scroll through a feed of strangers until your eyes ache. You choose the glow over the quiet.

The honest summary

The evidence is mixed, showing that while some individuals avoid solitude through self-inflicted pain, other studies suggest thinking is generally pleasant and that pain-seeking behavior may be driven by curiosity.

The research shows significant individual and situational variability in how people experience the affective qualities of thinking.
