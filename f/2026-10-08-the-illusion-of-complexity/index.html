Is your behavior a choice or a reflex?

File no. 0029October 8, 2026Science and reality

Complex behavior may not require complex cognition.

While cephalopods possess large, complex brains, their behavioral flexibility could be driven by simpler mechanisms.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Behavioral flexibility in cephalopods could be explained by putatively simpler mechanisms.

    “We then discuss different types of behavioural flexibility exhibited by cephalopods and, using examples from other taxa, highlight that behavioural flexibility could be explained by putatively simpler mechanisms.”

    Review, 2020. Schnell et al., Biological reviews/Biological reviews of the Cambridge Philosophical Society.

  2. The octopus brain contains lobes with higher order functions like storage of learned information and centers compared to the vertebrate cerebellum and frontal lobe.

    “The brain does not just have centralization of the molluscan ganglia but also contains lobes with “higher order” functions such as storage of learned information, and centres have been compared with the vertebrate cerebellum and frontal lobe.”

    Study, 2013. Mather & Kuba, Canadian Journal of Zoology.

  3. Substantial expansion of a handful of gene families and extensive remodelling of genome linkage and repetitive content played a critical role in the evolution of cephalopod morphological innovations, including their large and complex nervous systems.

    “Our analysis suggests that substantial expansion of a handful of gene families, along with extensive remodelling of genome linkage and repetitive content, played a critical role in the evolution of cephalopod morphological innovations, including their large and complex nervous systems.”

    Study, 2015. Albertin et al., Nature.

  4. The increased complexity of the cephalopod brain is likely due to other unknown factors.

    “Their expression patterns are broadly similar, indicating that the increased complexity of the cephalopod brain is likely due to other unknown factors.”

    Study, 2014. Wollesen et al., EvoDevo. This shows a link, not a cause.

  5. Cephalopods are considered to be the most cognitively advanced group of invertebrates.

    “The soft-bodied cephalopods including octopus, cuttlefish, and squid are broadly considered to be the most cognitively advanced group of invertebrates.”

    Review, 2020. Schnell et al., Biological reviews/Biological reviews of the Cambridge Philosophical Society.

The architecture of the octopus brain.

Cephalopods are the most cognitively advanced invertebrates. Their brains contain lobes for storage and centers comparable to vertebrate frontal lobes.

The mystery of neurological evolution.

The factors driving this brain complexity remain unknown. That may be why we struggle to define what truly creates intelligence versus simple reaction.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A door handle in a hallway.

You reach for the handle and turn it. You do not think about the mechanics of your hand or the neurons firing to move your fingers.

The honest summary

The sources confirm that cephalopods have complex nervous systems and cognitive abilities, but they suggest that complex behavior might not always require complex cognition.

The specific mechanisms driving the complexity of the cephalopod brain remain largely unknown.
