Who actually decides how your city grows?

File no. 0315November 22, 2026Money and power

Elite capture undermines policy in several sectors.

The evidence shows that elite capture influences urban planning, welfare distribution, and oral health policy, but it does not specifically address pharmaceutical profits.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. A household connected to a local political executive is linked to a significantly increased probability of obtaining a below-poverty-line ration card in national, rural, and urban contexts in India.

    “a household connected to a local political executive (somebody close or as a family member) vis-à-vis not connected significantly increases the probability of its obtaining an important poverty-alleviating entitlement; that is, a below-poverty-line ration card in all three contexts: national, rural, and urban.”

    Observational study, 2014. Panda, The Journal of Development Studies.

  2. The sugar industry is currently influencing dental research, oral health policy, and professional organisations through its well developed corporate strategies.

    “At present, the sugar industry is influencing dental research, oral health policy, and professional organisations through its well developed corporate strategies.”

    Review, 2019. Watt et al., Lancet (London, England).

  3. Governance fragmentation, elite capture, and weak enforcement of the 2020–2040 Master Plan in Islamabad have produced enduring contradictions between policy intent and urban reality.

    “The findings reveal that governance fragmentation between the Capital Development Authority (CDA) and Metropolitan Corporation Islamabad (MCI), combined with elite capture and weak enforcement of the 2020–2040 Master Plan, has produced enduring contradictions between policy intent and urban reality.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Ahmad et al., Land.

  4. In Nigeria, the dominance of the executive, lack of checks among institutions, capture by the elite, and informal governance practices continue to mediate and limit the transformations brought about by reforms.

    “The dominance of the executive, lack of checks among institutions, capture by the elite, and informal governance practices continue to mediate and limit the transformations brought about by reforms.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Oyeyemi et al., Advances in Applied Sociology.

  5. Elite capture and multi-dimensional exclusions perpetuate current housing and service failure in African cities.

    “Further, it highlights elite capture and multi-dimensional exclusions that perpetuate current housing and service failure in African cities.”

    Study, 2024. Boateng & Klopp, Urban Forum.

Policy intent meets reality.

Governance fragmentation and capture by the elite create contradictions between planned reforms and actual results. This mechanism limits transformations in public services and housing in various countries.

The systems you rely on are mediated.

That may be why public services and health policies often fail to reach those they were designed to help. The interests of the few may shape the reality of the many.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A walk through a neglected neighborhood.

You walk past a housing complex where the services promised by the city never arrived. The infrastructure is broken, and the gap between the plan and the street is visible.

The honest summary

The sources confirm that elite capture is a recurring mechanism that undermines policy intent in various sectors like urban planning, welfare, and oral health, but they do not specifically address the pharmaceutical profit motive.

The sources provide evidence of elite capture in diverse contexts but lack specific data on the pharmaceutical industry's role in public health policy.
