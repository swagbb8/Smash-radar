What is your body paying for your professionalism?

File no. 0200October 30, 2026Human relationships

Emotional labor is linked to higher salivary cortisol levels.

When you manage your emotions for others at work, your body produces more cortisol. This is a physiological stress response.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Emotional labor results in more harm than benefits for the employee.

    “For the employee, emotional labor results in more harm than benefits.”

    Review, 2024. Pinkawa & Dörfel, BMC Psychology. This shows a link, not a cause.

  2. Collaborative learning involves concomitant costs which are inherent to collaboration.

    “One reason for this omission is that cognitive load theory has only sporadically concerned itself with certain particulars of collaborative learning such as the concept of a collective working memory when collaborating along with issues associated with transactive activities and their concomitant costs which are inherent to collaboration.”

    Study, 2018. Kirschner et al., International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning.

  3. Emotional labor is linked to increased employee withdrawal behavior.

    “Emotional labor benefits the organization, e. g. , by improving customer conflict handling, but also causes harm by increasing employee withdrawal behavior.”

    Review, 2024. Pinkawa & Dörfel, BMC Psychology.

  4. Emotional labor is linked to higher salivary cortisol levels in a customer service group.

    “The results demonstrated that the degree of emotional labor reported by the customer service group was higher than that of the administrative work group.”

    Observational study, 2018. Lim et al., International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

  5. Emotional labor is linked to physiological responses to acutely stressful events in the workplace.

    “Findings indicate chronic forms of gendered workplace stress such as emotional labor, gender role stress and, posttraumatic stress each have differential associations with, and predict physiological responses to, acutely stressful events in the workplace.”

    Study, 2020. Birze et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

Collaborative work carries inherent costs.

Working with others involves costs that are built into the process. These tasks are linked to physiological responses to stressful events and increased employee withdrawal.

The cost of being civil.

That may be why you feel exhausted after a day of polite interactions. The evidence shows emotional labor results in more harm than benefits for the employee.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The morning meeting.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that emotional labor and collaborative tasks are associated with physiological stress, cognitive costs, and negative outcomes for employees.

The research on emotional labor is in its early stages with a small number of studies that need to be replicated to increase reliability.
