Does your neighbor's wealth dictate your own morals?

File no. 0169October 25, 2026Money and power

Social capital can buffer the effects of relative deprivation.

Your connections to others can lessen the impact of your neighbors' wealth on your own sense of well-being.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Social relative deprivation did not significantly reduce satisfaction or trust in isolated Tibetan communities.

    “Notably, social relative deprivation (SRD) did not significantly reduce satisfaction or trust.”

    Study, 2025. Gong et al., Tourism Recreation Research.

  2. Cultural insulation allows residents to prioritize cultural integrity and ecological harmony over material gain.

    “Cultural insulation allows residents to prioritize cultural integrity and ecological harmony over material gain.”

    Study, 2025. Gong et al., Tourism Recreation Research.

  3. Childhood perceived relative deprivation is linked to beliefs about justice, trust and social rank.

    “The purpose of this paper is to hypothesize that childhood perceived relative deprivation (PRD) would be linked to sub-syndromal psychotic symptoms and poor wellbeing via beliefs about justice, trust and social rank.”

    Observational study, 2014. Wickham et al., Journal of Public Mental Health.

  4. Social capital significantly reduced relative deprivation.

    “Moreover, it could be found that social capital significantly reduced relative deprivation.”

    Study, 2014. Cho, International journal of communication.

Your environment shapes your beliefs.

Childhood experiences with relative deprivation are linked to your beliefs about justice and trust. However, cultural insulation and social capital can change how these forces affect you.

The system is not absolute.

That may be why some people in high-wealth areas remain satisfied. If you prioritize cultural integrity or deep social ties, the status of your neighbor matters less.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The quiet of your street.

You walk past a house with a larger driveway than yours. You feel a slight weight in your chest, then you see a neighbor wave and the feeling fades.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that relative deprivation is associated with psychological wellbeing and trust, but its impact can be mitigated by social capital and cultural values.

Several studies are cross-sectional or observational, meaning they show associations rather than direct causation.
