The dark side of modern life

Why does bad news feel like a necessity?

File no. 0260November 10, 2026Social media

Doomscrolling is linked to sleep difficulties and anxiety.

Research shows a consistent association between habitual media use and poorer mental health outcomes. However, these studies are observational and do not prove that doomscrolling causes these issues.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Even the most connected citizens deliberately and intermittingly avoided news to cope with feelings of being scared or overwhelmed.

    “To cope with feelings of being scared or overwhelmed, even the most connected citizens deliberately and intermittingly avoided news.”

    Study, 2021. Ytre-Arne & Moe, Journalism Studies.

  2. The desire to cope with uncertainty and the quest for positive information are underlying reasons for the increase in individuals' consumption of negative news.

    “Among the underlying reasons for this behavior, the desire to cope with uncertainty and the quest for positive information stand out.”

    Review, 2024. Güme, Psikiyatride Guncel Yaklasimlar - Current Approaches in Psychiatry.

  3. Social media exposure was linked to increased depression and PTSD, especially for those with more severe maltreatment histories.

    “Using multilevel modeling, social media exposure was associated with increased depression and PTSD. This association was stronger for those with more severe maltreatment histories.”

    Study, 2022. Price et al., Psychological trauma : theory, research, practice and policy.

  4. Doomscrolling is linked to poorer mental-health and well-being outcomes.

    “Doomscrolling is associated with poorer mental-health and well-being outcomes, but the evidence does not establish causality.”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Li & Yin, Frontiers in Psychology.

  5. Doomscrolling is linked to anxiety, depressive symptoms, stress, and sleep difficulties.

    “Doomscrolling is associated with poorer mental health indicators, particularly anxiety, depressive symptoms, stress, and sleep difficulties.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Syafitri et al., Journal of Psychiatry Psychology and Behavioral Research.

  6. Doomscrolling is linked to passive social media use, habitual media use, anxiety, poor self-control, and several personality traits.

    “Doomscrolling was also linked to passive social media use, habitual media use, anxiety, poor self-control, and several personality traits.”

    Observational study, 2022. Sharma et al., Technology Mind and Behavior.

The cycle of uncertainty.

You consume negative news to cope with uncertainty. This quest for information is linked to increased depression and stress. Some people deliberately avoid news to cope with feeling overwhelmed.

A survival strategy.

That may be why you feel a constant need to check your phone. Your brain is searching for a way to handle the weight of an unpredictable world.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The late night scroll.

You lie in bed with the lamp off. The blue light hits your face. You keep moving your thumb up, looking for something that makes sense of the day.

The honest summary

The evidence shows a consistent association between doomscrolling and negative mental health outcomes, but it does not establish a causal link.

Most studies are observational or cross-sectional, meaning they cannot prove that doomscrolling causes the mental health issues.
