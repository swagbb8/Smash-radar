Is your intelligence actually decreasing?

File no. 0018October 7, 2026History

IQ scores may be falling because of cultural shifts.

Recent data suggests that national declines in test scores are linked to changes in the cultural content of the tests themselves.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. There is no evidence for a decrease of intelligence in France because the decline was actually due to a drift of item difficulty for older versions of the WAIS caused by cultural changes.

    “These results confirm that there is currently no evidence for a decrease of intelligence in France, with prior findings being attributable to a drift of item difficulty for older versions of the WAIS, due to cultural changes.”

    Study, 2020. Gonthier et al., Intelligence. This shows a link, not a cause.

  2. The observed Flynn effect, its turning point, and subsequent decline can all be fully recovered from within-family variation.

    “we show that the observed Flynn effect, its turning point, and subsequent decline can all be fully recovered from within-family variation.”

    Study, 2018. Bratsberg & Røgeberg, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  3. Research found evidence of a negative Flynn effect on the attention/working memory and learning trials (Trial 1, Trial 2, Trial 3, Trials 1–5 Total, List B; ps < . 007).

    “findings revealed evidence of a negative Flynn effect on the attention/working memory and learning trials (Trial 1, Trial 2, Trial 3, Trials 1–5 Total, List B; ps < . 007)”

    Observational study, 2019. Graves et al., The Clinical Neuropsychologist.

  4. Decline on a subtest correlated 0.95 with its cultural load.

    “Decline on a subtest correlated 0. 95 with its cultural load.”

    Study, 2020. Gonthier et al., Intelligence.

The evidence is mixed and contested.

Some specific trials for attention and working memory show a negative trend. However, other research suggests these declines are artifacts of changing test items.

How we measure your mind is changing.

That may be why your ability to solve a problem depends on the world you live in. The test might be moving, not your brain.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence is mixed, showing some specific cognitive declines while also providing evidence that reported national declines may be artifacts of cultural shifts in testing.

The study on France specifically notes that the past should be treated as a different country due to cultural changes in test items.

Are you getting less capable, or is the world just different?
