Does your diet change your internal biology?

File no. 0316November 22, 2026The future

Industrial diets change your microbes but not your diversity.

Western diets reshape the specific types of bacteria in your gut without necessarily reducing the total variety of species present.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The gut microbiome of people in industrialized populations differs from those living traditional lifestyles, but there were little differences in alpha diversity across lifestyles.

    “The composition of the gut microbiome in industrialized populations differs from those living traditional lifestyles. ... we found little differences in alpha diversity across lifestyles.”

    Study, 2018. Jha et al., PLoS Biology.

  2. Microbial targeting restored CD4(+) T cell balance and yielded significantly leaner animals regardless of their dietary 'fast food' indiscretions.

    “Our finding that microbial targeting restored CD4(+) T cell balance and yielded significantly leaner animals regardless of their dietary 'fast food' indiscretions”

    Study, 2013. Poutahidis et al., PLoS ONE.

  3. Ethnic/racial background proved to be one of the strongest associations of both pathways and microbes with clinical metadata.

    “Metagenomic carriage of metabolic pathways was stable among individuals despite variation in community structure, and ethnic/racial background proved to be one of the strongest associations of both pathways and microbes with clinical metadata.”

    Observational study, 2012. Consortium, Nature.

  4. Changes in agriculture and engineering have altered human eating and living behaviors, which are linked to changes in the bacterial communities within the human body.

    “Technological advances that have led to changes in agriculture and engineering have altered our eating and living behaviors in ways never before possible in human history. These changes also have altered the bacterial communities within the human body”

    Review, 2016. Broussard & Devkota, Molecular Metabolism.

  5. The abundance of the Western module correlated appropriately with dietary intake of saturated fatty acids, fat-soluble vitamins and fiber in 98 volunteers.

    “In 98 volunteers the abundance of the Western module correlated appropriately with dietary intake of saturated fatty acids, fat-soluble vitamins and fiber.”

    Study, 2013. Siddharth et al., PLoS ONE.

  6. The Western module was also coupled to dietary intake in children (and piglets) correlating with formula (vs breast) feeding.

    “The module was also coupled to dietary intake in children (and piglets) correlating with formula (vs breast) feeding”

    Study, 2013. Siddharth et al., PLoS ONE.

Agriculture has rewritten your internal biology.

Changes in agriculture and engineering have altered your eating behaviors. These behaviors are linked to specific changes in the bacterial communities within your body.

Your biology follows the supply chain.

That may be why your gut reflects the industrial food system. Your internal ecosystem is a mirror of the modern food supply.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A drive-thru window at midnight.

The fast food you eat is linked to a specific western module of microbes in your gut.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that industrialization and Western diets significantly reshape the specific types of microbes present in the human gut, but it does not confirm a systematic reduction in overall microbial diversity.

Several studies show that factors like genetics, geography, and ethnicity are also primary drivers of microbiome structure, making it difficult to isolate the impact of the food supply chain alone.
