Is your health a result of your own choices?

File no. 0067October 11, 2026History

Famine marks your biology before you are born.

Prenatal famine exposure is linked to increased coronary heart disease and obesity in offspring. However, the evidence on whether these effects reach the third generation is mixed.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The current estimates of a positive relationship between prenatal exposure to the Chinese famine and adult T2D are mainly driven by uncontrolled age differences between famine births and postfamine births.

    “The current estimates of a positive relationship between prenatal exposure to the Chinese famine and adult T2D are mainly driven by uncontrolled age differences between famine births and postfamine births.”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Li & Lumey, Nutrients.

  2. Offspring of prenatally undernourished fathers were linked to being heavier and more obese than offspring of fathers and mothers who had not been undernourished prenatally.

    “Offspring of prenatally undernourished fathers, but not mothers, were heavier and more obese than offspring of fathers and mothers who had not been undernourished prenatally.”

    Observational study, 2013. Veenendaal et al., BJOG An International Journal of Obstetrics & Gynaecology.

  3. F1 famine exposure in utero was linked to increased F2 neonatal adiposity and poor health in later life.

    “F1 famine exposure in utero was, however, associated with increased F2 neonatal adiposity and poor health in later life.”

    Observational study, 2008. Painter et al., BJOG An International Journal of Obstetrics & Gynaecology.

  4. People exposed to the Dutch famine in early gestation were linked to a higher prevalence of coronary heart disease (8.8% v 3.2%; odds ratio adjusted for sex 3.0, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.1 to 8.1).

    “The prevalence of coronary heart disease was higher in those exposed in early gestation than in non-exposed people (8.8% v 3.2%; odds ratio adjusted for sex 3.0, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.1 to 8.1).”

    Observational study, 2000. Roseboom, Heart.

  5. Severe malnutrition or famine during childhood is linked to an increased risk of cardiometabolic NCDs.

    “Severe malnutrition or famine during childhood is associated with increased risk of cardiometabolic NCDs, suggesting that developmental plasticity extends beyond prenatal life.”

    Systematic review, 2021. Grey et al., BMJ Global Health.

  6. No evidence of transgenerational effects of grandmaternal under-nutrition during gestation was found on the health of the relatively young F2 generation group.

    “We found no evidence of transgenerational effects of grandmaternal under-nutrition during gestation on the health of this relatively young group”

    Observational study, 2013. Veenendaal et al., BJOG An International Journal of Obstetrics & Gynaecology.

The legacy of hunger is not always consistent.

While some studies link undernourished fathers to heavier offspring, others show no evidence of these effects on the third generation. Some findings may be driven by age differences rather than biology.

Your health carries a hidden history.

That may be why certain metabolic conditions appear in families without clear lifestyle causes. Your body may be responding to environmental stresses from previous generations.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that prenatal famine exposure is linked to various metabolic and cardiovascular health issues in offspring and grandchildren, but the strength and consistency of these transgenerational effects vary.

Many studies are observational and face challenges such as confounding by age differences, famine intensity, and lack of firm causality.
