Does your child know why they want that snack?

File no. 0103October 16, 2026Modern society

Knowing how advertising works does not protect children.

Understanding the tactics of marketing fails to shield children from its impact on their brand and product attitudes.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Greater understanding of advertising did not protect against the impact of advertising on brand or product attitudes.

    “We found greater understanding did not protect against the impact of advertising on brand or product attitudes.”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Packer et al., PEDIATRICS.

  2. Online ('advergame') advertising combined with TV advertising causes a stronger influence on children's food consumption than TV advertising alone.

    “CONCLUSION: Online ('advergame') advertising combined with TV advertising exerted a stronger influence on children's food consumption than TV advertising alone.”

    Randomized trial, 2018. Norman et al., International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity.

  3. In children exposed to unhealthy dietary marketing, dietary intake significantly increased by a mean difference [MD] = 30.4 kcal (95% confidence interval [CI] 2.9 to 57.9) during or shortly after exposure to advertisements.

    “Our meta-analysis showed that in children exposed to unhealthy dietary marketing, dietary intake significantly increased (mean difference [MD] = 30. 4 kcal, 95% confidence interval [CI] 2. 9 to 57. 9, and MD = 4. 8 g, 95%CI 0. 8 to 8. 8) during or shortly after exposure to advertisements.”

    Meta-analysis, 2016. Sadeghirad et al., Obesity Reviews.

  4. Children exposed to food advertising on TV (11 studies) and advergames (five studies) respectively consumed an average 60.0 kcal (95% confidence interval [CI], 3.1-116.9) and 53.2 kcal (95% CI, 31.5-74.9) more than children exposed to nonfood advertising.

    “Meta-analysis revealed that children exposed to food advertising on TV (11 studies) and advergames (five studies) respectively consumed an average 60. 0 kcal (95% confidence interval [CI], 3. 1-116. 9) and 53. 2 kcal (95% CI, 31. 5-74. 9) more than children exposed to nonfood advertising.”

    Systematic review, 2018. Russell et al., Obesity Reviews.

  5. Acute exposure to food advertising causes increased food intake among children and adults.

    “These findings suggest that acute exposure to food advertising increases food intake among children and adults and that the middle occipital gyrus is an implicated brain region among children.”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Cardoza et al., Advances in Nutrition.

Combined media sources increase food consumption.

When children see food ads on TV and play advergames, the influence on their food consumption is stronger than TV ads alone.

The environment shapes dietary habits.

That may be why children develop specific food preferences and increased intake shortly after exposure to unhealthy marketing.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence consistently shows that exposure to unhealthy food marketing leads to increased dietary intake and influences preferences in children.

Many studies were at high risk of bias, and some research focuses on acute exposure rather than long-term real-world effects.
