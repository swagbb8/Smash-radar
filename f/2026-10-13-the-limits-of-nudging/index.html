Nudge effects drop when the intervention is removed.

In 68% of studies, choice architecture changed behavior while the intervention was present. This figure falls to 47% once the intervention is removed.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. In 68% of studies, choice architecture had an effect on behavior in the presence of the intervention, but this dropped to 47% after the intervention was removed.

    “In presence of the intervention, 68% of the studies reported an effect of choice architecture on behavior, whereas after removal of the intervention only 47% of the studies reported a significant effect.”

    Systematic review, 2020. Landais et al., International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity.

  2. The average effect size of choice architecture interventions is 0.45.

    “= 0.45 (95% CI [0.39, 0.52]).”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Mertens et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  3. Food choices are particularly responsive to choice architecture interventions, with effect sizes up to 2.5 times larger than those in other behavioral domains.

    “Food choices are particularly responsive to choice architecture interventions, with effect sizes up to 2.5 times larger than those in other behavioral domains.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Mertens et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  4. Changing choice architecture causes a 1.40 effect size in tourism-related environmental behavior, while leveraging social norms has a limited 0.18 effect size.

    “Changing choice architecture ( d = 1.40) and increasing pleasure ( d = 0.66) emerge as the most effective approaches. ... leveraging social norms to trigger sustainable behavior ( d = 0.18) have limited effectiveness.”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Greene et al., Journal of Travel Research.

  5. Interventions that target the organization and structure of choice alternatives consistently outperform interventions that focus on the description of alternatives or the reinforcement of behavioral intentions.

    “interventions that target the organization and structure of choice alternatives (decision structure) consistently outperform interventions that focus on the description of alternatives (decision information) or the reinforcement of behavioral intentions (decision assistance).”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Mertens et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  6. Nudging interventions that aim to increase fruit and/or vegetable choice/sales/servings have a moderately significant effect of 0.30.

    “The meta-analysis shows that nudging interventions that aim to increase fruit and/or vegetable choice/sales/servings have a moderately significant effect (d = 0. 30)”

    Meta-analysis, 2017. Broers et al., European Journal of Public Health.

Not all nudges are created equal.

Changing the structure of choices often performs better than describing alternatives or reinforcing intentions. The effectiveness of a nudge varies significantly by the specific domain and technique used.

The influence may be temporary.

That may be why some habits seem to change instantly but revert quickly. The nudge might be shifting your immediate path rather than your underlying behavior.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that nudges have measurable effects that vary by context, with specific techniques like decision structure performing better than others.

The analysis is complicated by a moderate publication bias toward positive results and a lack of standardized reporting practices in the field.
