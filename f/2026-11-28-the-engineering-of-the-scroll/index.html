What the research found

  1. Social media addiction is formalized as entrapment in an engineered wrong attractor built through intentional variable-ratio reinforcement design.

    “We apply the Displacement Framework to social media addiction, formalizing it as entrapment in an engineered wrong attractor built through intentional variable-ratio reinforcement design.”

    Preprint, not peer reviewed, 2026. Phronesis, Zenodo (CERN European Organization for Nuclear Research).

  2. The design of compulsion loops by attention engineers is ethically equivalent to designing addictive products like tobacco.

    “The intentional design of compulsion loops by attention engineers is analyzed as ethically equivalent to designing addictive products, with tobacco regulation as historical precedent.”

    Preprint, not peer reviewed, 2026. Phronesis, Zenodo (CERN European Organization for Nuclear Research).

  3. Infinite scrolling replaces deep interpersonal relations with fragmented, ephemeral interactions.

    “It replaces deep, sustained interpersonal relations with fragmented, ephemeral interactions, mediated by algorithmic infrastructures optimized for retention rather than human connection.”

    Study, 2026. Filipek, Addiction & Social Media Communication.

  4. Digital addiction is an overarching concept that is closely related to scrolling syndrome.

    “This was done as digital addiction is an overarching concept in relation to scrolling syndrome and closely related to the same.”

    Study, 2023. Sinha et al., Journal of Public Health and Primary Care.

  5. Scrolling syndrome shares common criteria with other clinical disorders.

    “Even for other clinical disorders, common criteria shared with scrolling syndrome have been listed as clinical markers”

    Study, 2023. Sinha et al., Journal of Public Health and Primary Care.

  6. Infinite scrolling immerses users in algorithmic loops that are difficult to break because there are no natural stopping points.

    “By continuously loading content without natural stopping points (e. g. the end of the page), infinite scrolling immerses users in algorithmic loops that are difficult to break.”

    Study, 2026. Filipek, Addiction & Social Media Communication.
