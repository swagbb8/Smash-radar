Why do you trust a machine with your secrets?

File no. 0022October 8, 2026The future

You tell chatbots the same secrets you tell humans.

People disclose equally intimate information to chatbots and humans.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. People disclose equally intimate information to chatbots and humans.

    “Results showed that there was no difference in the self-reported intimacy of self-disclosure between the human and chatbot conditions.”

    Experiment, 2024. Croes et al., Interacting with Computers.

  2. Evidence for sustained loneliness reduction in open-domain companion systems remains emerging.

    “Therapeutic chatbot studies provided the strongest evidence for short-term symptom reduction in selected contexts, whereas evidence for sustained loneliness reduction in open-domain companion systems remained emerging.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Li et al., Frontiers in artificial intelligence.

  3. Conversational agents may offer scalable support for reducing loneliness and depression in older adults.

    “These findings indicate that conversational agents may offer scalable support for older adults' mental health, with potential especially for reducing loneliness and depression.”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Satake et al., Psychological Medicine.

  4. AI companions are linked to momentary reductions in loneliness after use over the course of a week.

    “Study 3 uses a longitudinal design and finds that an AI companion consistently provides momentary reductions in loneliness after use over the course of a week.”

    Observational study, 2025. Freitas et al., Journal of Consumer Research.

  5. Reported and hypothesized risks of companion agents include dependency-like use and displacement of human interaction.

    “Reported and hypothesized risks included dependency-like use, displacement of human interaction, maladaptive validation or sycophancy, persuasive manipulation, privacy harms, and risks to minors or vulnerable users.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Li et al., Frontiers in artificial intelligence.

  6. Eight studies reported statistically significant improvements in anxiety, depression, or well-being in college students.

    “Nine studies (n=1,082 participants) were included, with eight reported statistically significant improvements in anxiety (e. g. , GAD-7 reductions), depression (e. g. , PHQ-9 scores), or well-being.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Nyakhar & Wang, Frontiers in Psychiatry.

The evidence for long-term relief is mixed.

Chatbots provide momentary reductions in loneliness and may offer scalable support for some. However, evidence for sustained loneliness reduction is still emerging.

The risk of replacement is real.

Because these agents provide immediate relief, there is a risk of dependency and the displacement of human interaction. That may be why the screen feels safer than a person.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A quiet room at 2 a.m.

The honest summary

The evidence suggests that chatbots can provide immediate relief and high levels of self-disclosure, but their long-term effectiveness for loneliness is not yet fully established.

Many studies suffer from high attrition rates, reliance on self-reported outcomes, and a lack of rigorous randomized controlled trials.
