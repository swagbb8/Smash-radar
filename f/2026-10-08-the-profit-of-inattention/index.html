The world you don’t see

Why do you keep paying for things you don't use?

File no. 0031October 8, 2026Money and power

Inattention doubles seller revenues.

Cancellation frictions driven by inattention or switching costs roughly double seller revenues on average. This finding is based on stylized models rather than a direct experimental trial.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Cancellation frictions driven by inattention or switching costs roughly double seller revenues on average, holding fixed initial subscribers.

    “Using two stylized models of consumer inertia—driven by inattention or switching costs—we estimate that these cancellation frictions roughly double seller revenues on average, holding fixed initial subscribers.”

    Study, 2025. Einav et al., American Economic Review.

  2. Bundling more add-on services can significantly decrease customer retention during the onboarding stage.

    “A field study involving a B2B SaaS provider further reveals that bundling more add-on services can significantly decrease customer retention during the onboarding stage.”

    Observational study, 2025. Steinhoff et al., Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science.

  3. Months during which cards are replaced, when active renewal is required, are associated with much higher rates of cancellation.

    “We use comprehensive data from a large payment card network to document that months during which cards are replaced, when active renewal is required, are associated with much higher rates of cancellation.”

    Study, 2025. Einav et al., American Economic Review.

  4. Complexity perceptions are the likely source of the detrimental effects of add-on bundling during the customer onboarding stage.

    “Finally, a cross-industry survey of B2B managers identifies complexity perceptions as the likely source of these detrimental effects of add-on bundling during the customer onboarding stage.”

    Observational study, 2025. Steinhoff et al., Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science.

  5. The effectiveness of digital goods subscriptions remains controversial in terms of the profitability for different supply chain members, including content providers and retailers.

    “However, the effectiveness of digital goods subscriptions remains controversial in terms of the profitability for different supply chain members, including content providers and retailers.”

    Study, 2026. Jang et al., Production and Operations Management.

Complexity creates friction.

Adding more services can decrease customer retention during onboarding. This is likely because the perceived complexity of the bundle deters people from staying.

The architecture of choice.

That may be why systems are designed to be difficult to leave. Profit relies on your willingness to overlook the effort of clicking away.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The monthly bill.

You sit at your kitchen table. You see a charge on your bank app for a service you haven't used in months. You decide it is too much effort to find the cancel button tonight.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while inattention creates significant profit for sellers, other factors like service complexity can negatively impact customer retention.

One study showing the link between inattention and revenue is based on stylized models rather than a direct experimental trial.
