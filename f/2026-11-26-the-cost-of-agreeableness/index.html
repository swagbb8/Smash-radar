Does being nice make you less happy?

File no. 0334November 26, 2026Human relationships

High agreeableness correlates with higher marital satisfaction.

The data shows that being agreeable is linked to better relationships and positive affect, rather than lower life satisfaction. The evidence on its impact on overall well-being remains mixed.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. Agreeableness is linked to a correlation coefficient of -0.12* to -0.353*** with higher levels of job burnout.

    “lower agreeableness (r from - 0. 12* to - 0. 353***; β from - 0. 08*** to - 0. 523*)”

    Systematic review, 2023. Angelini, BMC Psychology.

  2. Agreeableness is linked to a correlation coefficient of 0.855 with marital satisfaction.

    “r = 0. 855 with agreeableness (95% CI: 0. 80-0. 90)”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Sayehmiri et al., BMC Psychology.

  3. Agreeableness is linked to improved orthodontic outcomes and more favorable treatment attitudes.

    “positive traits, including extraversion, agreeableness, and conscientiousness, were correlated with improved orthodontic outcomes and more favorable treatment attitudes.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Nicita et al., Applied Sciences.

  4. Agreeableness is linked to predicting positive affect as well as Extraversion.

    “Positive affect was predicted equally well by Extraversion and Agreeableness.”

    Meta-analysis, 1998. DeNeve & Cooper, Psychological Bulletin.

  5. The Big Five traits were moderately associated with overall health (multiple R = . 35).

    “When entered simultaneously, the Big Five traits were moderately associated with overall health (multiple R = . 35).”

    Meta-analysis, 2017. Strickhouser et al., Health Psychology.

Agreeableness creates different outcomes for different lives.

Agreeableness is linked to improved orthodontic outcomes and favorable treatment attitudes. However, it is also linked to higher levels of job burnout.

The honest summary

The evidence does not support the idea that high agreeableness leads to lower life satisfaction; instead, it is linked to higher marital satisfaction and positive affect.

The sources provide mixed results on how agreeableness impacts different types of well-being, such as marital satisfaction versus job burnout.
