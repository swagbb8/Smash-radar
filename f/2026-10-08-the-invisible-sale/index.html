Your brain is being hijacked

Can you tell the difference between news and an ad?

File no. 0035October 8, 2026Social media

You are likely being sold to right now.

Fewer than 1 in 10 people can recognize native advertising. This means the vast majority of content you consume is actually paid promotion.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Fewer than 1 in 10 participants were able to recognize native advertising.

    “Although fewer than 1 in 10 participants were able to recognize native advertising, our study shows that effectively designed disclosure labels facilitate recognition.”

    Experiment, 2018. Amazeen & Wojdynski, Journalism.

  2. Recognition of a natively formatted ad was triggered mostly by prior experience with social media ads, rather than ad disclosure.

    “Findings suggest that recognition of the natively formatted ad was triggered mostly by prior experience with social media ads, rather than ad disclosure.”

    Experiment, 2018. Jung & Heo, Journal of Interactive Advertising.

  3. A prominent disclosure enhances users’ perceived sponsorship transparency when the higher congruence makes it harder to discern a genuine endorsement from a sponsored one.

    “Moreover, a prominent disclosure enhances users’ perceived sponsorship transparency when the higher congruence makes it harder to discern a genuine endorsement from a sponsored one.”

    Experiment, 2020. Cicco et al., International Journal of Advertising.

  4. When the position of an ad on a news page is controlled for, a native ad generates a higher click-through rate because it better resembles the surrounding editorial content.

    “The results show that when the position of an ad on a news page is controlled for, a native ad generates a higher click-through rate because it better resembles the surrounding editorial content.”

    Experiment, 2019. Aribarg & Schwartz, Journal of Marketing Research.

  5. A display ad leads to more visual attention, brand recognition, and trustworthiness for the website than a native ad.

    “However, a display ad leads to more visual attention, brand recognition, and trustworthiness for the website than a native ad.”

    Experiment, 2019. Aribarg & Schwartz, Journal of Marketing Research.

  6. Middle or bottom positioning and wording using “advertising” or “sponsored” increased advertising recognition compared to other conditions.

    “Findings show that middle or bottom positioning and wording using “advertising” or “sponsored” increased advertising recognition compared to other conditions”

    Experiment, 2015. Wojdynski & Evans, Journal of Advertising.

Disclosures do not always work.

Recognition of native ads often comes from your past experience with social media rather than labels. When an ad looks like the content around it, it generates higher click-through rates.

The line between news and ads is blurring.

That may be why you find yourself clicking on articles that feel like genuine recommendations. When ads mimic the style of editorial content, they bypass your usual skepticism.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

An article on your news feed.

You see a story about a new travel destination. It looks like a report from a journalist. You click it because it fits the flow of the page. It is actually a paid placement.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that native advertising is frequently difficult to recognize, but the effectiveness of disclosures varies significantly depending on their design, position, and the user's prior experience.

The research highlights that recognition is not solely dependent on disclosure, as factors like influencer-product fit and prior consumer knowledge also play significant roles.
