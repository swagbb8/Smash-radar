Your brain is being hijacked

Who chooses what you buy?

File no. 0270November 11, 2026Science and reality

Your choices are linked to neurobiological systems.

Consumer behavior is driven by a balance between impulsive rewards and executive functions. This is a contested finding regarding the exact timing of these choices.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Addiction is linked to an imbalance between an impulsive system that values immediate rewards and an executive system that values delayed rewards.

    “pathological patterns of responding result from hyperactivity in the evolutionarily older impulsive system (which values immediate and low-cost reinforcers) and/or hypoactivity in the more recently evolved executive system (which is involved in the valuation of delayed reinforcers).”

    Study, 2011. Bickel et al., Current psychiatry reports.

  2. Consumer behavior toward circular economy activities is linked to the function of memory and cognitive biases.

    “Key areas include the function of memory in establishing sustainable consumption patterns, cognitive biases that influence sustainability preferences, and ethical implications when using neuroscientific results to promote circular economy practices.”

    Study, 2024. Islam et al., Advances in logistics, operations, and management science book series.

  3. Neuroeconomics can be used to analyze consumer behavior in markets by prioritizing product variety to stimulate curiosity and creating engaging environments.

    “The research emphasizes the importance of prioritizing biosafety and cleanliness measures, maintaining product variety to stimulate consumer curiosity, and creating engaging market environments.”

    Study, 2025. Silva et al., Journal of Lifestyle and SDGs Review.

  4. Consumer behavior in the food market is linked to the interference of emotional, social, cognitive, and other factors.

    “It recognizes the impact of emotional, social, cognitive and other factors on the economic behavior of individual in the food market.”

    Study, 2016. Jakub et al., Nihon Keiei Gakkaishi.

The biology of your spending.

Markets prioritize product variety to stimulate curiosity. Your behavior is influenced by emotional, social, and cognitive factors. Memory and cognitive biases also shape your actions.

The architecture of your desires.

That may be why you feel a pull toward certain products. Your brain may be responding to environments designed to trigger specific reward systems.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A walk through the supermarket.

You stand in an aisle. The variety of items creates a sense of curiosity. You reach for a specific brand based on a memory or a social cue.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that consumer behavior is driven by neurobiological and psychological factors like cognitive biases and reward-seeking systems, but it does not specifically confirm that the brain selects options seconds before conscious awareness.

The sources focus on the variables influencing behavior rather than providing specific timing data on the speed of subconscious decision-making.
