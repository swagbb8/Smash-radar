Is your personality a biological byproduct?

File no. 0138October 21, 2026Science and reality

Your microbiome predicts your personality traits.

Specific bacterial genera in your gut are significantly predicted by your personality traits. This link is supported by research but is not yet proven as a direct cause.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The microbiota acts like an endocrine organ by secreting serotonin, dopamine or other neurotransmitters and may control the HPA axis in athletes.

    “indicating that the microbiota acts like an endocrine organ (e. g. secreting serotonin, dopamine or other neurotransmitters) and may control the HPA axis in athletes.”

    Systematic review, 2016. Clark & Mach, Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition.

  2. The abundances of specific bacterial genera are significantly predicted by personality traits.

    “Using regression models to control for possible confounding factors, the abundances of specific bacterial genera are shown to be significantly predicted by personality traits.”

    Study, 2019. Johnson, Human Microbiome Journal.

  3. Higher alpha diversity is associated with greater Surgency/Extraversion and High-Intensity Pleasure in males, and lower Effortful Control in females.

    “The findings suggest that there is an association between higher alpha diversity and temperament: greater Surgency/Extraversion and High-Intensity Pleasure in males, and lower Effortful Control in females.”

    Systematic review, 2022. Alving‐Jessep et al., Developmental Psychobiology.

  4. Fecal microbiota transplantation from chronic unpredictable mild stress mice donors affects anxiety-like and depression-like behavior in recipient mice via the gut microbiota-inflammation-brain axis.

    “Fecal microbiota transplantation from chronic unpredictable mild stress mice donors affects anxiety-like and depression-like behavior in recipient mice via the gut microbiota-inflammation-brain axis.”

    Study, 2019. Li et al., Stress.

  5. People with larger social networks tend to have a more diverse microbiome.

    “Diversity analyses of the gut microbiome reveal that people with larger social networks tend to have a more diverse microbiome, suggesting that social interactions may shape the microbial community of the human gut.”

    Study, 2019. Johnson, Human Microbiome Journal.

  6. Positive temperament traits are associated with gut microbial communities biased toward short-chain fatty acid production from a metabolism based on dietary fiber and complex carbohydrates.

    “An emerging pattern of positive temperament traits being associated with GM communities biased toward short-chain fatty acid production from a metabolism based on dietary fiber and complex carbohydrates was observed”

    Systematic review, 2022. Alving‐Jessep et al., Developmental Psychobiology.

Bacteria influence your temperament and stress.

The microbiota acts like an endocrine organ, secreting neurotransmitters like serotonin and dopamine. It may control the HPA axis. Research also links microbial diversity to extraversion and effortful control.

Your biology is a two-way street.

Your gut shapes your behavior, but your social networks and diet also shape your microbiome. Your personality and your bacteria are constantly influencing each other.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The coffee you drink and the people you meet.

You sit in a cafe with friends, eating fiber-rich bread. Every interaction and every bite alters the community of organisms inside you, subtly shifting your internal chemistry.

The honest summary

The evidence shows a measurable link between gut microbiome composition and behavioral traits like personality and temperament, but it does not definitively prove that the microbiome is the primary cause of personality.

Much of the evidence relies on observational associations or animal models rather than direct causal proof in humans.
