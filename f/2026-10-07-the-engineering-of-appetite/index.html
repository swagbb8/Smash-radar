Why do you eat more without feeling full?

File no. 0019October 7, 2026Health and environment

You eat more without noticing.

In a controlled trial, an ultra-processed diet caused an energy intake 508 kcal/day greater than an unprocessed diet. The evidence is mixed.,,

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. High consumption of breakfast cereals is linked to a lower mortality risk.

    “However, breakfast cereals were associated with a lower mortality risk (RR = 0. 85, 95% CI, 0. 79, 0. 92).”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Taneri et al., American Journal of Epidemiology.

  2. Each 10% increase in ultra-processed food consumption in daily calorie intake is linked to a 15% higher risk of all-cause mortality.

    “each 10% increase in UPF consumption in daily calorie intake was associated with a 15% higher risk of all-cause mortality (OR = 1. 15; 95% CI: 1. 09, 1. 21; I2 = 0. 0%; p < 0. 001).”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Suksatan et al., Nutrients.

  3. In a trial of 20 weight-stable adults, an ultra-processed diet caused an energy intake that was 508 ± 106 kcal/day greater than an unprocessed diet.

    “Energy intake was greater during the ultra-processed diet (508 ± 106 kcal/day; p = 0. 0001), with increased consumption of carbohydrate (280 ± 54 kcal/day; p < 0. 0001) and fat (230 ± 53 kcal/day; p = 0. 0004), but not protein (-2 ± 12 kcal/day; p = 0. 85).”

    Randomized trial, 2019. Hall et al., Cell metabolism.

  4. Highest consumption of ultra-processed foods is linked to an increased risk of all-cause mortality in five studies.

    “highest UPF consumption was found to be associated with increased risk of all-cause mortality in five studies (risk ratio (RR) 1·25, 95 % CI 1·14, 1·37; P < 0·00001)”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Pagliai et al., British Journal Of Nutrition. This shows a link, not a cause.

The data is contradictory.

While ultra-processed foods are linked to a 15% higher risk of all-cause mortality, high consumption of breakfast cereals is linked to a lower mortality risk.

Design influences your appetite.

That may be why ultra-processed foods lead to higher calorie intake in controlled settings. Your choices are influenced by how your food is engineered.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that ultra-processed foods cause higher calorie intake and weight gain in a controlled setting and are linked to increased mortality risks in observational studies.

The evidence for mortality risk comes from observational studies, while the evidence for increased calorie intake comes from a small trial of only 20 adults.
