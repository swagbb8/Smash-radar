Are you mostly human?

File no. 0021October 8, 2026Science and reality

You are not ten times more human than microbe.

The number of bacteria in your body is of the same order as your own human cells.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. The number of bacteria in the body is of the same order as the number of human cells.

    “Our analysis also updates the widely-cited 10:1 ratio, showing that the number of bacteria in the body is actually of the same order as the number of human cells”

    Study, 2016. Sender et al., PLoS biology.

  2. The number of bacteria in a 70 kg man is estimated to be 3.8·1013.

    “We estimate the total number of bacteria in the 70 kg "reference man" to be 3.8·1013.”

    Study, 2016. Sender et al., PLoS biology.

  3. The total mass of bacteria in the body is about 0.2 kg.

    “and their total mass is about 0.2 kg.”

    Study, 2016. Sender et al., PLoS biology.

  4. The number of human cells in the body is revised to 3.0·1013.

    “For human cells, we identify the dominant role of the hematopoietic lineage to the total count (≈90%) and revise past estimates to 3.0·1013 human cells.”

    Study, 2016. Sender et al., PLoS biology.

  5. The total body counts are estimated at ≈36 trillion cells in the male, ≈28 trillion in the female, and ≈17 trillion in the child.

    “In total, we estimate total body counts of ≈36 trillion cells in the male, ≈28 trillion in the female, and ≈17 trillion in the child.”

    Study, 2023. Hatton et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

The math of your body.

In a 70 kg man, the count of bacteria is about 3.8·1013. The count of human cells is revised to 3.0·1013.

A shared existence.

That may be why the line between yourself and your microbiome is less of a wall and more of a shared space.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A quiet breath.

You sit in a chair and breathe. You feel like a single person, but you are a host for trillions of lives moving in sync with yours.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that bacterial cell counts are roughly comparable to human cell counts, contradicting the common 10:1 ratio.

The bacterial count is based on a 70 kg "reference man" and does not account for individual variation.
