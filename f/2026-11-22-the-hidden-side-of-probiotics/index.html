Is your probiotic helping the wrong bacteria?

File no. 0318November 22, 2026Health and environment

Probiotics can act as a reservoir for antibiotic resistance.

The most popular probiotic, Lactobacillus, can harbor genes that protect bacteria from antibiotics. These genes can then move from the probiotic to harmful bacteria in your body.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. The most popular probiotic, Lactobacillus, is a potential reservoir of antibiotic resistance.

    “Lactobacillus , the most popular probiotic, has recently gained more attention because it is a potential reservoir of antibiotic resistance.”

    Systematic review, 2023. Shahali et al., Research in Pharmaceutical Sciences.

  2. Nine studies reported the transfer of antibiotic resistance from Lactobacillus probiotic as donor species to pathogenic bacteria, mostly using in vitro methods.

    “Nine of these studies reported the transfer of antibiotic resistance from Lactobacillus probiotic as donor species to pathogenic bacteria and mostly used in vitro methods for resistance gene transfer.”

    Systematic review, 2023. Shahali et al., Research in Pharmaceutical Sciences.

  3. Horizontal gene transfer (HGT) is driven by 2 bacteriophages carried by the resident strain, which cause an epidemic phage infection of the invader.

    “HGT is driven by 2 bacteriophages carried by the resident strain, which cause an epidemic phage infection of the invader.”

    Experiment, 2019. Frazão et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  4. The transferability of resistance genes such as tet and erm in Lactobacillus increases the risk of spreading antibiotic resistance.

    “The transferability of resistance genes such as tet and erm in Lactobacillus increases the risk of spreading antibiotic resistance.”

    Systematic review, 2023. Shahali et al., Research in Pharmaceutical Sciences.

  5. Genes uptaken by HGT enhance the metabolism of specific gut carbon sources and provide a fitness advantage to lysogenic invader lineages.

    “We show that the genes uptaken by HGT enhance the metabolism of specific gut carbon sources and provide a fitness advantage to lysogenic invader lineages.”

    Experiment, 2019. Frazão et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  6. Duplicated antibiotic resistance genes are highly enriched in bacteria isolated from humans and livestock.

    “Duplicated ARGs are highly enriched in bacteria isolated from humans and livestock.”

    Experiment, 2024. Maddamsetti et al., Nature Communications.

How bacteria share resistance.

Bacteriophages, which are viruses that carry the probiotic strains, drive horizontal gene transfer. This process allows resistance genes to move to invader bacteria, giving them a survival advantage.

The risk in your gut.

That may be why your gut microbiome is more complex than a simple balance of good and bad. Probiotics can inadvertently spread resistance to pathogens, potentially making infections harder to treat.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A morning routine.

You eat a bowl of yogurt with live cultures to stay healthy. You believe you are strengthening your system, but you may be introducing genes that help pathogens survive.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that Lactobacillus probiotics can harbor and transfer antibiotic resistance genes to pathogens, particularly in laboratory settings.

Most evidence regarding the transfer of resistance from probiotics to pathogens was conducted using in vitro methods rather than in humans.
