The world you don’t see

Is your personality a choice or a construction?

File no. 0303November 19, 2026Human relationships

Economic inequality shapes how you see yourself.

When you perceive high levels of economic inequality, you develop a more independent self-construal. This shift is linked to the social structures around you.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Perceived high economic inequality leads to a more independent and less interdependent self-construal compared to the low economic inequality condition.

    “In Study 2, using an experimental design, we found that perceived high economic inequality leads to a more independent and less interdependent self-construal compared to the low economic inequality condition.”

    Experiment, 2017. Sánchez‐Rodríguez et al., International Journal of Psychology.

  2. Interdependent self-construal facilitated participants' recovery from some of the negative effects of ostracism, but did not have an impact on the initial pain.

    “Interdependent self‐construal facilitated participants' recovery from some of the negative effects of ostracism, but did not have an impact on the initial pain.”

    Study, 2013. Ren et al., Asian Journal Of Social Psychology.

  3. Shared identity and group membership are important for developing and sustaining positive identities.

    “This review highlights the important role that shared identity and group membership play in developing and sustaining positive identities.”

    Systematic review, 2020. Rushton & Reiss, Studies in Science Education.

  4. People who perceive lower levels of economic inequality tend to show higher levels of interdependent self-construal.

    “Correlational data from Studies 1a and 1b revealed that people who perceive lower levels of economic inequality tend to show higher levels of interdependent self-construal, even after controlling for social class.”

    Experiment, 2017. Sánchez‐Rodríguez et al., International Journal of Psychology.

  5. Studies that view professional identity as socially constructed are common in fields like management, organizational, medical tourism, and neuroscience.

    “Studies that envisage professional identity as socially constructed dominate in management, organizational, medical tourism, and neuroscience.”

    Systematic review, 2019. Kasperiūnienė & Žydžiūnaitė, SAGE Open.

Your identity is a social construct.

Research views professional identity as a social construction across many fields. Shared identity and group membership are the foundations for your sense of self.

Your sense of self is not internal.

That may be why your personality feels different depending on your environment. Your independence or interdependence might be a response to the economic landscape you inhabit.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A commute through a divided city.

You sit in a car or on a train. You look at the distance between luxury high-rises and crumbling apartments. You feel a shift in how you view your own place in the world.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that identity is heavily shaped by social structures and group memberships, but it also demonstrates that self-construal can shift based on economic perceptions.

The research focuses heavily on specific contexts like professional identity, social media, and economic inequality rather than providing a universal theory of the self.
