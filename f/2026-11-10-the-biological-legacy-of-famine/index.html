Is your metabolism inherited from your ancestors' hunger?

File no. 0264November 10, 2026History

Famine effects may be inherited across generations.

Non-genetic heredity from events like famine is often mediated by epigenetic regulations of gene expression. These changes may be transferred even across several generations.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Non-genetic heredity of effects from events like famine is often mediated by epigenetic regulations of gene expression and may be transferred even across several generations.

    “This heredity is often mediated by epigenetic regulations of gene expression and may be transferred even across several generations.”

    Review, 2023. Švorcová, Genes.

  2. Methodological shortcomings in studies of the 20th-century Dutch and Chinese famines may have led to the misinterpretation of some findings.

    “Such methodological shortcomings may have led to the misinterpretation of some findings.”

    Review, 2026. Khatib et al., Frontiers in genetics. This shows a link, not a cause.

  3. The effects of famine might be heritable through epigenetics.

    “research on the Dutch Hunger Winter (1944–1945), which indicated transgenerational transmission of biological effects through epigenetics (i.e., that the effects of famine might be “heritable”).”

    Study, 2026. Kerr & Dumitriu, Leonardo.

  4. POMC DNA methylation in children is linked to elevated lipids and insulin, independent of body habitus.

    “Studies in children propose a link between POMC DNA methylation and elevated lipids and insulin, independent of body habitus.”

    Review, 2019. Candler et al., Frontiers in Neuroendocrinology.

  5. Stress exposure from events like famine is linked to changes in hormonal production, the HPA axis, cognition, behavior, or a propensity to certain psychiatric or metabolic disorders.

    “The reviewed studies show a plethora of universal changes which stress exposure initiates on multiple levels of organisation ranging from hormonal production and the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis modulation all the way to cognition, behaviour, or propensity to certain psychiatric or metabolic disorders.”

    Review, 2023. Švorcová, Genes.

  6. POMC DNA methylation is linked to maternal nutrition in early pregnancy and is associated with childhood and adult obesity.

    “Recent human studies suggest POMC DNA methylation is influenced by maternal nutrition in early pregnancy and associated with childhood and adult obesity.”

    Review, 2019. Candler et al., Frontiers in Neuroendocrinology.

Nutritional deprivation programs your metabolism.

Maternal nutrition in early pregnancy is linked to POMC DNA methylation. This is associated with childhood and adult obesity and linked to elevated lipids and insulin.

Your health history is not just your own.

Stress exposure from events like famine is linked to changes in hormonal production and a propensity to certain metabolic disorders. That may be why your health profile feels predetermined.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The sources provide evidence that famine and early-life nutrition can cause epigenetic changes and metabolic programming that may be passed across generations.

Some existing studies on the Dutch and Chinese famines may contain methodological shortcomings that could lead to the misinterpretation of findings.
