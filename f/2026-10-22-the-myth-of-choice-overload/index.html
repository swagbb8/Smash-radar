When does your freedom feel like a burden?

File no. 0148October 22, 2026Psychology

Choice overload is not a universal rule.

A meta-analysis of 63 conditions found a mean effect size of virtually zero for choice overload.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. A meta-analysis of 63 conditions from 50 published and unpublished experiments (N=5,036) found a mean effect size of virtually zero for choice overload.

    “In a meta-analysis of 63 conditions from 50 published and unpublished experiments (N=5,036), we found a mean effect size of virtually zero but considerable variance between studies.”

    Meta-analysis, 2010. Scheibehenne et al., Journal of Consumer Research.

  2. More choices may instead facilitate choice and increase satisfaction.

    “A number of studies found strong instances of choice overload in the lab and in the field, but others found no such effects or found that more choices may instead facilitate choice and increase satisfaction.”

    Meta-analysis, 2010. Scheibehenne et al., Journal of Consumer Research.

  3. When moderating variables are taken into account the overall effect of assortment size on choice overload is significant.

    “Finally, we document that when moderating variables are taken into account the overall effect of assortment size on choice overload is significant—a finding counter to the data reported by prior meta-analytic research.”

    Review, 2014. Chernev et al., Journal of Consumer Psychology.

  4. Higher levels of decision task difficulty, greater choice set complexity, higher preference uncertainty, and a more prominent, effort‐minimizing goal facilitate choice overload.

    “We further show that each of these four factors has a reliable and significant impact on choice overload, whereby higher levels of decision task difficulty, greater choice set complexity, higher preference uncertainty, and a more prominent, effort‐minimizing goal facilitate choice overload.”

    Review, 2014. Chernev et al., Journal of Consumer Psychology.

  5. Diversification increases the perceived diversity and attractiveness of the item set, while at the same time reducing the perceived difficulty of choosing from the set.

    “Study 1a tests the effectiveness of the latent feature diversification, and shows that diversification increases the perceived diversity and attractiveness of the item set, while at the same time reducing the perceived difficulty of choosing from the set.”

    Experiment, 2016. Willemsen et al., User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction.

  6. Diversification can increase users’ satisfaction with the chosen option, especially when they are choosing from small, diverse item sets.

    “Study 1b subsequently shows that diversification can increase users’ satisfaction with the chosen option, especially when they are choosing from small, diverse item sets.”

    Experiment, 2016. Willemsen et al., User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction.

Conditions dictate your experience.

Choice overload is significant only when tasks are difficult, sets are complex, preferences are uncertain, and you aim to minimize effort.

Diversity can simplify your world.

That may be why a diverse set of items can increase your satisfaction and reduce the perceived difficulty of choosing.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A shelf of spices.

You stand in a kitchen aisle. You look at a wall of different jars. Some are familiar. Others are new.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while choice overload can occur under specific conditions, it is not a consistent effect and can sometimes be mitigated or even reversed by factors like diversification.

No sufficient conditions for choice overload could be identified in the meta-analysis of 63 conditions.
