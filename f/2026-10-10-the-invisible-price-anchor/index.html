Who sets the price you are willing to pay?

File no. 0061October 10, 2026Psychology

The first number you hear sets the price.

Your brain uses a preceding number as a reference point to make judgments about what you are willing to pay.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Indirect methods for measuring hypothetical willingness to pay actually overestimate real willingness to pay significantly stronger than direct methods.

    “In contrast with conventional wisdom, indirect methods actually overestimate RWTP significantly stronger than direct methods.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Schmidt & Bijmolt, Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science.

  2. There is evidence for a moderate perceptual anchoring deficit in individuals with dyslexia.

    “Of the included studies, we find evidence for a moderate perceptual anchoring deficit in individuals with dyslexia.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Shulver & Badcock, Journal of Speech Language and Hearing Research.

  3. The average hypothetical bias is 21%, which is the difference between a hypothetically measured willingness to pay and a consumer's real willingness to pay.

    “First, on average, the hypothetical bias is 21%, and this study provides a reference point for the expected magnitude of the hypothetical bias.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Schmidt & Bijmolt, Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science.

  4. The hypothetical bias is greater for higher valued products, specialty goods, and within-subject designs.

    “Third, the hypothetical bias is greater for higher valued products, specialty goods (cf. other product types), and within-subject designs (cf. between-subject designs), thus a stronger downward adjustment of HWTP values is necessary to reflect consumers’ RWTP.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Schmidt & Bijmolt, Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science.

  5. Numeric anchoring is a robust phenomenon where a comparison against a presumed-to-be arbitrary number preceding a judgment can influence a myriad of real-world-relevant judgments.

    “One of the most robust phenomena studied across the social behavioral sciences is numeric anchoring, in which a comparison against a presumed-to-be arbitrary number preceding a judgment can influence a myriad of real-world-relevant judgments.”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Schley & Weingarten, Management Science.

  6. Anchoring effects exist in legal contexts, but their influence seems to depend on some situational factors.

    “Existing research indicates anchoring effects exist in legal contexts. The influence of anchors seems to depend on some situational factors, which paves the way for future research on countering the problematic effect in legal settings.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Bystranowski et al., Law and Human Behavior.

The measurement method changes the result.

Indirect ways of measuring your willingness to pay overestimate the actual amount you would spend compared to direct methods.

Value dictates the strength of the influence.

The bias in your willingness to pay is stronger when you are considering specialty goods or high-value products.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The price tag on the luxury watch.

You walk into a store. You see a high-end watch first. The price on that tag becomes the anchor for every other item you see next.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that anchoring influences judgments and willingness to pay, though the effect size varies based on product type and measurement methodology.

The study on perceptual anchoring in dyslexia is limited by a small number of included studies.
