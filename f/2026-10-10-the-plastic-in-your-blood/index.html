What is circulating in your blood right now?

File no. 0060October 10, 2026Health and environment

Microplastics are present in human blood.

Studies have detected microplastics in the blood of humans. 88% of these particles are fragments and 79% are white or clear.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Microplastics were found in the blood of humans, with 88% being fragments and 79% being white/clear.

    “The MPs were predominantly categorised as fragments (88 %) and were white/clear (79 %).”

    Study, 2024. Leonard et al., Environment International.

  2. Microplastic pollution in the placenta is linked to a 5.06 increased risk of intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR).

    “Microplastic pollution in the placenta significantly increases the risk of intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR) (95 % CI: 2. 07-8. 06; OR: 5. 06).”

    Meta-analysis, 2025. Ali‐Hassanzadeh et al., Reproductive Toxicology.

  3. Microplastics were detected in human placenta and fetal meconium, with sizes ranging from 2.1 to 100 micrometers.

    “The results of the included studies confirmed the existence of microplastics with varying sizes (2. 1 to 100 micrometers) in the placenta and the fetal body.”

    Systematic review, 2024. Sharma et al., Cureus.

  4. Placental microplastic levels are linked to reduced birthweights and 1-minute Apgar scores.

    “One reported placental microplastic levels correlated with reduced birthweights and 1-minute Apgar scores.”

    Systematic review, 2024. Hunt et al., BJOG An International Journal of Obstetrics & Gynaecology.

  5. Microplastics were found in 4 out of 6 human placentas, with 5 in the fetal side, 4 in the maternal side and 3 in the chorioamniotic membranes.

    “In total, 12 microplastic fragments (ranging from 5 to 10 μm in size), with spheric or irregular shape were found in 4 placentas (5 in the fetal side, 4 in the maternal side and 3 in the chorioamniotic membranes)”

    Review, 2021. Ragusa et al., Environment international.

  6. Consuming food in plastic containers is linked to a 5.39 increased chance of microplastic contamination in pregnant women.

    “the results showed that consuming food in plastic containers increased the chance of contamination with microplastics in pregnant women (95 % CI: 1. 32-21. 9; OR: 5. 39).”

    Meta-analysis, 2025. Ali‐Hassanzadeh et al., Reproductive Toxicology.

Particles enter the reproductive system.

Microplastics are found in the human placenta and fetal meconium. They appear in both the maternal and fetal sides of the placenta.

This may be how plastic enters the body.

Consuming food in plastic containers is linked to a higher chance of microplastic contamination in pregnant women.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A meal in a plastic container.

You sit at a table and eat a meal from a thin plastic tray. The particles move from the container into your body.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that microplastics are present in human blood and reproductive tissues, with some correlations to adverse pregnancy outcomes.

Many studies are small-scale or observational, and more large-scale investigations are needed to confirm the specific hazards and health impacts.
