Your brain is being hijacked

Does your screen provide connection or just a constant fear of being left out?

File no. 0047October 8, 2026Social media

Online interactions can reduce loneliness.

Specific types of online communication, such as one-to-one friendship and self-disclosure, can mitigate feelings of loneliness and stress.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. One-to-one communication, self-disclosure in the context of mutual online friendship, as well as positive and funny online experiences mitigated feelings of loneliness and stress.

    “In particular, one-to-one communication, self-disclosure in the context of mutual online friendship, as well as positive and funny online experiences mitigated feelings of loneliness and stress.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Marciano et al., Frontiers in public health.

  2. Parasocial engagement is reliably linked to both enhanced well-being and problematic engagement.

    “Parasocial engagement functions as a psychological double-edged sword, reliably linked to both enhanced well-being and problematic engagement.”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Li et al., JMIR mental health.

  3. Fear of missing out (FoMO) is most strongly related to both problematic smartphone use and social smartphone use compared to negative affect and fears of negative and positive evaluation.

    “Results demonstrated that FoMO was most strongly related to both problematic smartphone use and social smartphone use relative to negative affect and fears of negative and positive evaluation, and these relations held when controlling for age and gender.”

    Observational study, 2018. Wolniewicz et al., Psychiatry research.

  4. Fear of missing out (FoMO) may be part of social networking site (SNS) addiction.

    “fear of missing out (FOMO) may be part of SNS addiction;”

    Review, 2017. Kuss & Griffiths, International journal of environmental research and public health.

  5. Fear of missing out, social comparison, and exposure to negative contents were more likely to happen during social isolation and confinement due to the pandemic.

    “That would include making adolescents more aware of adverse mechanisms such as social comparison, fear of missing out, and exposure to negative contents, which were more likely to happen during social isolation and confinement due to the pandemic.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Marciano et al., Frontiers in public health.

The link between fear and use.

Fear of missing out is most strongly related to problematic smartphone use. It may be part of social networking site addiction. However, the evidence shows mixed results regarding well-being.

The nuance of your screen time.

That may be why your phone feels like a burden in some moments and a lifeline in others. The impact depends on the quality of the interaction.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A late night message.

You sit in a dark room. You send a private message to a friend. You feel less alone for a moment before looking at the next notification.

The honest summary

The evidence shows a strong association between FoMO and problematic smartphone use, but also highlights that specific positive online behaviors can have beneficial mental health outcomes.

Several key studies are observational, meaning they show associations rather than proving that FoMO causes problematic smartphone use.
