Is a classic psychiatry study a lie?

File no. 0097October 15, 2026History

Rosenhan's research may be scientific fraud.

Recent evidence identifies the study as a successful case of scientific fraud.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Based on an investigative journalist and shared records, Rosenhan's research is a spectacularly successful case of scientific fraud.

    “Yet based on the findings of an investigative journalist, Susannah Cahalan, and on records she shared with the author, we now know that this research is a spectacularly successful case of scientific fraud.”

    Study, 2023. Scull, History of psychiatry.

  2. Only 3 out of 74 emergency room psychiatrists offered a diagnosis of psychotic depression when presented with a specific case vignette.

    “In sharp contrast to what Slater reported, we found that only three psychiatrists offered a diagnosis of psychotic depression.”

    Study, 2005. Spitzer et al., The Journal of nervous and mental disease.

  3. Only one third of the 74 emergency room psychiatrists recommended medication for the case vignette.

    “Moreover, only one third recommended medication.”

    Study, 2005. Spitzer et al., The Journal of nervous and mental disease.

  4. The study found scant support for the claim that psychiatric diagnoses are mostly products of fashion or fad.

    “Our study raises questions regarding Slater's results and conclusions, and provides scant support for the claim that psychiatric diagnoses are mostly products of fashion or fad, as claimed by Slater.”

    Study, 2005. Spitzer et al., The Journal of nervous and mental disease.

  5. Rosenhan's article should be seen as an attempt to reform psychiatry in its entirety and not its diagnostic processes alone.

    “His article[2] should be seen as an attempt to reform psychiatry in its entirety and not its diagnostic processes alone.”

    Experiment, 2023. Agarwal, Indian Journal of Psychiatry.

The evidence on psychiatry is mixed.

One source labels the study as fraud. Another study found that psychiatric diagnoses are not mostly products of fashion or fad.

This changes how you see the system.

That may be why the goal of the original study was to reform the entire system of psychiatry rather than just the diagnostic process.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.
