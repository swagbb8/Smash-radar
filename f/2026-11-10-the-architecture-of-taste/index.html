Is your taste being curated or created?

File no. 0262November 10, 2026The future

Algorithms can be designed to expand your taste.

While filtering often limits diversity, systems can be built to increase variety and support your creative growth.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Recommendation systems can facilitate both algorithmic control and creative self-transformation.

    “Thus, recommendation systems can facilitate both algorithmic control and creative self-transformation, which calls for a theorization of this new cultural medium as a space of tension.”

    Study, 2017. Karakayalı et al., Theory Culture & Society.

  2. Users on the music recommendation website last.fm use recommender systems to examine and transform their own music taste.

    “User practices on the music recommendation website last.fm reveal that many users do not only utilize the website to receive guidance about music products but also to examine and transform an aspect of their self, i.e. their ‘music taste’.”

    Study, 2017. Karakayalı et al., Theory Culture & Society.

  3. A new algorithm can obtain simultaneous gains in both accuracy and diversity of recommendations without relying on any semantic or context-specific information.

    “By tuning the hybrid appropriately we are able to obtain, without relying on any semantic or context-specific information, simultaneous gains in both accuracy and diversity of recommendations.”

    Study, 2010. Zhou et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  4. A recommendation algorithm considering both the effects of similar and dissimilar users performs better than standard collaborative filtering for both accuracy and diversity.

    “Extensive analyses on three datasets, namely MovieLens, Netflix and Amazon, show that our method performs much better than the standard collaborative filtering algorithm for both accuracy and diversity.”

    Study, 2010. Zeng et al., International Journal of Modern Physics C.

  5. Algorithmic filtering and adaptation of online content to personal preferences is often associated with a decrease in the diversity of information to which users are exposed.

    “The algorithmic filtering and adaption of online content to personal preferences and interests is often associated with a decrease in the diversity of information to which users are exposed.”

    Study, 2016. Helberger et al., Information Communication & Society.

The evidence on algorithmic curation is mixed.

Current research shows that online content adaptation is often linked to a decrease in information diversity. However, specific designs can achieve simultaneous gains in accuracy and variety.

The design choice dictates your boundaries.

That may be why your digital experience feels narrow. It is not just a byproduct of your habits, but a result of how the system is built to function.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

You scroll through a music app.

You listen to a new artist. You wonder if you would have found them alone or if the system led you there first.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while algorithms can narrow information exposure, they are also capable of being designed to increase diversity and support user self-cultivation.

The sources do not provide a definitive causal link between algorithmic curation and the narrowing of human aesthetic preferences, as some studies focus on design possibilities rather than outcomes.
