Where do you go when your phone dies?

File no. 0102October 16, 2026Technology

Your brain is losing its internal map.

Heavy GPS use is linked to a steeper decline in hippocampal-dependent spatial memory and reduced brain connectivity.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Using GPS navigation in AR glasses for three months does not change navigational abilities.

    “Importantly, no effect of intervention on navigational abilities was observed.”

    Experiment, 2018. Fajnerová et al., BioMed Research International.

  2. Greater GPS use over a three-year period was linked to a steeper decline in hippocampal-dependent spatial memory.

    “we observed an important effect of GPS use over time, whereby greater GPS use since initial testing was associated with a steeper decline in hippocampal-dependent spatial memory.”

    Observational study, 2020. Dahmani & Bohbot, Scientific Reports.

  3. Using GPS navigation in AR glasses for three months reduces the functional coupling between the hippocampus and associated brain regions.

    “the experimental group showed FC decrease after three months of using GPS navigation implemented in AR glasses in contrast to FC increase in the control group without such intervention.”

    Experiment, 2018. Fajnerová et al., BioMed Research International.

  4. People with more lifetime GPS experience are linked to worse spatial memory when they have to navigate without GPS.

    “We first present cross-sectional results that show that people with greater lifetime GPS experience have worse spatial memory during self-guided navigation, i. e. when they are required to navigate without GPS.”

    Observational study, 2020. Dahmani & Bohbot, Scientific Reports.

  5. The hippocampus is not dedicated solely to spatial cognition and navigation, but organizes experiences in memory, where spatial mapping is a metaphor for relational memory organization.

    “These observations suggest that the hippocampal system is not dedicated to spatial cognition and navigation, but organizes experiences in memory, for which spatial mapping and navigation are both a metaphor for and a prominent application of relational memory organization.”

    Review, 2017. Eichenbaum, Journal of neurophysiology.

The evidence is mixed.

Studies show a correlation between high GPS usage and poorer spatial memory. However, experiments show that using GPS navigation in AR glasses does not change actual navigational abilities.

The map is no longer in your head.

That may be why you feel lost without your phone. The hippocampus organizes your experiences as a relational memory. By outsourcing your route, you may be thinning the structure of your own mental landscape.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The familiar turn.

You drive to a friend's house. You see the turn ahead, but you do not recognize the street name or the landmarks. You wait for the blue line to tell you when to move.

The honest summary

The evidence shows a correlation between high GPS usage and poorer spatial memory and reduced hippocampal connectivity, but it does not prove that GPS use causes a loss of navigation skills.

The longitudinal study showing memory decline had a small sample size, and the experiment showing no change in navigational abilities may have limitations regarding the mechanism of change.
