Why does your brain prioritize others?

File no. 0111October 17, 2026Human relationships

Your brain has a specific circuit for learning only when acting for others.

Activity in the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex and basal forebrain drives learning specifically in prosocial contexts.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Evolution may have selected mechanisms for emotional contagion to prepare animals for dangers by using others as sentinels.

    “Together, this suggests evolution selected mechanisms for emotional contagion to prepare animals for dangers by using others as sentinels.”

    Review, 2022. Keysers et al., Trends in Cognitive Sciences.

  2. The age effect in prosociality is only significant when older adults have a higher socioeconomic status than younger adults.

    “Additionally, the age effect was only significant when older adults had higher socioeconomic status than younger adults.”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Li et al., The Gerontologist.

  3. Activity in a posterior portion of the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex/basal forebrain (sgACC) drives learning only when acting in a prosocial context.

    “fMRI revealed that activity in a posterior portion of the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex/basal forebrain (sgACC) drives learning only when we are acting in a prosocial context”

    Study, 2016. Lockwood et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  4. Older adults are more likely to engage in sharing than younger adults, but there is no significant age difference in helping, comforting, or mixed prosociality.

    “We discovered a moderate age effect in sharing (Hedges' g = 0. 53), but a nonsignificant age effect in helping (Hedges' g = 0. 11), comforting (Hedges' g = -0. 20), or mixed prosociality (Hedges' g = 0. 15).”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Li et al., The Gerontologist.

  5. Extreme acts of altruism and aggression are linked to individual differences in sensitivity to cues of vulnerability and the tendency to generalize care outward.

    “variations in caring motivations and behavior reflect individual differences in sensitivity to cues that signal vulnerability and distress and in the tendency to generalize care outward from socially close to distant others.”

    Review, 2018. Marsh, Annual Review of Psychology.

  6. Clusters of significant spatial convergence across studies in the insulae, inferior frontal gyrus, middle cingulum, and the precentral gyrus are associated with prosocial behavior in children and adolescents.

    “We found clusters of significant spatial convergence across studies in the insulae, inferior frontal gyrus, middle cingulum, and the precentral gyrus.”

    Meta-analysis, 2025. Bonandrini et al., Social Neuroscience.

Prosocial behavior is shaped by your status and age.

Older adults show higher prosociality only when they have higher socioeconomic status than younger adults. Helping and comforting behaviors do not show a significant age difference.

Your empathy is not a constant state.

That may be why your urge to help fluctuates based on your social standing or your sensitivity to others' vulnerability.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

You see a stranger struggle with a heavy bag.

You feel a brief tug of awareness. You decide whether to step in or keep walking based on how much their distress registers in your own mind.

The honest summary

The evidence identifies specific neural circuits and evolutionary mechanisms for prosociality but shows that these behaviors are not uniform and are influenced by external factors like socioeconomic status.

The sources provide limited information on whether these behaviors actually override biological self-preservation instincts in life-threatening situations.
