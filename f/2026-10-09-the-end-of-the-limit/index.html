Is there a limit to how long you can live?

File no. 0055October 9, 2026The future

The current human age limit will likely break.

There is a greater than 99% probability that the current maximum reported age at death of 122 will be broken by 2100. The evidence is mixed.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Hormesis defines the quantitative rules within which adaptive processes operate and determines the extent to which biological performance, such as lifespan, can be enhanced or extended.

    “Hormesis defines the quantitative rules within which adaptive processes operate and is central to evolution and biology... the extent to which biological performance (e.g. , memory, resistance to injury/disease, wound healing, hair growth or lifespan) can be enhanced/extended”

    Review, 2023. Calabrese et al., Ageing Research Reviews.

  2. Late life intervention was sufficient to yield a potent health benefit from the natural product fisetin.

    “Late life intervention was sufficient to yield a potent health benefit.”

    Study, 2018. Yousefzadeh et al., EBioMedicine.

  3. There is a greater than 99% probability that the current maximum reported age at death of 122 will be broken by 2100.

    “Based on the Bayesian analysis, there is a greater than 99% probability that the current MRAD of 122 will be broken by 2100.”

    Study, 2021. Pearce & Raftery, Demographic Research.

  4. The natural product fisetin has senotherapeutic activity in mice and in human tissues.

    “The natural product fisetin has senotherapeutic activity in mice and in human tissues.”

    Study, 2018. Yousefzadeh et al., EBioMedicine.

Biology follows quantitative rules.

Hormesis defines the rules for how biological performance and lifespan can be enhanced. Natural products like fisetin show senotherapeutic activity in mice and human tissues.

Your lifespan is a variable.

That may be why your aging is being viewed as a biological process that can be adjusted through specific interventions rather than a fixed end point.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence suggests that human lifespan is not currently capped at 122 and may be extendable through biological and pharmacological interventions.

The probability of reaching extreme ages like 130 remains relatively low at 13% according to current Bayesian projections.
