Why do the least skilled feel the most capable?

File no. 0175October 25, 2026Psychology

The unskilled are often unaware of their own incompetence.

Poor performers frequently overestimate their ability because they lack the metacognitive skill to recognize their own errors. This finding is mixed across different settings, from laboratory tests to medical exams.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. In a study of humor, grammar, and logic, participants in the bottom quartile estimated themselves to be in the 62nd percentile despite scoring in the 12th percentile.

    “Although their test scores put them in the 12th percentile, they estimated themselves to be in the 62nd.”

    Study, 1999. Kruger & Dunning, Journal of personality and social psychology.

  2. The unskilled and unaware effect was reduced when the task was perceived to have low self-relevance.

    “As predicted, the unskilled and unaware effect was attenuated when the task was perceived to have low self-relevance.”

    Experiment, 2015. Kim et al., PLoS ONE.

  3. In a real-world anatomy exam, students in all quartiles were unable to self-assess their performance, except for a group of mid-range students in the 2nd quartile.

    “Students in all quartiles were unable to self-assess their exam performance, except for a group of mid-range students in the 2nd quartile.”

    Study, 2013. Sawdon & Finn, Journal of Anatomy.

  4. A meta-analysis showed that poor performers' overly optimistic estimates are linked to a lack of insight into their own errors rather than mistaken assessments of their peers.

    “An additional meta-analysis showed that it was lack of insight into their own errors (and not mistaken assessments of their peers) that led to overly optimistic estimates among poor performers.”

    Meta-analysis, 2008. Ehrlinger et al., Organizational behavior and human decision processes.

  5. Improving the skills of participants in a study helped them recognize the limitations of their abilities.

    “Paradoxically, improving the skills of participants, and thus increasing their metacognitive competence, helped them recognize the limitations of their abilities.”

    Study, 1999. Kruger & Dunning, Journal of personality and social psychology.

Metacognition is the missing link.

Research suggests that overconfidence is linked to a lack of insight into personal mistakes rather than just judging others incorrectly. This effect is also influenced by how much you care about the task.

Competence creates self-awareness.

That may be why improving a skill actually helps you see your own limitations. The more you know, the more you realize what you do not know.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The confident novice.

You sit at your desk, starting a new project. You feel certain you can finish it quickly and perfectly, even though you have never done it before.

The honest summary

The evidence suggests that the unskilled are unaware of their incompetence due to a lack of metacognitive ability, though the effect can be influenced by self-relevance.

The studies vary in setting, ranging from laboratory tests of humor and grammar to real-world high-stakes medical exams.
