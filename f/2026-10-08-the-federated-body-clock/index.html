Who is setting your internal pace?

File no. 0045October 8, 2026Science and reality

Your cells have their own clocks.

Circadian clocks exist in nearly every cell of your body, not just in your brain.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. A surprisingly small number of tissue rhythms are lost when only local clocks are ablated.

    “First, a surprisingly small number of tissue rhythms are lost when only local clocks are ablated”

    Review, 2020. Harder & Oster, BioEssays.

  2. Changing feeding times can change the phase of circadian gene expression in peripheral cell types by up to 12 h while leaving the SCN unaffected.

    “Here we show that temporal feeding restriction under light-dark or dark-dark conditions can change the phase of circadian gene expression in peripheral cell types by up to 12 h while leaving the phase of cyclic gene expression in the SCN unaffected.”

    Study, 2000. Damiola et al., Genes & development.

  3. Peripheral clocks act as gatekeepers that temporally adjust cellular responses to external stimuli.

    “peripheral clocks controlling some local rhythms while at the same time acting as gatekeepers that temporally adjust cellular responses to external stimuli.”

    Review, 2020. Harder & Oster, BioEssays.

  4. Circadian clocks exist in nearly every body cell, not just in the brain's suprachiasmatic nucleus.

    “In mammals, the circadian timekeeping system has a complex architecture, composed of a central pacemaker in the brain's suprachiasmatic nuclei (SCN) and subsidiary clocks in nearly every body cell.”

    Review, 2010. Dibner et al., Annual review of physiology.

  5. Food-induced phase resetting proceeds faster in liver than in kidney, heart, or pancreas.

    “Food-induced phase resetting proceeds faster in liver than in kidney, heart, or pancreas, but after 1 wk of daytime feeding, the phases of circadian gene expression are similar in all examined peripheral tissues.”

    Study, 2000. Damiola et al., Genes & development.

  6. Dysregulation of clock genes is linked to alterations in proliferation, metabolism, epithelial plasticity, immune regulation, metastatic potential, and treatment responsiveness in colorectal cancer.

    “statistically significant associations were consistently reported between dysregulation of clock genes such as PER1 , PER3 , CLOCK , BMAL1 , CRY1 , TIMELESS , and ARNTL2 and alterations in proliferation, metabolism, epithelial plasticity, immune regulation, metastatic potential, and treatment responsiveness.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Tarasewicz et al., International journal of molecular sciences.

Local clocks operate independently.

These clocks can be adjusted by metabolism and local signals. They act as gatekeepers that temporally adjust your cellular responses to external stimuli.

The brain is not the only master.

Your body functions as a federated system. Local factors interact with systemic signals to regulate your rhythms.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A meal at midnight.

Eating late at night shifts the phase of gene expression in your liver while your brain's master clock remains unaffected.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that circadian clocks are distributed throughout the body's tissues and can function independently of the central brain pacemaker.

The traditional hierarchical model is being replaced by a more integrated 'federated' model where local and systemic factors interact to regulate rhythms.
