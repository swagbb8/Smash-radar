The uncomfortable truth

Can your thoughts create a physical illness?

File no. 0182October 27, 2026Psychology

Negative expectations can trigger real physical symptoms.

The nocebo effect occurs when a treatment context generates negative expectations that cause symptoms independent of the treatment itself.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Depressive children and adolescents allocated to placebo or active groups had a similar risk to develop adverse events.

    “There was similar risk for the incidence of adverse events between non-active and active group (global RR 1. 04, 95% CI: 0. 97-1. 11).”

    Meta-analysis, 2014. Rojas-Mirquez et al., Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience.

  2. The overall magnitude of the nocebo effect in pain was moderate to large, with a range of g=-0.43 to 4.05.

    “The overall magnitude of the nocebo effect was moderate to large (lowest g=0. 62 [0. 24-1. 01] and highest g=1. 03 [0. 63-1. 43]) and highly variable (range of g=-0. 43 to 4. 05).”

    Meta-analysis, 2014. Petersen et al., Pain.

  3. Nocebo manipulations make negative expectancy increase with a large effect of g = . 837.

    “Nocebo manipulations reliably increased negative expectancy with a large effect (g = . 837) and state anxiety with a small effect (g = . 312).”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Rooney et al., Health Psychology Review.

  4. Social learning can contribute to the development of negative health expectations, leading to worsened health outcomes.

    “While often adaptive, social learning can contribute to the development of negative health expectations, leading to worsened health outcomes, a phenomenon known as the nocebo effect.”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Saunders et al., Health Psychology Review.

  5. Changes in negative expectancy and state anxiety are linked to larger nocebo effects.

    “Changes in expectancy and state anxiety due to the nocebo manipulation were associated with larger nocebo effects (r = . 376 and . 234, respectively).”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Rooney et al., Health Psychology Review.

  6. Nocebo effects occur when the treatment context generates negative expectancies that trigger the experience or worsening of negative symptoms beyond any effects attributable to the treatment itself.

    “Nocebo effects occur when the treatment context generates negative expectancies that trigger the experience or worsening of negative symptoms beyond any effects attributable to the treatment itself.”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Rooney et al., Health Psychology.

Your mind can create physical illness.

Social learning and verbal suggestions can develop negative health expectations. These expectations are linked to larger nocebo effects and state anxiety.

The information you consume shapes your body.

That may be why you feel physical pain or discomfort after hearing about a potential side effect, even if the treatment is inactive.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The wait in a doctor's office.

You sit in a chair and read a brochure about side effects. You feel a dull ache in your chest before you even take the pill.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that nocebo effects are significant and can be reliably induced through verbal suggestions, conditioning, and social learning.

The magnitude of nocebo effects is highly variable and can sometimes be negative, as seen in the range of g=-0.43 to 4.05.
