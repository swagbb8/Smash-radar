Does owning a home actually make you healthier?

File no. 0301November 18, 2026Money and power

Homeownership does not guarantee better health for everyone.

The health benefits of owning a home depend on your race, ethnicity, and the value of the property. Evidence is mixed or contested.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. There is little evidence that owning a home has beneficial effects on children's cognitive achievement, behavior problems, and health.

    “We find little evidence of beneficial homeownership effects and suggest that previous analyses may have mistaken selection differences for the effect of homeownership itself.”

    Observational study, 2012. Holupka & Newman, Real Estate Economics.

  2. Homeownership is linked to health outcomes for non-Latino Whites, but this association is not statistically significant for racial/ethnic minorities.

    “Although homeownership was a robust, independent predictor for each health outcome in the non-Latino White population, the association disappeared in statistical significance for racial/ethnic minorities.”

    Observational study, 2013. Ortiz & Zimmerman, American Journal of Public Health.

  3. The health-promoting effects of homeownership may be contingent upon house values.

    “Findings are consistent with the thesis that health-promoting effects of homeownership may be contingent upon house values.”

    Observational study, 2017. Mehdipanah et al., International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

  4. Historic redlining is linked to modern-day health outcomes across the lifespan.

    “This review demonstrates the persistent effect of historic redlining on individuals' health.”

    Systematic review, 2023. Kraus et al., Public Health Nursing.

  5. Interventions that promote housing affordability and stability were associated with improved adult health outcomes, but the evidence is mixed and mostly low-certainty.

    “This systematic review found mixed and mostly low-certainty evidence that interventions that promote housing affordability and stability were associated with improved adult health outcomes.”

    Systematic review, 2022. Chen et al., JAMA Network Open.

The link is inconsistent.

Research shows homeownership is linked to health for non-Latino Whites, but not for many other groups. Benefits may also depend on house values. Most studies are observational and cannot prove causation.

Systemic history shapes your body.

Historic redlining is linked to modern-day health outcomes across a person's lifespan. That may be why your neighborhood's history affects your physical well-being today.

The honest summary

The evidence does not support a universal link between homeownership and improved health, as the effects are inconsistent across different demographics and socioeconomic conditions.

Most studies are observational and cannot establish whether homeownership causes better health or if healthier people are simply more likely to own homes.
