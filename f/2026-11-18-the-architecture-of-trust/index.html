Why do you trust the crowd more than the person?

File no. 0300November 18, 2026Social media

High follower counts increase perceived trustworthiness.

A larger number of followers is linked to higher source trustworthiness and purchase intention.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Results demonstrated some evidence consumers’ intentions to engage with athlete-related social media content were greater when this content was posted by an indirect source, rather than directly by the athlete.

    “Results demonstrated some evidence consumers’ intentions to engage with athlete-related social media content were greater when this content was posted by an indirect source, rather than directly by the athlete.”

    Experiment, 2019. Na et al., European Sport Management Quarterly.

  2. Content was perceived as more credible and less biased than when the same information was sent from the athlete directly.

    “This content was perceived as more credible and less biased than when the same information was sent from the athlete directly.”

    Experiment, 2019. Na et al., European Sport Management Quarterly.

  3. The type of source does not affect message credibility or CR but a corporate source generates more WOM.

    “Results showed that the type of source does not affect message credibility or CR but a corporate source generates more WOM.”

    Experiment, 2022. Dalla-Pria & Rodríguez‐de‐Dios, Corporate Communications An International Journal.

  4. Followers' perceived attractiveness of influencers, similarity to influencers, procedural fairness, and interpersonal fairness of their interactions with influencers are positively related to the strength of their parasocial relationship with influencers, which further mediates the effect of the aforementioned factors on followers’ interests in influencer-promoted products.

    “The results of an online survey showed that followers’ perceived attractiveness of influencers, similarity to influencers, procedural fairness, and interpersonal fairness of their interactions with influencers are positively related to the strength of their parasocial relationship with influencers, which further mediates the effect of the aforementioned factors on followers’ interests in influencer-promoted products.”

    Observational study, 2020. Yuan & Lou, Journal of Interactive Advertising.

  5. The number of followers positively influences source attractiveness, source trustworthiness as well as purchase intention.

    “Furthermore, the results reveal that the number of followers positively influences source attractiveness, source trustworthiness as well as purchase intention.”

    Study, 2020. Weismueller et al., Australasian Marketing Journal (AMJ).

The source of the message matters.

Content from indirect sources can be perceived as more credible and less biased than content from the athlete directly.

Your trust is shaped by scale.

That may be why you trust a large public figure more than a peer. Similarity and fairness build a connection, but scale builds the authority you accept.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

Scrolling a feed at night.

You see a product review from a massive account. You believe the claim because of the numbers behind it, not the person behind the camera.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while interpersonal factors like similarity and fairness build parasocial relationships that drive interest, larger follower counts and third-party sources also play significant roles in credibility and purchase intent.

The study on parasocial relationships is an observational study showing associations rather than direct causation.
