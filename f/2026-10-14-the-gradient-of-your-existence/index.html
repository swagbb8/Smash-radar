Is your head moving faster through time than your feet?

File no. 0085October 14, 2026Science and reality

Time passes faster at your head than at your feet.

Clocks at a higher gravitational potential tick faster than identical clocks at a lower potential. This difference is detectable over height changes of less than 1 meter.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. The emission rate of an atom in a coherent superposition of separated wave packets in a gravitational field is different from the emission rate of an atom in a classical mixture of these packets.

    “In particular, we show that the emission rate of an atom prepared in a coherent superposition of separated wave packets in a gravitational field is different from the emission rate of an atom in a classical mixture of these packets, which gives rise to a quantum gravitational time dilation effect.”

    Study, 2024. Paczos et al., Quantum.

  2. The time measured by a vertical light clock is equivalent to the proper time defined at its center.

    “In particular, we show that the time measured by the vertical light clock is equivalent to the proper time defined at its center.”

    Study, 2023. Bravo et al., AVS Quantum Science.

  3. Researchers measured a fractional frequency gradient of [ − 12.4 ± 0. 7 (stat) ± 2. 5 (sys) ] × 10 −19 /cm across a height difference of 1 cm.

    “We measure a fractional frequency gradient of [ − 12.4 ± 0. 7 (stat) ± 2. 5 (sys) ] × 10 −19 /cm, consistent with the expected redshift gradient of − 10.9 × 10 −19 /cm.”

    Study, 2023. Zheng et al., Nature Communications.

  4. Time dilation was observed from relative speeds of less than 10 meters per second by comparing two optical atomic clocks connected by a 75-meter length of optical fiber.

    “We observed time dilation from relative speeds of less than 10 meters per second by comparing two optical atomic clocks connected by a 75-meter length of optical fiber.”

    Study, 2010. Chou et al., Science (New York, N.Y.).

  5. Time dilation due to a change in height near Earth's surface of less than 1 meter can be detected.

    “We can now also detect time dilation due to a change in height near Earth's surface of less than 1 meter.”

    Study, 2010. Chou et al., Science (New York, N.Y.). This shows a link, not a cause.

  6. A clock at a higher gravitational potential will tick faster than an otherwise identical clock at a lower potential.

    “Einstein’s theory of general relativity predicts that a clock at a higher gravitational potential will tick faster than an otherwise identical clock at a lower potential, an effect known as the gravitational redshift.”

    Study, 2023. Zheng et al., Nature Communications.

Gravity dictates the speed of your seconds.

Time dilation is linked to height near Earth's surface. Researchers measured a fractional frequency gradient across a height difference of 1 cm.

Your body exists in different moments.

That may be why your physical presence is not a single point in time. You are stretched across a gradient of seconds.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

Standing in a tall hallway.

You stand still in a room with high ceilings. Your head is closer to the sky than your feet are to the floor. Time moves at different speeds for different parts of your body. You are physically separated from yourself by a fraction of a second.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that clocks at higher gravitational potentials tick faster, with measurements capable of detecting these differences at the centimeter and millimeter scales.

While the effect is measurable, the precision of these clocks is now reaching a regime where the physical size of the clock itself must be accounted for in the description.
