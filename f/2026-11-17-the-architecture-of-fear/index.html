Why do you fear certain crimes more than others?

File no. 0295November 17, 2026Modern society

The availability heuristic shapes your perception of crime.

Your brain uses ease of recall to estimate risk. This influences how you weigh the costs and benefits of target characteristics.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Television effects for several different program types are located primarily among individuals who perceive they live in a neighborhood with high percentages of blacks.

    “television effects for several different program types located primarily among individuals who perceive they live in a neighborhood with high percentages of blacks.”

    Observational study, 2003. Eschholz et al., Social Problems.

  2. The most significant effects of media reporting are broadly ideological rather than narrowly attitudinal.

    “it is likely that the most significant effects of media reporting are broadly ideological rather than narrowly attitudinal.”

    Study, 1995. Sacco, The Annals of the American Academy of Political and Social Science.

  3. Canadian students reported significantly higher levels of fear, particularly of violent crime, than U.S. students.

    “The results show distinct differences between Canadian and U.S. students, with Canadian students reporting significantly higher levels of fear, particularly of violent crime.”

    Observational study, 2011. Kohm et al., Canadian Journal of Criminology and Criminal Justice/La Revue canadienne de criminologie et de justice pénale.

  4. Media messages of risk and fear seem to influence viewers similarly regardless of gender or race.

    “media messages of risk, and fear seem to influence viewers similarly regardless of gender or race, providing additional support for cultivation theory and mainstreaming effects.”

    Study, 2015. Callanan & Rosenberger, Criminal Justice Review.

  5. Media do not totally shape the fear of crime experiences, as a panoply of meanings has emerged, which makes their influence differential.

    “Indeed, media do not totally shape the fear of crime experiences, as a panoply of meanings has emerged, which makes their influence differential.”

    Study, 2022. Silva & Guedes, Criminal Justice Review.

  6. The availability heuristic can help explain how target characteristics affect situational perceptions of crime benefits and costs.

    “the availability heuristic can help explain how target characteristics affect situational perceptions of crime benefits and costs”

    Experiment, 2018. Pickett, Crime & Delinquency.

Media influence is not uniform.

Your fear of crime is mediated by your geography and your own ideological framing. Some effects are broadly ideological rather than narrowly attitudinal.

Your neighborhood shapes your reality.

That may be why you feel safer or more at risk than others. Your surroundings and the messages you consume create a specific, individual perception of danger.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A walk home at dusk.

You see a shadowed doorway and your heart rate increases. You recall a news story from last week about a robbery on a street like this one.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that media influence on fear is not uniform, as it is mediated by individual perceptions of neighborhood safety, geography, and ideological framing.

Several studies show inconsistent results regarding the direct impact of media on fear, with some findings showing no significant difference between demographics.

Is your fear based on what happened, or what you remember?
