Is your conscience a physical sensation?

File no. 0272November 12, 2026Psychology

Disgust increases the severity of your moral judgments.

While bodily sensations like disgust influence the intensity of your moral decisions, they are not the sole or primary drivers of your judgment. The evidence on this is mixed.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Dampening visceral arousal did not directly impact moral judgment.

    “Dampening visceral arousal (via propranolol) did not directly impact moral judgment, but instead changed the relative contribution of affective appraisals to moral judgment-and only in unusual purity scenarios.”

    Experiment, 2022. Gray et al., Journal of Personality and Social Psychology.

  2. The study did not find evidence that somatic marker production explains the connection between utilitarianism and psychopathy in a college population.

    “The present study did not find evidence that somatic marker production explains the connection between utilitarianism and psychopathy in a college population.”

    Study, 2020. Fagan et al., Brain Sciences.

  3. The role of disgust in severity of moral judgments depends on participants' sensitivity to their own bodily sensations.

    “Experiments 2-4 showed that the role of disgust in severity of moral judgments depends on participants' sensitivity to their own bodily sensations.”

    Experiment, 2008. Schnall et al., Personality & social psychology bulletin.

  4. High psychopathic traits and low skin conductance level reactivity were associated with increased utilitarian decisions in dilemmas involving direct personal harm.

    “High psychopathic traits and low SCL-R were associated with increased utilitarian decisions in dilemmas involving direct personal harm.”

    Study, 2020. Fagan et al., Brain Sciences.

  5. Disgust can increase the severity of moral judgments relative to controls.

    “In each case, the results showed that disgust can increase the severity of moral judgments relative to controls.”

    Experiment, 2008. Schnall et al., Personality & social psychology bulletin.

  6. Disgust seems to show a mediating effect on guilt decisions, whereas fear-related emotions, and general positive and negative affect do not.

    “A synthesis of the literature suggests that specific emotions play a different role: disgust seems to show a mediating effect, whereas fear-related emotions, and general positive and negative affect do not.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Doorn et al., Psychology Crime and Law.

Your body signals may not be the full story.

Dampening visceral arousal does not directly impact your moral judgment. Furthermore, somatic markers may not explain the link between psychopathy and utilitarian decision-making.

You might trust your gut less than you think.

That may be why your moral compass feels like a mix of physical instinct and complex reasoning. Your body reacts, but it does not always lead.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The feeling in your stomach.

You watch a news report about a crime. You feel a slight wave of nausea or a tightening in your chest as you decide if the person is wrong.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while bodily sensations like disgust influence the intensity of moral judgments, they are not the sole or primary driver of moral decision-making.

The research is limited by conflicting results regarding whether somatic markers are the underlying mechanism for psychopathic moral behavior and the specific conditions under which visceral arousal affects judgment.
