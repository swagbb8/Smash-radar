Why do you feel lonely in a crowd?

File no. 0265November 11, 2026Psychology

Competition drives loneliness more than a lack of contact.

Exposure to neoliberal ideology increases feelings of competition, which correlates with higher loneliness and lower well-being. This evidence is mixed and may only apply to liberals.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Receiving more Instagram Likes than desired reduces loneliness but increases negative affect.

    “Findings indicate that receiving greater Likes than desired reduces loneliness but increases negative affect, and this result is exacerbated by the visibility of Likes.”

    Experiment, 2020. Wallace & Buil, Personality and Individual Differences.

  2. Exposure to neoliberal ideology causes an increase in loneliness and a decrease in well-being by increasing perceptions of being in competition with others.

    “exposure to neoliberal ideology increased loneliness and decreased well-being by reducing people's sense of connection to others and by increasing perceptions of being in competition with others.”

    Experiment, 2021. Becker et al., British Journal of Social Psychology.

  3. People who believe they are worse off than similar others are less inclined to help others.

    “people higher in PRD were less inclined to help others.”

    Study, 2016. Callan et al., Social Psychological and Personality Science.

  4. In 20s and 30s youth, lower relative deprivation is linked to lower social isolation.

    “가족에 대한 신뢰가 높고 상대적 박탈감이 낮을수록 청년들의 사회적 고립감은 통계적으로 유의미하게 낮아졌다.”

    Study, 2023. Kim, Korean Public Administration Quarterly.

  5. During the COVID-19 lockdown, social isolation and feelings of loneliness in the general population have increased.

    “During the COVID-19 lockdown, social isolation and feelings of loneliness (SIFL) in the older population have increased”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Lazzari & Rabottini, International Journal of Psychiatry in Clinical Practice.

The mechanics of relative deprivation.

When you believe you are worse off than others, you are less likely to help. In youth, lower relative deprivation is linked to lower social isolation.

The cost of comparison.

That may be why you feel lonely even when you are around people. The system encourages you to see others as rivals rather than peers.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The scroll through your feed.

You sit on your couch at night and watch the lives of people you barely know. You see their highlights and measure your own progress against them.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that perceived competition and relative deprivation are significant drivers of loneliness, but it does not rule out the impact of objective social isolation.

The study on neoliberalism found that the effect on well-being was evident for liberals only.
