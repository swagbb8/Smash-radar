Your brain is being hijacked

Does your software make you more capable?

File no. 0271November 12, 2026Modern society

Automation can increase your sense of self-efficacy.

While systems are often used for control, specific designs like support-after-processing can improve worker satisfaction and confidence.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Workers in the support-after-processing group showed a steeper increase in self-efficacy in personnel selection compared to other groups.

    “Furthermore, participants in this group showed a steeper increase in self-efficacy in personnel selection compared to the other groups.”

    Experiment, 2020. Langer et al., Journal of Business and Psychology.

  2. Algorithmic management is shaped by, as much as it shapes, the autonomy and value to workers.

    “Second, we argue that algorithmic management is shaped by, as much as it shaping, the autonomy and value to workers.”

    Study, 2021. Meijerink & Bondarouk, Human Resource Management Review.

  3. Companies have mostly used algorithmic systems in a controlling manner, rather than using them to enable workers.

    “Based on the reviewed literature, we claim that so far, companies have mostly utilized algorithmic systems in a controlling manner, neglecting the technology’s enabling potential in organizing.”

    Systematic review, 2023. Noponen et al., Management Review Quarterly.

  4. Satisfaction with the decision was higher for the support-after-processing group.

    “Results showed that satisfaction with the decision was higher for the support-after-processing group.”

    Experiment, 2020. Langer et al., Journal of Business and Psychology.

  5. Algorithmic management in the platform economy is linked to growing technostress, data opacity, and burnout.

    “The review uncovers growing technostress, data opacity, and burnout, along with a rising resistance from workers through forms of algo-activism.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Kashyap & Pandey, Labour & Industry a journal of the social and economic relations of work.

The duality of algorithmic management.

Companies mostly use these systems to control workers, leading to burnout and data opacity. However, the technology also shapes the autonomy and value workers find in their roles.

Your autonomy is being reshaped.

That may be why you feel both more capable and more exhausted at work. The way a system is built determines whether it enables you or manages you.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows a complex duality where algorithmic management often functions as a tool of control, yet its specific design can either diminish or enhance worker autonomy and self-efficacy.

Most research on algorithmic fairness and perceptions comes almost exclusively from Western-democratic contexts.
