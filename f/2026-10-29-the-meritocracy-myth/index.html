Does your belief in hard work hide a deeper inequality?

File no. 0194October 29, 2026Money and power

Belief in meritocracy protects existing social hierarchies.

Those in the most privileged positions are most likely to believe in meritocracy. This belief reduces support for policies that redistribute wealth or provide affirmative action.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Belief in school meritocracy reduces the perceived unfairness of social class inequality in society.

    “the belief that schools are meritocratic carries consequences beyond the school context as it is associated with attitudes that maintain social class and economic inequality.”

    Experiment, 2022. Batruch et al., Social Psychological and Personality Science.

  2. Belief in school meritocracy reduces support for affirmative action policies at university.

    “belief in school meritocracy reduces the perceived unfairness of social class inequality in society, support for affirmative action policies at university and support for policies aimed at reducing income inequality.”

    Experiment, 2022. Batruch et al., Social Psychological and Personality Science.

  3. Those with the strongest belief in meritocracy in the creative sector are those in the most privileged positions.

    “Second, those with the strongest belief in meritocracy in the sector are those in the most privileged positions, specifically those are best rewarded by the sector.”

    Observational study, 2017. Taylor & O’Brien, Sociological Research Online.

  4. In Indonesia, information describing (high) income inequality and (low) social mobility rocked our participants’ belief in meritocracy.

    “In Indonesia, information describing (high) income inequality and (low) social mobility rocked our participants’ belief in meritocracy.”

    Experiment, 2020. Mijs & Hoy, Social Problems.

  5. Subjective upward mobility is linked to stronger meritocratic beliefs.

    “subjective upward mobility is associated with stronger meritocratic beliefs, and downward mobility is associated with stronger structuralist beliefs—but has no bearing on people’s meritocracy beliefs.”

    Study, 2022. Mijs et al., Social Psychology Quarterly.

Meritocracy masks social inequality.

When you believe success is solely based on merit, the unfairness of class inequality becomes less visible. This belief is linked to a lower desire for social mobility policies.

The ideology of effort can be a barrier.

That may be why social cooperation stalls. If you believe everyone is in the right place because they worked hard enough, you may find it harder to support those who are left behind.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The office coffee machine.

You stand in line with people from different backgrounds. You assume everyone here worked just as hard as you to get this job. You do not see the different starting lines they faced.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that meritocratic beliefs are associated with the legitimization of existing social hierarchies and a reduction in support for policies aimed at addressing inequality.

Several key findings are based on observational studies that show associations rather than direct causation.
