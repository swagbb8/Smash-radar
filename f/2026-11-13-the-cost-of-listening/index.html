Does being a good listener drain you?

File no. 0275November 13, 2026Human relationships

Active-empathic listening involves significant emotional involvement.

In certain professions, this emotional labor is linked to emotional exhaustion and burnout. This finding is specific to early childhood teachers and may not apply to everyone.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Emotional labor is associated with emotional exhaustion and burnout in early childhood teachers.

    “Research suggests that emotional labor is associated with emotional exhaustion and burnout in early childhood teachers, but there is limited research available on this issue.”

    Review, 2023. Purper et al., Early childhood education journal.

  2. Active-empathic listening is a three-part process consisting of sensing, processing, and responding.

    “The scale is based on cognitive listening theory in terms of the three-part process of sensing, processing, and responding.”

    Study, 2019. Stamp, Routledge eBooks.

  3. Active-empathic listening is the active and emotional involvement of a listener that can take place in at least three key stages of the listening process.

    “Active-empathic listening (AEL) is the active and emotional involvement of a listener that can take place in at least three key stages of the listening process.”

    Study, 2022. Guo et al., Frontiers in Psychiatry.

  4. Techniques such as active listening, using open questions and emotional words, responding appropriately to patients' emotional cues, and a patient-centred consulting style can assist in detection [of psychological distress].

    “Techniques such as active listening, using open questions and emotional words, responding appropriately to patients' emotional cues, and a patient-centred consulting style can assist in detection.”

    Review, 2005. Ryan et al., European Journal of Cancer Care.

  5. Oncology patients' responses to an open-ended question about empathic clinician behavior have revealed insights into a variety of behaviors that are perceived as demonstrative of empathy... such as listening and understanding and attention to the whole person.

    “Oncology patients' responses to an open-ended question about empathic clinician behavior have revealed insights into a variety of behaviors that are perceived as demonstrative of empathy. These include behaviors that imply sensitivity to the clinician-patient relationship, such as listening and understanding and attention to the whole person.”

    Observational study, 2021. Sanders et al., Cancer.

Listening is a three-part process.

Active-empathic listening requires sensing, processing, and responding. It is an active and emotional involvement that occurs in at least three key stages of the listening process.

Your empathy has a physical cost.

That may be why you feel drained after supporting others. If listening requires constant emotional involvement, it can lead to exhaustion in high-demand environments.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A quiet room at the end of the day.

You sit alone in a chair. Your head feels heavy. You can still hear the voices of the people you spent your shift supporting.

The honest summary

The sources confirm that active-empathic listening involves significant emotional involvement and that emotional labor is linked to exhaustion, but they do not explicitly prove that being a 'good listener' causes stress for everyone.

The research on emotional exhaustion is specifically focused on early childhood teachers and does not generalize to all types of listeners.
