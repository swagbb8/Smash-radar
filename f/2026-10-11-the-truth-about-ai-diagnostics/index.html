Can ai read your scans better than a doctor?

File no. 0068October 11, 2026The future

99% of studies have high risk of bias.

The evidence on ai diagnostic accuracy is frequently compromised by poor study quality and lack of external validation.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. In a review of 2976 studies, 99% of studies identified for inclusion had at least one area at high or unclear risk of bias or applicability concerns.

    “99% of studies identified for inclusion had at least one area at high or unclear risk of bias or applicability concerns.”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. McGenity et al., npj Digital Medicine.

  2. Extensive variation in methodology, terminology and outcome measures in deep learning studies can lead to an overestimation of the diagnostic accuracy of DL algorithms on medical imaging.

    “Heterogeneity was high between studies and extensive variation in methodology, terminology and outcome measures was noted. This can lead to an overestimation of the diagnostic accuracy of DL algorithms on medical imaging.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Aggarwal et al., npj Digital Medicine.

  3. Deep learning models have diagnostic performance that is equivalent to that of health-care professionals.

    “Our review found the diagnostic performance of deep learning models to be equivalent to that of health-care professionals.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Liu et al., The Lancet Digital Health.

  4. Radiologists were more specific than AI overall and in several subgroups, and were more sensitive to hip fractures before FDR correction.

    “Radiologists, on the other hand, were more specific than AI overall and in several subgroups, and more sensitive to hip fractures before FDR correction.”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Nowroozi et al., Clinical Radiology.

Ai matches human performance in some tasks.

Deep learning models have diagnostic performance equivalent to healthcare professionals. However, variation in methodology and terminology can lead to an overestimation of their accuracy.

The data is not settled.

That may be why claims about ai replacing specialists remain contested. While it performs well in specific areas, the underlying evidence is often inconsistent.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that AI can perform as well as specialists in some areas, but the reliability of these claims is frequently compromised by poor study quality and lack of external validation.

A significant majority of studies identified in these reviews showed at least one area of high or unclear risk of bias or applicability concerns.
