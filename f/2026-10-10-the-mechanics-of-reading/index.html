The uncomfortable truth

Does your screen actually help you remember?

File no. 0062October 10, 2026The human brain

Digital books can outperform paper with the right design.

While paper generally shows a small to moderate advantage in comprehension, digital books with story-congruent enhancements can result in higher scores. This finding is currently considered emerging and the evidence is mixed.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The negative effect of screen reading was most pronounced among high-performing girls.

    “and the negative effect of screen reading was most pronounced among high-performing girls.”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Støle et al., Computers & Education.

  2. The paper-based reading advantage is consistent for informational texts or a mix of informational and narrative texts, but not on those using only narrative texts.

    “the paper-based reading advantage was consistent across studies using informational texts, or a mix of informational and narrative texts, but not on those using only narrative texts”

    Meta-analysis, 2018. Delgado et al., Educational Research Review.

  3. Digital books with story-congruent enhancements outperformed paper books.

    “However, with story-congruent enhancements, digital books outperformed paper books.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Furenes et al., Review of Educational Research.

  4. Leisure digital reading is linked to a small significant effect on reading comprehension (r = . 055), which is smaller than the effects found for print reading.

    “The average effect size reflects a small significant effect on reading comprehension (r = . 055), which contrasts with the medium size effects found in the literature related to print reading habits and comprehension.”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Altamura et al., Review of Educational Research.

  5. Children's story comprehension scores were lower for digital books compared to paper books that only differed by digitization.

    “The comparison of digital versus paper books that only differed by digitization showed lower comprehension scores for digital books.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Furenes et al., Review of Educational Research.

  6. Students achieved lower scores on a digital reading comprehension test than on a paper version.

    “Results showed that the students in average achieved lower scores on the digital test than on the paper version.”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Støle et al., Computers & Education.

The medium depends on the content.

Paper reading provides a consistent advantage for informational texts. However, the gap narrows or disappears for narrative texts. Children's comprehension scores are lower on digital versions that only differ by digitization.

Design dictates your retention.

That may be why some digital experiences feel effortless while others feel shallow. The way a story is presented on a screen can either hinder your focus or enhance your understanding.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A commute on a train.

You hold a phone in one hand. You scroll through a story while the train moves. You finish the chapter but cannot recall the details of the setting or the characters' names.

The honest summary

The evidence generally shows that reading on paper leads to slightly better comprehension than reading on a screen, though this effect varies significantly depending on the text genre, the reader's age, and the specific design of the digital medium.

The quality of evidence is noted as low in some studies due to risks of bias, and findings are inconsistent across different types of content like narrative versus informational texts.
