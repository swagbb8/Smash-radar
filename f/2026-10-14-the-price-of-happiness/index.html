The uncomfortable truth

Does more money actually make you happier?

File no. 0088October 14, 2026Money and power

The data on your happiness is contested.

Some studies show well-being rises linearly with income for everyone. Others suggest emotional well-being stops improving at $75,000. This depends on how well-being is measured.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. There was no evidence for an experienced well-being plateau above $75,000/y.

    “There was no evidence for an experienced well-being plateau above $75,000/y, contrary to some influential past research.”

    Study, 2021. Killingsworth, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  2. Both experienced and evaluative well-being increased linearly with log(income), with an equally steep slope for higher earners as for lower earners.

    “the present results show that both experienced and evaluative well-being increased linearly with log(income), with an equally steep slope for higher earners as for lower earners.”

    Study, 2021. Killingsworth, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  3. Happiness increases steadily with log(income) among happier people, and even accelerates in the happiest group.

    “Happiness increases steadily with log(income) among happier people, and even accelerates in the happiest group.”

    Study, 2023. Killingsworth et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  4. Emotional well-being rises with log income, but there is no further progress beyond an annual income of ~$75,000.

    “Emotional well-being also rises with log income, but there is no further progress beyond an annual income of ~$75,000.”

    Observational study, 2010. Kahneman & Deaton, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  5. High income is linked to a steady rise in life evaluation when plotted against log income.

    “When plotted against log income, life evaluation rises steadily.”

    Observational study, 2010. Kahneman & Deaton, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

Different metrics show different paths.

Research shows experienced and evaluative well-being can increase steadily with higher income. However, one observational study found no further progress in emotional well-being past $75,000.

The goalpost might be moving.

That may be why you feel a constant need for more. If your emotions plateau while your life evaluation continues to rise, the pursuit of a higher number may never satisfy your internal state.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence is conflicting, with some studies showing a linear increase in well-being for all earners and others identifying a specific plateau for emotional well-being at $75,000.

The findings are complicated by different ways of measuring well-being, such as distinguishing between daily emotions and overall life evaluation.
