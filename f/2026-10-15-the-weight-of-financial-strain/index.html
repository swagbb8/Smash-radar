Does giving money actually cure depression?

File no. 0093October 15, 2026Money and power

Cash transfers do not significantly reduce depressive symptoms.

While 85% of interventions showed some positive impact on mental health outcomes in children, the evidence shows no significant impact on depressive symptoms specifically.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Eleven interventions (85%) showed a significant positive impact of cash transfers on at least one mental health outcome in children and young people, but the meta-analysis showed no impact of cash transfers on depressive symptoms (0.02, 95% CIs: −0.19 to 0.23; p=0.85).

    “Eleven interventions (85%) showed a significant positive impact of cash transfers on at least one mental health outcome in children and young people. However, no study found a positive effect on all mental health outcomes examined, and the meta-analysis showed no impact of cash transfers on depressive symptoms (0.02, 95% CIs: −0.19 to 0.23; p=0.85).”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Zimmerman et al., BMJ Global Health.

  2. Perceived and fluid socioeconomic indicators, specifically economic concerns and financial strain, could best predict depressive and anxiety symptoms.

    “We found that perceived and fluid SEC indicators, particularly economic concerns and financial strain could best predict depressive and anxiety symptoms.”

    Systematic review, 2024. Chan et al., BMC Psychology.

  3. Approximately 31% of parents of children with intellectual and developmental disabilities reach the clinical cut-off score for moderate depression, compared with 7% of parents of children without IDD.

    “Approximately one third (31%) of parents of children with IDD reach the clinical cut-off score for moderate depression, compared with 7% of parents of children without IDD.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Scherer et al., PLoS ONE.

  4. Medical student debt is linked to high levels of financial stress.

    “In addition, reported levels of financial stress were high among medical students, and correlated with debt.”

    Systematic review, 2019. Pisaniello et al., BMJ Open.

  5. Medical student debt is linked to poorer academic performance.

    “Finally, debt was also shown to be associated with poorer academic performance.”

    Systematic review, 2019. Pisaniello et al., BMJ Open.

Financial strain is a primary predictor of mental distress.

Economic concerns and financial strain are the strongest predictors of anxiety and depression. For example, medical student debt is linked to high levels of financial stress and poorer academic performance.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that financial strain and debt are linked to mental health problems, but the impact of financial interventions on specific symptoms like depression can be inconsistent.

The sources provide mixed results on whether financial changes directly improve specific mental health outcomes, such as depression.
