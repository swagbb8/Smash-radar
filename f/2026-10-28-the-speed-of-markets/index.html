Does high-frequency trading stabilize the market?

File no. 0187October 28, 2026The future

High-frequency trading both stabilizes and risks markets.

Studies show high-frequency trading can increase price efficiency while simultaneously exacerbating systemic risks like the 2010 Flash Crash. The evidence is mixed.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Available evidence indicates that high-frequency trading and algorithmic trading may have several beneficial effects on markets.

    “Despite commonly held negative perceptions, the available evidence indicates that HFT and algorithmic trading may have several beneficial effects on markets.”

    Review, 2018. Linton & Mahmoodzadeh, Annual Review of Economics.

  2. High-frequency traders facilitate price efficiency by trading in the direction of permanent price changes and in the opposite direction of transitory pricing errors.

    “Overall HFTs facilitate price efficiency by trading in the direction of permanent price changes and in the opposite direction of transitory pricing errors, both on average and on the highest volatility days.”

    Study, 2014. Brogaard et al., Review of Financial Studies.

  3. Moving from a bull to a bear market, Ethereum and Litecoin appear to become more illiquid, as opposed to Bitcoin, which appears to become more liquid.

    “Moving from a bull to a bear market, Ethereum and Litecoin appear to become more illiquid, as opposed to Bitcoin, which appears to become more liquid.”

    Study, 2020. Zhang et al., Journal of risk and financial management.

  4. The direction of high-frequency trading is linked to public information, such as macro news announcements and market-wide price movements.

    “The direction of HFTs' trading is correlated with public information, such as macro news announcements, market-wide price movements, and limit order book imbalances.”

    Study, 2014. Brogaard et al., Review of Financial Studies.

  5. High-frequency trading can exacerbate market instability and systemic risks, as demonstrated by incidents like the 2010 Flash Crash.

    “The review examines how HFT can exacerbate market instability and systemic risks, as demonstrated by incidents like the 2010 Flash Crash.”

    Experiment, 2024. Nahar et al., GLOBAL MAINSTREAM JOURNAL.

  6. High-frequency trading is the most examined strategy in studies, with 30% of studies focusing on it.

    “Most studies focus on equities (35%) and forex (30%), with high-frequency trading being the most examined strategy (30%).”

    Systematic review, 2025. Jukl & Lánský, Acta Informatica Pragensia.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that high-frequency trading has complex, dual effects, contributing to both market stability and efficiency while also posing risks of instability.

Several sources note a lack of data and the need for further work to inform long-term policies in this rapidly evolving field.
