Are you sober when you are tired?

File no. 0106October 17, 2026Health and environment

Your tired brain mirrors a drunk one.

After 20-25 hours of wakefulness, your performance decrements can equal a blood alcohol concentration of 0.10%.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. After 24 h of sustained wakefulness and with 0.08%BAC, individuals showed maximal cognitive impairment; however, the magnitude of impairment found for fatigue was equivalent only to that observed for 0.05%BAC.

    “After 24 h of sustained wakefulness and with 0.08%BAC, individuals showed maximal cognitive impairment; however, the magnitude of impairment found for fatigue was equivalent only to that observed for 0.05%BAC.”

    Study, 2005. Maruff et al., Journal of Sleep Research.

  2. Fatigue caused greater impairment than alcohol on the speed of continuous attention and memory and learning, and on the accuracy of complex matching.

    “It was demonstrated that fatigue caused greater impairment than alcohol on the speed of continuous attention and memory and learning, and on the accuracy of complex matching.”

    Study, 2003. Falleti et al., Journal of Sleep Research. This shows a link, not a cause.

  3. Acute sleep deprivation produced a disproportionate risk to driving safety by selectively and rapidly degrading operational vehicle control compared to cumulative sleep restriction.

    “cumulative sleep restriction produced only limited changes in driving performance, whereas acute sleep deprivation produced a disproportionate risk to driving safety by selectively and rapidly degrading operational vehicle control.”

    Randomized trial, 2026. Lakämper et al., Sleep.

  4. After 17-19 hours without sleep, performance on some tests was equivalent or worse than that at a BAC of 0.05%.

    “After 17-19 hours without sleep, corresponding to 2230 and 0100, performance on some tests was equivalent or worse than that at a BAC of 0.05%.”

    Study, 2000. Williamson & Feyer, Occupational and Environmental Medicine.

  5. After longer periods without sleep, performance reached levels equivalent to the maximum alcohol dose given to subjects (BAC of 0.1%).

    “After longer periods without sleep, performance reached levels equivalent to the maximum alcohol dose given to subjects (BAC of 0. 1%).”

    Study, 2000. Williamson & Feyer, Occupational and Environmental Medicine.

  6. Approximately 20-25 h of wakefulness produced performance decrements equivalent to those observed at a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.10%.

    “equating the performance impairment in the two conditions indicated that, depending on the task measured, approximately 20-25 h of wakefulness produced performance decrements equivalent to those observed at a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0. 10%.”

    Study, 1999. Lamond & Dawson, Journal of Sleep Research.

The impairment depends on what you do.

Evidence is mixed. Fatigue may match 0.05% BAC in some tests but 0.10% in others. The severity depends on the specific task and the method used to measure it.

Your alertness is not a constant.

That may be why your ability to drive or learn fluctuates. Your brain does not fail all at once, but degrades specific functions at different rates.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The late night drive.

You sit in your car at 2 a.m. The road is clear. Your hands feel heavy on the wheel as you try to stay focused on the white lines.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that sleep deprivation causes significant cognitive and motor impairments that can match alcohol intoxication, but the severity of these effects is task-dependent and may be overestimated in some studies.

The magnitude of fatigue-related impairment is highly dependent on the specific task measured and the statistical methods used to account for performance variability.
