The world you don’t see

Where do your antibiotics go after you stop taking them?

File no. 0199October 30, 2026The future

The environment may only reflect your clinic.

Antibiotic resistance in our surroundings might not be an independent force. It may simply mirror the resistance levels found in regional hospitals.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The plastisphere acts as a potential enrichment site for high-risk resistance genes.

    “these findings highlighted the plastisphere acts as a potential enrichment site for high-risk resistance genes”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Zarean et al., Environmental pollution (Barking, Essex : 1987).

  2. Antibiotic resistance in the environment can be a mere reflection of the regional antibiotic resistance situation in the clinic.

    “In this Review, we present our current understanding of the roles of the environment, including antibiotic pollution, in resistance evolution, in transmission and as a mere reflection of the regional antibiotic resistance situation in the clinic.”

    Review, 2022. Larsson & Flach, Nature reviews. Microbiology.

  3. Non-biodegradable polymers consistently demonstrated stronger enrichment than biodegradable ones in the plastisphere.

    “Non-biodegradable polymers consistently demonstrated stronger enrichment than biodegradable ones.”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Zarean et al., Environmental pollution (Barking, Essex : 1987).

  4. Antibiotic resistance genes are found in 50 samples from 10 typical environments, with 260 ARG subtypes belonging to 18 ARG types detected.

    “A metagenomic approach and network analysis was used to investigate the wide-spectrum profiles of antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) and their co-occurrence patterns in 50 samples from 10 typical environments. In total, 260 ARG subtypes belonging to 18 ARG types were detected”

    Study, 2015. Li et al., The ISME Journal.

  5. Environmental biofilms can be hotspots for the dissemination of antibiotic resistance genes.

    “Several studies have demonstrated that environmental biofilms can be hotspots for the dissemination of ARGs.”

    Review, 2023. Michaelis & Grohmann, Antibiotics.

  6. The spread of vancomycin resistance genes in different environments is linked to the selective pressure resulting from vancomycin use.

    “The widespread occurrences and abundance variation trend of vancomycin resistance genes in different environments might imply the spread of vancomycin resistance genes because of the selective pressure resulting from vancomycin use.”

    Study, 2015. Li et al., The ISME Journal.

Resistance genes inhabit your everyday objects.

Non-biodegradable polymers in the plastisphere enrich high-risk genes. Biofilms also act as hotspots for these genes to move between different environments.

The cycle of resistance is closed.

That may be why the spread of vancomycin resistance is so closely linked to its use. The environment acts as a reservoir for the medicines we already use.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A discarded plastic bottle in a stream.

Water flows past it. Bacteria settle on the surface. They share genes that help them survive the drugs used in nearby towns.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that antibiotic resistance genes are widespread in various environments and can be concentrated in specific niches like biofilms and microplastics.

It is unclear whether environmental resistance is an independent driver or merely a reflection of clinical antibiotic use.
