The uncomfortable truth

Does stress always change your brain?

File no. 0092October 15, 2026The human brain

Chronic stress does not always shrink your brain.

While some studies show reduced hippocampal volume, others show no effect or even volume increases in resilient subjects.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Chronic unpredictable mild stress failed to affect hippocampal volume in rats.

    “Moreover, we found an increased hippocampal volume in rats exposed to CMS after clomipramine treatment while CMS failed to affect hippocampal volume.”

    Experiment, 2022. Zhang et al., Translational Psychiatry.

  2. Chronic social defeat stress caused a significant increase in left hippocampal volume in resilient mice, but not in susceptible mice.

    “After chronic social defeat stress, we found significant increases in left hippocampal volume in resilient and nonstressed control mice. Intriguingly, this increase in hippocampal volume was not found in susceptible mice”

    Observational study, 2014. Tse et al., Hippocampus. This shows a link, not a cause.

  3. No association was observed between maternal cortisol in pregnancy and child hippocampus volume in either sex.

    “No association between maternal cortisol in pregnancy and child hippocampus volume was observed in either sex.”

    Observational study, 2012. Buß et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  4. Chronic stress-induced atrophy of hippocampal astrocyte size is associated with decreased glycogen content.

    “Results suggest that decreased glycogen content was associated with chronic stress-induced atrophy of hippocampal astrocyte size and depression-like behavior.”

    Experiment, 2017. Zhao et al., Scientific Reports.

  5. Prolactin protects neurogenesis in the dentate gyrus of chronically stressed mice and promotes neuronal fate.

    “The results demonstrate that PRL protects neurogenesis in the DG of chronically stressed mice and promotes neuronal fate.”

    Experiment, 2009. Torner et al., Journal of Neuroscience.

  6. Chronic stress causes a reduction in hippocampal volume.

    “Chronic exposure to stress results in a reduction of hippocampal neurogenesis and of hippocampal volume.”

    Experiment, 2009. Torner et al., Journal of Neuroscience.

The brain responds differently to pressure.

Chronic stress can cause hippocampal atrophy and reduced glycogen content. However, some resilient subjects show increased volume. Results vary by stressor type and individual susceptibility.

Resilience is not a constant state.

That may be why your reaction to pressure feels unpredictable. Your brain structure might be adapting in ways that are not uniform or visible to everyone.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence is mixed, showing that chronic stress can reduce hippocampal volume in some contexts but does not consistently cause it across all subjects or conditions.

The findings vary significantly depending on the specific type of stressor, the species studied, and whether the subjects are susceptible or resilient to the stress.
