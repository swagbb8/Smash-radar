Is your social capital actually disappearing?

File no. 0162October 24, 2026The future

Remote work shows no evidence of eroding weak ties.

While telecommuting is linked to work overload in secret places and an eroded line between personal and professional life, there is no data showing it harms long-term innovation or organizational social capital.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. The magnitude of peer examiner influences are just as strong, or stronger, than the influence of the examination styles of supervisors.

    “the magnitude of these peer examiner influences are just as strong, or stronger, than the influence of the examination styles of supervisors.”

    Study, 2017. Frakes & Wasserman, National Bureau of Economic Research.

  2. The loss of local newspapers is linked to significant increases in property and victimless crimes, declines in voter turnout, and heightened political polarization.

    “the loss of local newspapers is associated with significant increases in property and victimless crimes, declines in voter turnout, and heightened political polarization.”

    Study, 2026. Haddock et al., Journal of Regional Science.

  3. Knowledge spillovers among patent examiners are linked to flows regarding specific pieces of prior art that bear on the patentability of the applications in question.

    “some amount of these spillovers may reflect knowledge flows regarding specific pieces of prior art that bear on the patentability of the applications in question”

    Study, 2017. Frakes & Wasserman, National Bureau of Economic Research.

  4. Telecommuting is linked to work overload in secret places and an eroded line between personal and professional life.

    “Telecommuting has resulted in a work overload in secret places. This has eroded the line between personal and professional life, increasing the risk of conflict.”

    Study, 2023. Bhat et al., Cogent Business & Management.

  5. Job autonomy and FSSBs are linked to work–life balance and job satisfaction, respectively.

    “job autonomy and FSSBs have significant positive direct and indirect effects on work–life balance and job satisfaction, respectively”

    Study, 2021. Jamal et al., Sustainability.

The evidence is mixed and limited.

Research links telecommuting to secret work overload and blurred boundaries between home and work. However, the data does not support the claim that remote work destroys social capital or innovation.

The boundaries of your work are thinning.

That may be why you feel like you are always on call. The evidence suggests the issue is not a loss of social connection, but a lack of physical separation between your life and your job.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The provided sources do not contain data regarding the erosion of organizational social capital or 'weak ties' due to remote work.

The sources focus on patent office knowledge spillovers, farming social capital, and news deserts rather than organizational social capital in remote work settings.
