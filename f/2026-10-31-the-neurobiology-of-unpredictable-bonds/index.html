Your brain is being hijacked

Why does uncertainty feel like a connection?

File no. 0210October 31, 2026Human relationships

Intermittent reinforcement is linked to anxious-dependent attachment.

The evidence shows that inconsistent patterns of reward contribute to anxious-dependent attachment in digital romantic relationships.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. In rats, chronic intermittent cocaine use, even during short daily bouts, is sufficient to promote addiction symptoms.

    “This suggests that chronic intermittent cocaine use, even during short daily bouts, is sufficient to promote addiction symptoms.”

    Study, 2018. Allain & Samaha, Addiction Biology.

  2. Intermittent reinforcement is linked to anxious-dependent attachment patterns in digital romantic relationships.

    “ANXIOUS-DEPENDENT ATTACHMENT IN DIGITAL ROMANTIC RELATIONSHIPS: EMOTIONAL DYSREGULATION, INTERMITTENT REINFORCEMENT, AND FEAR OF ABANDONMENT”

    Study, 2025. Klimovskyi, PSYCHOLOGICAL JOURNAL.

  3. Stress, alcohol, and other drugs of abuse are linked to increased Toll-like receptor signaling in the brain.

    “Multiple TLRs, HMGB1, and miRNAs are induced in the brain by stress, alcohol, and other drugs of abuse and are increased in the postmortem human alcoholic brain.”

    Review, 2017. Crews et al., Psychopharmacology.

  4. Methamphetamine use is linked to difficulties employing effective emotion regulation and difficulty engaging in adaptive or goal-directed decision-making.

    “Together, the results suggest methamphetamine users have an altered experience of negative outcomes, difficulties employing effective emotion regulation, and difficulty engaging in adaptive or goal-directed decision-making.”

    Review, 2020. May et al., Frontiers in Psychiatry.

  5. Alcohol and negative reinforcement are associated with alcohol consumption as a function of alcohol.

    “Alcohol and negative reinforcement are associated with alcohol consumption as a function of alcohol”

    Review, 2020. Koob et al., American Journal of Psychiatry.

Reward patterns shape your behavior.

Intermittent reinforcement is linked to addiction symptoms. Research shows this pattern promotes behaviors similar to substance use and contributes to specific attachment styles.

The loop of uncertainty.

That may be why you find yourself drawn to unpredictable interactions. Your brain may be responding to a reinforcement pattern similar to addiction.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The blue light in the dark.

You wait for a notification that does not come. You check your screen again. You wait for the next inconsistent reply to arrive in the middle of the night.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that intermittent reinforcement promotes addiction-like behaviors and contributes to anxious attachment, but it does not directly compare the brain's preference for toxic bonds over healthy ones.

The sources do not provide a direct neurobiological comparison between the dopamine responses of healthy romantic bonds versus toxic ones.
