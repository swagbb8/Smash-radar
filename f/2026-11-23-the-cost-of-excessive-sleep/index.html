Is your sleep actually impairing your brain?

File no. 0321November 23, 2026Psychology

Chronic excessive sleep causes large impairments in attention.

People with narcolepsy type 1 and idiopathic hypersomnia show large impairments in attention, with effect sizes of -0.90 and -0.97 respectively.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Cognitive performance mainly improves until 120 min after a nap, but there are conflicting results during the sleep inertia period.

    “Whatever the model used, performance mainly improved until 120 min after nap, with conflicting results during the sleep inertia period.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Dutheil et al., International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

  2. People with narcolepsy type 1 (NT1) and idiopathic hypersomnia (IH) show large impairments in attention, with d = -0.90 and d = -0.97 respectively.

    “Attention showed large impairment in people with NT1 (d = -0. 90) and IH (d = -0. 97), and moderate impairment in NT2 (d = -0. 60).”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Harel et al., SLEEP Advances.

  3. Napping in the afternoon improves cognitive performance, with early naps before 1.00 p.m. giving better results of 0.24, -0.07 to 0.34.

    “Early naps in the afternoon (before 1. 00 p.m.) gave better cognitive performance (0. 24, -0. 07 to 0. 34).”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Dutheil et al., International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

  4. Athletes should allow 30 min to reduce sleep inertia prior to training or competition to obtain better performance outcomes.

    “Finally, athletes should allow 30 min to reduce sleep inertia prior to training or competition to obtain better performance outcomes.”

    Systematic review, 2021. Lastella et al., Nature and Science of Sleep.

  5. Sleep inertia causes a temporary period of reduced alertness and impaired performance where alertness and sustained attention are more affected than cognitive throughput and working memory.

    “The transition from sleep to wakefulness entails a temporary period of reduced alertness and impaired performance known as sleep inertia. ... However, alertness and sustained attention were more affected than cognitive throughput and working memory.”

    Study, 2013. Santhi et al., PLoS ONE. This shows a link, not a cause.

  6. Sustained attention is the most impaired domain in narcolepsy type 1, narcolepsy type 2, and idiopathic hypersomnia, with an effect size of approximately -0.5 to -1.

    “A secondary meta-analysis identified sustained attention as the most impaired domain in NT1, NT2, and IH (d ≈ -0. 5 to -1).”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Harel et al., SLEEP Advances.

Napping provides benefits if timed correctly.

Afternoon naps before 1.00 p.m. can improve cognitive performance. However, sleep inertia causes a temporary period of reduced alertness and impaired performance immediately after waking.

Timing dictates your cognitive state.

That may be why a poorly timed nap leaves you feeling groggy while a strategic one improves your output. The evidence on these outcomes is mixed.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The afternoon slump.

You sit at your desk at 2.00 p.m. and lean your head on your hand. You feel heavy. You take a quick rest and return to the screen feeling confused.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while chronic hypersomnolence is linked to significant cognitive deficits, strategic napping can be beneficial for performance provided it avoids the period of sleep inertia.

The research is limited by a lack of studies intersecting sleep restriction and workload overload, and many napping studies were conducted in laboratory settings rather than real work conditions.
