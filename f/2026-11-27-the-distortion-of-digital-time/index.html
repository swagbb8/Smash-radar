Your brain is being hijacked

Does your screen change how you feel time?

File no. 0340November 27, 2026Science and reality

Interactive media increases your perception of time.

Engaging with interactive content changes how you experience the passage of hours. This finding is currently supported but not definitively proven as a universal rule.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Processing of stimuli from both the inside and outside of the body converges primarily in the supramarginal gyrus bilaterally.

    “Using a multilevel kernel density analysis, we found that processing of stimuli of the two domains converges primarily in the supramarginal gyrus bilaterally.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Salvato et al., Human Brain Mapping.

  2. A right-lateralized set of areas, including the precentral and postcentral, and superior temporal gyri, are candidates to match multiple body-related signals.

    “Furthermore, we found a right-lateralized set of areas, including the precentral and postcentral, and superior temporal gyri. We discuss these results and propose this set of areas as ideal candidates to match multiple body-related signals”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Salvato et al., Human Brain Mapping.

  3. Interactive media is linked to increased time perception compared to non-interactive media.

    “Results showed reduced agentive involuntariness, but increased flow, presence, character involvement, and time perception for interactive compared to non-interactive media.”

    Experiment, 2021. Polito & Hitchens, Psychology of Popular Media.

  4. Personality traits of openness and conscientiousness are linked to moderate alteration of aspects of self representation during media engagement.

    “Personality traits of openness and conscientiousness were also associated with moderate alteration of aspects self representation during media engagement.”

    Experiment, 2021. Polito & Hitchens, Psychology of Popular Media.

  5. Absorption is linked to increased flow, presence, and character involvement.

    “Absorption was associated with increased flow, presence and character involvement.”

    Experiment, 2021. Polito & Hitchens, Psychology of Popular Media.

Your brain integrates body signals to map reality.

Specific regions in your brain, like the supramarginal gyrus, process signals from both inside and outside your body. These areas help match multiple body-related signals together.

Your sense of presence is being recalibrated.

That may be why interactive media creates a deeper sense of flow and character involvement. It alters how you represent yourself while you are engaged.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The late night scroll.

You sit on your couch at 11 p.m. The screen glows in your hand. You lose track of the room around you as the game or app responds to your every move.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that interactive media can increase time perception and flow, but it does not specifically confirm that high-frequency consumption distorts neural processing speed.

The study on time perception (S2) used 220 participants and did not specify the frequency or duration of digital consumption.
