Is your willpower actually your own?

File no. 0048October 8, 2026Psychology

Family background explains the marshmallow test.

The link between waiting for a marshmallow and later success is largely a reflection of a child's early environment and social support.,

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. The marshmallow test is linked to problem behavior because it reflects aspects of a child's early environment rather than self-control.

    “These relationships were better explained by social support than by self-control, suggesting that the marshmallow test is predictive because it reflects aspects of a child's early environment that are important over the long term.”

    Observational study, 2020. Michaelson & Munakata, Psychological science.

  2. When controlling for family background, early cognitive ability, and the home environment, the bivariate correlation between waiting an extra minute at age 4 and achievement at age 15 was reduced by two thirds.

    “But this bivariate correlation was only half the size of those reported in the original studies and was reduced by two thirds in the presence of controls for family background, early cognitive ability, and the home environment.”

    Study, 2018. Watts et al., Psychological science.

  3. Participants were less willing to wait for delayed rewards from less trustworthy characters, and perceived trustworthiness predicted willingness to delay gratification.

    “Across two experiments, participants were less willing to wait for delayed rewards from less trustworthy characters, and perceived trustworthiness predicted willingness to delay gratification.”

    Experiment, 2013. Michaelson et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

  4. A board game designed to increase delay of gratification improved wait times during the Marshmallow Test in one study, but had no effect in a second study.

    “In Study 1, the intervention group also increased wait times during the Marshmallow Test versus controls (p=0. 047). In Study 2, there was no effect on Marshmallow Test wait times.”

    Experiment, 2020. Anzman‐Frasca et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

  5. Individuals who generally think and act in a future-oriented manner have a stronger ability to delay gratification.

    “Individuals who generally think and act in a future-oriented manner have a stronger ability to delay gratification.”

    Experiment, 2018. Goellner et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

Context matters more than willpower.

When researchers account for family background and home environment, the correlation between waiting and achievement drops by two thirds. Trust in the person offering the reward also predicts behavior.,

Your habits are not just choices.

That may be why people in different circumstances face different hurdles. Success might depend more on the safety of your environment than on your internal strength.,

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while the marshmallow test correlates with certain outcomes, these links are significantly weakened or explained away by family background and social factors.

The studies show mixed results regarding the effectiveness of specific interventions, such as board games, in actually improving delay of gratification.
