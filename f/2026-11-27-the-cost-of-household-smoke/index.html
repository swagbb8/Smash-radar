Is your home's warmth costing you your health?

This global burden of premature mortality is a consequence of household air pollution from solid fuels. The evidence is mixed regarding the specific impact of rustic wood smoke on cardiovascular risk.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Exposure to household air pollution is linked to a 19% increased risk of postnatal stunting.

    “A 19% increased risk of postnatal stunting (95% CI: 1. 10, 1. 29) was also associated with postnatal exposure to household air pollution.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Pun et al., Environmental Science and Pollution Research.

  2. Exposure to CO is not a consistently valid surrogate measure of exposure to PM2.5.

    “Our results suggest that exposure to CO is not a consistently valid surrogate measure of exposure to PM2. 5.”

    Systematic review, 2017. Carter et al., Environmental Health Perspectives.

  3. Household cooking with solid fuels accounted for 12% of APM2.5 globally in 2010.

    “In 2010, household cooking with solid fuels accounted for 12% of APM2.5 globally, varying from 0% of APM2.5 in five higher-income regions to 37% (2.8 μg/m3 of 6.9 μg/m3 total) in southern sub-Saharan Africa.”

    Study, 2014. Chafe et al., Environmental Health Perspectives.

  4. Household air pollution is linked to a substantial premature mortality burden globally of about 0.77(0.54-1) million excess deaths.

    “The median estimate indicates that the household contribution to ambient air pollution results in a substantial premature mortality burden globally of about 0. 77(0. 54-1) million excess deaths”

    Review, 2023. Chowdhury et al., Environment International.

Solid fuels shape the air you breathe.

Household cooking with solid fuels accounted for 12% of global air pollution in 2010. This pollution is linked to a 19% increased risk of postnatal stunting.

That may be why the air inside your home carries a weight you cannot see. It affects the physical development of children and the lifespan of adults.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that household air pollution from solid fuels is a major driver of global mortality and developmental issues, but it does not specifically isolate 'rustic' wood smoke from fireplaces as a primary cause of cardiovascular risk.

Several studies note that the global burden of health impacts may have been underestimated due to limitations in exposure assessment and confounding factors.
