The uncomfortable truth

Can you trust your own memories?

File no. 0136October 21, 2026Psychology

You can remember things that never happened.

Misinformation can implant rich autobiographical false memories. This effect is stronger when guided imagery or pressure to answer is used.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. A substantial number of participants yielded to the misinformation effect even though they had correct memory about the original materials.

    “A substantial number of the participants yielded to the misinformation effect in the memory test even though they had correct memory about the original (and postevent) materials.”

    Experiment, 2017. Polczyk, Polish Psychological Bulletin.

  2. Guided imagery and pressure to answer make it more likely to implant rich autobiographical false memories than non-directive instructions.

    “The moderator techniques for implanting false memories revealed a significantly lower probability of implanting false rich memory with non-directive instructions ( d = 0. 90[0. 53, 1. 27]) than with guided imagery ( d = 1. 45[1. 32, 1. 58]), or with pressure to answer ( d = 1. 56[1. 17, 1. 95]) instructions.”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Arce et al., International journal of clinical and health psychology : IJCHP.

  3. Post-warnings are surprisingly effective, reducing the misinformation effect to less than half of its size on average.

    “post-warnings are surprisingly effective, reducing the misinformation effect to less than half of its size on average.”

    Meta-analysis, 2014. Blank & Launay, Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition.

  4. DRM 'false memories' and misinformation effect 'false memories' do not appear to be equivalent.

    “Thus DRM 'false memories' and misinformation effect 'false memories' do not appear to be equivalent.”

    Experiment, 2013. Ost et al., PLoS ONE.

  5. People with better analytical reasoning skills and a high level of interest in the news topic are least likely to report false memories for fake news.

    “Participants with better analytical reasoning skills and a high level of interest in the news topic were least likely to report false memories for fake news”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Schincariol et al., Memory Mind & Media.

  6. Higher levels of ability are associated with reduced susceptibility to misinformation.

    “Across almost all categories, higher levels of ability were associated with reduced susceptibility to misinformation.”

    Systematic review, 2024. Brassil et al., Psychonomic Bulletin & Review.

Memory is not a fixed record.

A substantial number of people yield to the misinformation effect even when they have correct memories of the original materials. Social influence may play a role alongside memory failure.

Your history is malleable.

That may be why some people are less likely to report false memories. Analytical reasoning and high interest in a topic can reduce your susceptibility to distorted information.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that false memories can be successfully implanted or distorted by misinformation, though the extent of this effect varies by individual cognitive traits and the specific methods used.

The research suggests that some people may give wrong answers even when they correctly remember the original information, indicating that social influence may play a role alongside memory failure.
