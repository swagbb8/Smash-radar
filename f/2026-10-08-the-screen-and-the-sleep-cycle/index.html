Does a screen change how a child sleeps?

File no. 0023October 8, 2026Technology

The evidence for preschooler sleep disruption is mixed.

While screen time is linked to poorer sleep outcomes in infants and toddlers, meta-analysis did not confirm these unfavorable associations in preschoolers.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Meta-analysis confirmed these unfavorable associations in infants and toddlers but not preschoolers.

    “Meta-analysis confirmed these unfavorable associations in infants and toddlers but not preschoolers.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Janssen et al., Sleep Medicine Reviews.

  2. Screen time is linked to poorer sleep outcomes in infants, toddlers and preschoolers.

    “Results indicate that screen time is associated with poorer sleep outcomes in infants, toddlers and preschoolers.”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Janssen et al., Sleep Medicine Reviews.

  3. Excessive screen time is linked to shorter sleep duration among toddlers and preschoolers.

    “Strong evidence of the meta-analysis suggested that excessive screen time was associated with overweight/obesity and shorter sleep duration among toddlers and preschoolers.”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Li et al., International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

  4. Excessive screen time is linked to disrupted sleep.

    “Excessive screen time ([Formula: see text] 2 h per day) is associated with childhood overweight and obesity, physical inactivity, increased sedentary time, unfavorable dietary behaviors, and disrupted sleep.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Jones et al., International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity.

  5. Sleep latency, percentages of stage 1 and stage 2 significantly increased with age in adults.

    “In adults, it appeared that sleep latency, percentages of stage 1 and stage 2 significantly increased with age while percentage of REM sleep decreased.”

    Meta-analysis, 2004. Ohayon et al., SLEEP.

Screen time affects young children differently.

Excessive screen time is linked to shorter sleep duration in toddlers and preschoolers. It is also linked to disrupted sleep across these age groups.

Age changes the impact of the screen.

The strength and consistency of the link between screens and sleep vary by age group. That may be why the impact is not uniform for every child.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows a link between screen time and poorer sleep in young children, but the strength and consistency of this association vary by age group.

The overall quality of evidence for these associations in children under 5 was reported as very low.
