How much nature do you actually need?

File no. 0037October 8, 2026Health and environment

Nature benefits peak at specific doses.

While 120 minutes a week is linked to better health, the most effective interventions for mental health occur in doses of 20 to 90 minutes.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. The most effective nature-based interventions were offered for between 8 and 12 weeks, and the optimal dose ranged from 20 to 90 min.

    “The most effective interventions were offered for between 8 and 12 weeks, and the optimal dose ranged from 20 to 90 min.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Coventry et al., SSM - Population Health.

  2. Positive associations between nature contact and health peaked between 200-300 minutes per week with no further gain.

    “Positive associations peaked between 200-300 mins per week with no further gain.”

    Observational study, 2019. White et al., Scientific Reports.

  3. Residential greenness is linked to a statistically significant association with respiratory disease, low birth weight, cardiovascular disease, and other three diseases, except for allergic disease.

    “The meta-analysis result showed a statistically significant association between the residential greenness versus respiratory disease, low birth weight, cardiovascular disease, other three diseases, except for allergic disease.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Qiu et al., Ecological Indicators.

  4. Spending at least 120 minutes a week in nature is linked to a significantly greater likelihood of reporting good health and high well-being compared to no nature contact.

    “Compared to no nature contact last week, the likelihood of reporting good health or high well-being became significantly greater with contact ≥120 mins (e. g. 120-179 mins: ORs [95%CIs]: Health = 1. 59 [1. 31-1. 92]; Well-being = 1. 23 [1. 08-1. 40]).”

    Observational study, 2019. White et al., Scientific Reports.

  5. Seven (78%) of the nine studies found a significant inverse relationship between an increase in surrounding greenness per 0·1 NDVI in a buffer zone of 500 m or less and the risk of all-cause mortality.

    “Seven (78%) of the nine studies found a significant inverse relationship between an increase in surrounding greenness per 0·1 NDVI in a buffer zone of 500 m or less and the risk of all-cause mortality”

    Meta-analysis, 2019. Rueda et al., The Lancet Planetary Health.

The limits of nature contact.

Positive associations with health peak between 200-300 minutes per week. Beyond this point, there is no further gain.

The math of well-being.

That may be why you do not need hours in the woods to see a change. Precise, shorter intervals may be the intended mechanism for mental relief.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A walk to the corner.

You stand by the tree on your street for twenty minutes before entering your apartment building. The air is still. The leaves are heavy with dust. You breathe in once before the door clicks shut behind you.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that regular nature contact and structured nature-based interventions are linked to improved mental and physical health outcomes, with specific benefits observed for disadvantaged groups.

The evidence for physical health improvements from nature-based interventions is less substantial than the evidence for mental health benefits.
