Is your school schedule hurting your sleep?

File no. 0008October 5, 2026The human brain

The data on school start times is unreliable.

While some studies link later start times to better sleep, the quality of this evidence is very low due to a high risk of bias.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Later school start times are linked to better overall developmental outcomes, longer sleep duration, and less negative mood.

    “There is converging evidence that later SSTs are associated with better overall developmental outcomes, longer sleep duration, and less negative mood.”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Yip et al., PEDIATRICS.

  2. The quality of the body of evidence regarding the effects of later school start times is very low because most studies were rated as being at high or unclear risk of bias.

    “Overall, the quality of the body of evidence was very low, as we rated most studies as being at high or unclear risk of bias with respect to allocation, attrition, absence of randomization, and the collection of baseline data.”

    Systematic review, 2017. Marx et al., Campbell Systematic Reviews.

  3. Reduced sleep duration was significantly higher in morning-shift students than in afternoon-shift students.

    “Reduced sleep duration was significantly higher in morning-shift students.”

    Experiment, 2020. Estevan et al., Journal of Biological Rhythms.

  4. The prevalence of reduced sleep durations (average weekly sleep duration, SDweek <8 h) was higher in morning-shift students (almost 80%) than in afternoon-shift ones (34%).

    “the prevalence of reduced sleep durations (average weekly sleep duration, SDweek <8 h) to be higher in morning-shift students (almost 80%) than in afternoon-shift ones (34%).”

    Experiment, 2020. Estevan et al., Journal of Biological Rhythms.

  5. Later school start times are linked to lower levels of sleepiness for high school youth compared to middle school youth.

    “Later SSTs were more strongly associated with lower levels of sleepiness for high school (versus middle school) youth”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Yip et al., PEDIATRICS.

  6. Later school start times promoted longer sleep duration over 1 week in one study, but that study had a high risk of bias.

    “Later school start times promoted longer sleep duration over 1 week (1 study, high risk of bias).”

    Systematic review, 2024. Gaskin et al., SLEEP Advances.

Sleep duration varies by schedule.

Students in morning shifts have significantly higher rates of reduced sleep. Almost 80% of morning-shift students had average weekly sleep of less than 8 hours.

Your schedule may dictate your biology.

That may be why you feel constant fatigue. The evidence suggests a link between later start times and improved sleep, but the results remain inconsistent.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence suggests a link between later school start times and improved sleep for adolescents, but the data is inconsistent and often of low quality.

Many of the studies cited have a high or uncertain risk of bias, making it difficult to be confident about the actual effects of later school start times.
