Does every noise affect a child's brain?

File no. 0335November 26, 2026Science and reality

Noise affects children differently depending on the source.

Evidence links aircraft noise to lower reading performance, but some road and railway noise show no effect on attention or executive function.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. There was no effect for some outcomes such as attention and executive function for some noise sources such as road traffic noise and railway noise.

    “no effect for other outcomes such as attention and executive function and for some noise sources such as road traffic noise and railway noise.”

    Systematic review, 2018. Clark & Paunović, International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

  2. Aircraft noise effects on reading comprehension and on long-term memory were found to be of moderate quality.

    “the quality of the evidence across studies ranged from being of moderate quality for an effect for some outcomes, e.g. , aircraft noise effects on reading comprehension and on long-term memory”

    Systematic review, 2018. Clark & Paunović, International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

  3. Indoor noise and reverberation in classroom settings is linked to poorer performance of children in verbal tasks.

    “Indoor noise and reverberation in classroom settings were found to be associated with poorer performance of the children in verbal tasks.”

    Review, 2013. Klatte et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

  4. High exposure to aircraft noise is linked to lower reading performance.

    “Regarding chronic exposure to aircraft noise, studies consistently found that high exposure is associated with lower reading performance.”

    Review, 2013. Klatte et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

  5. The evidence so far suggests that noise exposure is associated with cognition, but more good quality research using standardised methodology is required.

    “The evidence so far suggests that noise exposure is associated with cognition, but more good quality research using standardised methodology is required to corroborate these results”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Thompson et al., Environment International.

The evidence on noise and cognition is inconsistent.

Studies show links between noise and cognitive performance, but many lack standardized methodology or high-quality evidence. Results vary by noise type and cognitive domain.

The environment shapes cognitive development.

That may be why children in noisy classrooms struggle with verbal tasks. It suggests that specific types of noise may have distinct impacts on how children learn.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence suggests a link between noise and cognitive performance in children, but results are inconsistent and often of low or moderate quality.

Many studies have low-quality evidence, lack standardized methodology, or fail to sufficiently control for confounding variables.
