Is your loneliness causing a physical reaction in your brain?

File no. 0176October 25, 2026The human brain

Your loneliness may be a biological inflammation.

Social isolation and psychosocial stress are linked to neuroinflammation and microglial overactivation. This evidence is mixed and relies on a mix of mouse models and human observational studies.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Social isolation in mice makes the number of hippocampal microglia decline while causing them to become overactive.

    “a decline in microglia number and overactivation as determined by significant morphological changes including decreases in lacunarity, perimeter, and cell size and increase in cell density.”

    Study, 2022. Omran et al., Journal of Neuroinflammation. This shows a link, not a cause.

  2. Specific gene regulation and polymorphisms can directly or indirectly cause the feeling of loneliness and social isolation.

    “It was found that the specific gene regulation (CTRA and APOE-e4 genes expression) along with polymorphism (CHRNA4:rs1044396 and BDNF) can directly or indirectly cause the feeling of loneliness and social isolation.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Salari et al., The Journal of Genetic Psychology. This shows a link, not a cause.

  3. Social isolation in mice makes serum corticosterone levels increase and activates the NF-κB pathway.

    “social isolation induced an increase in serum corticosterone level and activation in NF-κB pathway.”

    Study, 2022. Omran et al., Journal of Neuroinflammation.

  4. Neuroinflammation and involvement of genetic changes in the Nucleus Accumbens can potentially lead to loneliness feelings.

    “Reports showed that neuroinflammation and involvement of genetic changes in the Nucleus Accumbens can potentially lead to loneliness feelings.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Salari et al., The Journal of Genetic Psychology. This shows a link, not a cause.

  5. Chronic stress can eventually lead to atherosclerosis, a chronic vascular inflammatory disease.

    “If the patient has a genetic predisposition and a combination of environmental factors triggers the pathogenesis, then chronic stress can eventually lead to AS.”

    Systematic review, 2021. Meng et al., Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine. This shows a link, not a cause.

  6. Psychosocial stressors are linked to elevated microglial activity in the prefrontal cortex in ∼75 % of studies.

    “Of those examining the prefrontal cortex, ∼75 % found psychosocial stress resulted in elevated Iba-1 activity.”

    Systematic review, 2016. Calcia et al., Psychopharmacology.

The body reacts to isolation like a physical stress.

Social isolation in mice is linked to increased serum corticosterone levels and the activation of the NF-κB pathway. This correlates with overactive microglia in the brain.

Your social life is a physiological state.

That may be why chronic stress is linked to atherosclerosis. Your internal chemistry may be reacting to the lack of human connection as a systemic threat.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The sources confirm that social isolation and psychosocial stress trigger neuroinflammatory responses like microglial overactivation, but they do not explicitly state that these pathways are identical to those of physical injury.

The evidence relies on a mix of mouse models and human observational studies, which limits the ability to confirm exact biological similarities between loneliness and physical injury.
