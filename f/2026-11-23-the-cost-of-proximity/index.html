Your brain is being hijacked

Does your phone drain your brain when it is silent?

File no. 0322November 23, 2026Social media

The mere presence of a phone reduces your cognitive capacity.

Even if you do not touch the device, it occupies your mental resources. The evidence on this is mixed, however, as some larger studies find no significant effect.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The mere presence of a smartphone reduces available cognitive capacity, even when people successfully avoid checking the device.

    “even when people are successful at maintaining sustained attention—as when avoiding the temptation to check their phones—the mere presence of these devices reduces available cognitive capacity.”

    Experiment, 2017. Ward et al., Journal of the Association for Consumer Research.

  2. A meta-analysis found that the mere presence of a smartphone negatively impacts working memory capacity, but has no significant effect on other cognitive functions.

    “Working memory capacity was negatively impacted by the mere presence of a smartphone, while results for the remaining cognitive functions produced null summary effects.”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Parry, Media Psychology.

  3. Participants with higher positive urgency showed more cognitive interference in a silent-mode smartphone condition compared to a turned-off smartphone condition.

    “The results showed that participants with higher positive urgency presented increased cognitive interference (reflected by poorer task performance) in the "silent-mode smartphone" condition compared with participants in the "turned-off smartphone" condition.”

    Experiment, 2019. Canale et al., Scientific Reports.

  4. The cognitive costs of a smartphone's presence are highest for people who have a high level of smartphone dependence.

    “Moreover, these cognitive costs are highest for those highest in smartphone dependence.”

    Experiment, 2017. Ward et al., Journal of the Association for Consumer Research.

  5. In an experiment with participants aged 20-34, the mere presence of a smartphone results in lower cognitive performance.

    “The results of the conducted experiment imply that the mere presence of a smartphone results in lower cognitive performance, which supports the hypothesis of the smartphone presence using limited cognitive resources.”

    Experiment, 2023. Skowronek et al., Scientific Reports.

The cost of proximity.

A smartphone nearby can drain your working memory. This effect is most pronounced for people with a high level of smartphone dependence. For others, the impact may be negligible.

The invisible drain.

That may be why you feel less focused when your phone is on the desk. Your brain may be constantly calculating the possibility of a notification.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The desk on your table.

You sit down to write a report. Your phone sits face down next to your laptop. You feel a slight tension in your focus as you work.

The honest summary

The evidence is mixed, with some studies showing a 'brain drain' effect on working memory and cognitive capacity while others find no statistically significant impact.

The research is characterized by substantial methodological heterogeneity and generally poor statistical power.
