Does your career actually age your cells?

File no. 0247November 7, 2026Money and power

Job strain does not accelerate your cellular aging.

Research shows no significant differences in telomere length across occupational categories or stress levels.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. There were no mean differences in telomere lengths across current employment status, occupational category, job strain categories or levels of most O*NET exposure measures.

    “There were no mean differences in telomere lengths across current employment status, occupational category, job strain categories or levels of most O*NET exposure measures.”

    Study, 2013. Fujishiro et al., Occupational and Environmental Medicine.

  2. There was no evidence that being in lower status occupational categories or being exposed to higher levels of adverse physical or psychosocial exposures accelerated the association between age and telomere shortening.

    “There was also no evidence that being in lower status occupational categories or being exposed to higher levels of adverse physical or psychosocial exposures accelerated the association between age and telomere shortening.”

    Study, 2013. Fujishiro et al., Occupational and Environmental Medicine.

  3. The overall correlation between oxidative stress markers and telomere dynamics was indistinguishable from zero (r = 0.027).

    “The overall correlation between oxidative stress markers and telomere dynamics was indistinguishable from zero (r = 0. 027).”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Armstrong & Boonekamp, Ageing Research Reviews.

The link between work and aging is missing.

Studies found no evidence that lower status or adverse psychosocial exposures accelerate telomere shortening. The correlation between oxidative stress markers and telomere dynamics was indistinguishable from zero.

The narrative of the broken executive is unproven.

That may be why the physical toll of your career is often more of a social construct than a biological certainty.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence fails to support the idea that high-powered leadership or job strain causes rapid biological aging, as studies found no significant differences in telomere length across various occupational categories or stress levels.

The lack of a coherent relationship in some studies may be due to differences in exposure levels, dose rates, and the specific models used.
