Does your brain prioritize your energy?

File no. 0273November 12, 2026The human brain

Complex communication does not cost more energy.

The metabolic cost of complex bird song is no greater than simpler songs. The evidence on whether personality is tied to metabolic efficiency is mixed or contested.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The metabolic cost of song production in the canary, which has the temporally most complex song, was no greater than in the other two species studied.

    “Surprisingly, the metabolic cost of song production in the species with the temporally most complex song, the canary, was no greater than in the other two species.”

    Study, 2001. Oberweger & Goller, Journal of Experimental Biology.

  2. Taking steps of equal length did not minimize metabolic cost or mechanical cost when walking on a split-belt treadmill.

    “We found that taking steps of equal length did not minimize metabolic cost or mechanical cost.”

    Study, 2017. Sánchez et al., Scientific Reports.

  3. Symmetry was identified as the optimal strategy when energetic cost was estimated from a composite metric that combined both metabolic and mechanical costs.

    “However, symmetry was identified as the optimal strategy when energetic cost was estimated from a composite metric that combined both metabolic and mechanical costs.”

    Study, 2017. Sánchez et al., Scientific Reports.

  4. The metabolic cost of singing is similar to that of calling in frogs and of human speech production.

    “Our analysis shows that the metabolic cost of singing is also similar to that of calling in frogs and of human speech production.”

    Study, 2001. Oberweger & Goller, Journal of Experimental Biology.

Energy and behavior are not always linked.

While symmetry may optimize for a composite of metabolic and mechanical costs, complex behaviors like bird song or human speech do not show a corresponding increase in metabolic cost.

Your habits may not be about efficiency.

That may be why you engage in complex behaviors that seem demanding. Your brain may not be prioritizing energy savings for every action you take.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The way you speak to a friend.

You choose complex words and patterns in a late night conversation. It feels like effort, but your body might not be paying the price you think it is.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while some physical movements optimize for energy, complex communicative behaviors do not always show a corresponding increase in metabolic cost.

The sources do not provide direct evidence linking these metabolic costs to specific personality traits.
