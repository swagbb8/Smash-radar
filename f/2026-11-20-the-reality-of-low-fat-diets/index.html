Does low-fat dairy really cause weight gain?

File no. 0307November 20, 2026Health and environment

Low-fat diets with high fiber and protein are supported for weight management.

The evidence does not link low-fat dairy to weight gain. Instead, it supports a diet reduced in fat and sugar-rich beverages, with more fiber-rich carbohydrates and protein.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. The division of nutrients into total fat versus carbohydrate appears to be less helpful in predicting outcomes.

    “The division of nutrients into total fat (regardless of fatty acids) versus carbohydrate (type and quantity not specified) appears to be less helpful in predicting outcomes.”

    Review, 2003. Hung et al., Current Opinion in Clinical Nutrition & Metabolic Care.

  2. Diets higher in monounsaturated fatty acids, fiber, and low glycemic index foods appear to have advantages in insulin resistance, glycemic control and blood lipids in a number of studies.

    “Diets that are higher in monounsaturated fatty acids, fiber and low glycemic index foods appear to have advantages in insulin resistance, glycemic control and blood lipids in a number of studies.”

    Review, 2003. Hung et al., Current Opinion in Clinical Nutrition & Metabolic Care.

  3. The current evidence strongly supports a diet with reduced content of fat and sugar-rich beverages, and more carbohydrates rich in fibre and grain (whole grain foods, fruit and vegetables) and protein (lean meat and dairy products) as the best choice for the prevention of weight gain, obesity, type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

    “The current evidence strongly supports a diet with reduced content of fat and sugar-rich beverages, and more carbohydrates rich in fibre and grain (whole grain foods, fruit and vegetables) and protein (lean meat and dairy products) as the best choice for the prevention of weight gain, obesity, type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.”

    Meta-analysis, 2003. Astrup, Research at the University of Copenhagen (University of Copenhagen).

  4. The evidence linking particular fatty acids to body fatness is weak.

    “The evidence linking particular fatty acids to body fatness is weak.”

    Meta-analysis, 2003. Astrup, Research at the University of Copenhagen (University of Copenhagen).

  5. All hypocaloric regimens are suitable, as long as they work, for any given subject.

    “All hypocaloric regimens are suitable, as long as they work, for any given subject.”

    Review, 2010. Dubnov‐Raz & Berry, Mount Sinai Journal of Medicine A Journal of Translational and Personalized Medicine.

The evidence is mixed on the best diet.

Research shows that dividing nutrients into total fat versus carbohydrate is not helpful for predicting outcomes. The best diet for weight loss is not yet identified and may need to be prescribed on an individual basis.

Your choices may depend on your individual needs.

That may be why looking at fat content alone is insufficient. Your specific metabolic needs might require a personalized approach rather than a universal rule.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The sources do not provide evidence that low-fat dairy products specifically cause weight gain; instead, they suggest that a low-fat diet with high fiber and protein is a supported strategy for weight management.

The research highlights that the 'best diet' for weight loss is not yet identified and may need to be prescribed on an individual basis.
