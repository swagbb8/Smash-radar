Does your bank account change your kindness?

File no. 0244November 7, 2026Modern society

Wealth is consistently linked to prosociality.

Research across many countries shows a positive association between wealth and prosocial preferences and behaviors.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. There is a consistent positive association between wealth and prosociality, across both measures and for both financial and nonfinancial prosocial preferences and behaviors.

    “We show a consistent positive association between wealth and prosociality, across both measures and for both financial and nonfinancial prosocial preferences and behaviors.”

    Study, 2025. Vanags et al., PNAS Nexus.

  2. Individuals' experience of precarity reduced the strength of wealth associations for prosocial preferences but increased them for prosocial behaviors.

    “Individuals' experience of precarity reduced the strength of wealth associations for prosocial preferences but increased them for prosocial behaviors.”

    Study, 2025. Vanags et al., PNAS Nexus.

  3. Mothers who believed that they had more than others in their village had children who were more likely to engage in instrumental helping.

    “subjective beliefs showed that mothers who believed that they had more than others in their village had children who were more likely to engage in instrumental helping”

    Experiment, 2018. Chernyak et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

  4. HHI was positively associated with altruism, positive reciprocity, donating money, volunteering, and helping a stranger, but negatively associated with trust.

    “HHI was positively associated with altruism, positive reciprocity, donating money, volunteering, and helping a stranger, but negatively associated with trust.”

    Study, 2025. Vanags et al., PNAS Nexus.

  5. Objective family wealth did not predict any of the child’s prosocial behaviors.

    “Objective family wealth did not predict any of the child’s prosocial behaviors.”

    Experiment, 2018. Chernyak et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

The evidence is mixed.

While wealth correlates with helping others, objective family wealth did not predict a child's prosocial behaviors. The studies do not say if these acts occur in secret.

Wealth shapes how you see others.

That may be why individuals with more resources are more likely to volunteer or donate. It suggests your financial status influences your willingness to help.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A quiet donation.

You see a person leave a bag of groceries on a porch in the rain. They walk away before anyone notices them.

The honest summary

The evidence contradicts the proposed angle by showing a consistent positive association between wealth and prosociality across various measures and countries.

The studies do not provide specific data on whether these prosocial behaviors occur in secret or anonymous settings.
