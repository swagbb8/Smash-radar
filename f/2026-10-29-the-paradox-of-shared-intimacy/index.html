Are your secrets making you lonely?

File no. 0195October 29, 2026Human relationships

Sharing deeper information with friends can reduce attraction.

Increasing the depth of information in a social network profile reduces interpersonal attraction. This finding is mixed as it does not measure subjective well-being.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Increasing the depth of information shared in a social network profile reduced interpersonal attraction.

    “Analyses indicated that, while increasing the breadth of information had a positive impact on interpersonal attraction to profile owners, increasing the depth of information reduced attraction.”

    Experiment, 2018. Baruh & Cemalcılar, Cyberpsychology Journal of Psychosocial Research on Cyberspace.

  2. Increasing the depth of information shared in a social network profile enhanced attributional confidence only when the breadth of information shared was low.

    “Additionally, there was a significant interaction between breadth and depth of information in predicting attributional confidence; increasing the depth of information shared in an SNS profile enhanced attributional confidence only when the breadth of information shared was low.”

    Experiment, 2018. Baruh & Cemalcılar, Cyberpsychology Journal of Psychosocial Research on Cyberspace.

  3. Increasing the breadth of information shared in a social network profile increased interpersonal attraction.

    “Analyses indicated that, higher breadth of information shared in the profile increased interpersonal attraction and that attributional confidence mediated this relationship.”

    Experiment, 2018. Baruh & Cemalcılar, Cyberpsychology Journal of Psychosocial Research on Cyberspace.

  4. Feelings of being alone were correlated with the frequency of talking to oneself and using the TV for company.

    “Study 1 ( N = 240) found that ratings of how alone participants felt were correlated with frequency of talking to themselves and using the TV for company.”

    Study, 2008. Jonason et al., Evolutionary Psychology.

  5. Loneliness was correlated with talking to oneself and singing to oneself.

    “Study 2 ( N = 66) was a daily diary study where loneliness was correlated with both Study 1 behaviors and singing to oneself.”

    Study, 2008. Jonason et al., Evolutionary Psychology.

Breadth attracts. Depth repels.

Sharing a wide range of information increases interpersonal attraction. However, increasing the depth of that information reduces it. Confidence in your character only improves when the breadth of information is low.

You may be pushing people away.

That may be why sharing every intimate detail of your inner life feels isolating. You might be trading connection for exposure, creating a barrier where there should be a bridge.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The late night scroll.

You sit in a dark room. You scroll through a friend's feed. You see their deepest secrets and feel a sudden distance instead of closeness. You put the phone down and feel alone.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that social network profile depth can decrease interpersonal attraction and that loneliness is linked to behaviors that trick the brain into feeling socially interactive.

The studies on social network profiles do not measure subjective well-being or the specific impact of high-frequency, low-depth interactions with friends.
