Why does kindness sometimes feel like a burden?

File no. 0166October 24, 2026Human relationships

Social friction comes from perceived shirking.

People are moved by honor and altruism to contribute to public goods, but they respond to being taken advantage of with resentment and retaliation.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. Second movers' behavior is characterized by positive reciprocity but not by significant negative reciprocity.

    “Furthermore, the first movers' behavior is based on rational expectations because the second movers' behavior is characterized by positive reciprocity but not by significant negative reciprocity.”

    Experiment, 2001. Cox et al., UvA-DARE (University of Amsterdam).

  2. First movers in the moonlighting game behave based on trust in positive reciprocity and an absence of fear of negative reciprocity.

    “Data from the experiment support the conclusion that first movers' behavior in the moonlighting game is characterized by trust in positive reciprocity and an absence of fear of negative reciprocity.”

    Experiment, 2001. Cox et al., UvA-DARE (University of Amsterdam).

  3. When people see others behaving cooperatively, they are moved by honor and altruism to contribute to public goods without material incentives.

    “When they perceive that others are behaving cooperatively, individuals are moved by honor, altruism, and like dispositions to contribute to public goods even without the inducement of material incentives.”

    Study, 2003. Kahan, Michigan Law Review.

  4. People often are altruistic, trust others, and reciprocate the benevolence of others far more than economic models of rational human selfishness predict.

    “Interest began with psychological experiments showing that people often are altruistic, trust others, and reciprocate the benevolence of others far more than economic models of "rational" human selfishness predict.”

    Experiment, 2005. Dent, Case Western Reserve University School of Law Scholarly Commons (Case Western Reserve University).

  5. When people see others taking advantage of them, they are moved by resentment and pride to withhold cooperation and engage in retaliation.

    “When, in contrast, they perceive that others are shirking or otherwise taking advantage of them, individuals are moved by resentment and pride to withhold their own cooperation and even to engage in personally costly forms of retaliation.”

    Study, 2003. Kahan, Michigan Law Review.

Altruism is not the primary source of tension.

Humans are naturally more altruistic and trusting than economic models predict. Conflict arises when people see others withholding cooperation or taking advantage of them. This triggers resentment and a desire for retaliation.

Reciprocity defines your social standing.

That may be why you feel a sense of unease when someone accepts your help without contributing back. Social cohesion depends on the perception of mutual cooperation.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A group project at the office.

You finish your tasks and notice a coworker doing nothing. You feel a sudden shift from cooperation to a desire to withhold your help.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while people are often naturally altruistic and trust positive reciprocity, social friction arises from perceived shirking rather than the act of being an over-benefactor.

The sources do not provide specific data on the 'over-benefactor' effect or the specific perception of debt caused by excessive altruism.
