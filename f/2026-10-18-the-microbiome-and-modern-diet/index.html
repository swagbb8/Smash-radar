What is your food doing to your microbes?

File no. 0115October 18, 2026The future

Ultra-processed foods alter your microbial community.

These foods are linked to persistent inflammation and chronic disorders. The evidence is mixed regarding the specific markers affected.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The choice of control diet has a significant impact on outcomes and interpretation related to diet effects on gut microbiota.

    “Collectively, our results show that the choice of control diet has a significant impact on outcomes and interpretation related to diet effects on gut microbiota.”

    Experiment, 2020. Morrison et al., Microbiome.

  2. A meta-analysis found that dietary fiber significantly increased the relative abundance of Bifidobacterium and significantly decreased LPS, total cholesterol, and BMI as compared with the control.

    “DF significantly (p < 0. 05) increased the relative abundance of Bifidobacterium and significantly decreased (p < 0. 05) LPS, total cholesterol, and BMI as compared with the control.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Ojo et al., Nutrients.

  3. In a study of rats, a dose-dependent relationship was observed between increased fiber intake and a decrease in alveolar bone loss and pro-inflammatory markers.

    “A dose-dependent relationship between the increased fiber intake and decrease in alveolar bone loss and pro-inflammatory markers was observed.”

    Systematic review, 2023. Tailor et al., Frontiers in Nutrition.

  4. Overall microbiome composition is linked to a large panel of cardiometabolic blood markers including fasting and postprandial glycemic, lipemic and inflammatory indices.

    “overall microbiome composition was predictive for a large panel of cardiometabolic blood markers including fasting and postprandial glycemic, lipemic and inflammatory indices.”

    Clinical trial, 2021. Asnicar et al., Nature medicine.

  5. Ultra-processed foods are linked to alterations in the microbial community that contribute to persistent inflammation associated with various chronic disorders.

    “These alterations in the microbial community contribute to persistent inflammation, which is associated with various chronic disorders including metabolic syndrome, irritable bowel syndrome, type 2 diabetes, and colorectal cancer.”

    Study, 2025. Rondinella et al., Nutrients.

Fiber changes your internal biology.

Dietary fiber is linked to increased Bifidobacterium and decreased LPS and cholesterol. In some models, it also correlates with reduced pro-inflammatory markers.

Your diet shapes your internal environment.

The choice of control diet changes how we interpret these effects. That may be why your current food choices correlate with your cardiometabolic blood markers.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The convenience of a packaged meal.

You reach for a shelf-stable snack during a busy afternoon. It is engineered for shelf life, not your microbiome.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that ultra-processed foods are linked to gut dysbiosis and inflammation, while dietary fiber interventions can improve specific microbial markers and metabolic outcomes.

Many studies are limited in scope, such as the animal models used for periodontitis or the lack of significant effects on several specific inflammatory markers in the meta-analysis.
