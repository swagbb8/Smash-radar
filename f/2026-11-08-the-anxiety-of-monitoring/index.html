Does your watch know your anxiety better than you do?

File no. 0253November 8, 2026Technology

Constant biometric monitoring is linked to technohypochondria.

This is a specific anxiety framework driven by the continuous stream of data from wearable devices. It differs from seeking health information online. This finding is currently theoretical and awaits future empirical validation.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Constant biometric monitoring from wearable devices is linked to a specific anxiety framework called technohypochondria.

    “the pathological reliance on these metrics represents an emerging, yet empirically unexamined, digital anxiety framework.”

    Study, 2026. Durmus, Healthcare (Basel, Switzerland).

  2. Technohypochondria is different from cyberchondria because it is driven by constant biometric monitoring rather than seeking health information online.

    “Technohypochondria operationalizes a specific anxiety framework driven by constant biometric monitoring, conceptually diverging from cyberchondria's focus on online health-information seeking.”

    Study, 2026. Durmus, Healthcare (Basel, Switzerland).

  3. Wearable devices have the clinical potential to detect anxiety disorders using biosignals like electrocardiography.

    “This review underscores the clinical potential of wearable devices for anxiety detection, emphasizing the value of multimodal approaches.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Elgendi et al., Communications Medicine.

  4. Wearable sensors can be used to collect biomarkers that may correlate with levels of elevated stress.

    “Wearable sensors have shown promise as a non-intrusive method for collecting biomarkers that may correlate with levels of elevated stress.”

    Review, 2023. Vos et al., International Journal of Medical Informatics.

  5. Information gathered by monitoring physiological, behavioural, and social aspects of our lives can be used to achieve a positive impact on quality of life, health, and well-being.

    “Information gathered by monitoring physiological, behavioural, and social aspects of our lives, can be used to achieve a positive impact on quality of life, health, and well-being.”

    Review, 2017. King et al., Medical Engineering & Physics.

  6. Medical grade wearables will play an increasingly central role in medicine by providing continuous, cost-effective measurement and interpretation of physiological data.

    “Medical grade wearables-noninvasive, on-body sensors operating with clinical accuracy-will play an increasingly central role in medicine by providing continuous, cost-effective measurement and interpretation of physiological data relevant to patient status and disease trajectory”

    Review, 2022. Xu et al., Science Translational Medicine.

The dual nature of wearable data.

Wearables have the clinical potential to detect anxiety disorders and measure stress biomarkers. They can improve your quality of life, but they also provide constant access to your internal physiological data.

A shift in how you feel your body.

That may be why your pulse becomes a source of worry rather than just a metric. You are moving from living in your body to monitoring it from the outside.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A notification at 3 a.m.

You wake up in a dark room. Your wrist vibrates. You look at the heart rate graph and wonder why it is elevated while you are still asleep.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that wearables can be used for positive health monitoring and anxiety detection, but they also create a new framework for anxiety driven by constant data access.

The study identifying technohypochondria is theoretical and awaits future empirical validation and scale development.
