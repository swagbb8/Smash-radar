File no. 0274November 12, 2026The human brain

Your brain constructs reality by minimizing errors.

The cerebral cortex acts as a generative model that predicts sensory data to create your perception of the world.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The available volumetric evidence does not support a consistent pattern of brain abnormalities following COVID-19.

    “the available volumetric evidence does not support a consistent pattern.”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Costa et al., Neuroscience and biobehavioral reviews.

  2. The brain uses a Bayesian minimization of the error between the input to the brain and the brain's prior expectations to manifest perception and learning.

    “perception and learning is manifested through the brain's Bayesian minimization of the error between the input to the brain and the brain's prior expectations.”

    Review, 2014. Vuust & Witek, Frontiers in Psychology.

  3. The cerebral cortex is interpreted as a Bayesian, generative model capable of predicting the sensory data consistent with any given percept.

    “interpret the hierarchical construction of the cerebral cortex as a Bayesian, generative model capable of predicting the sensory data consistent with any given percept.”

    Review, 2016. Shipp, Frontiers in Psychology.

  4. The frontal cortex encodes prediction errors that mediate endogenous perceptual transitions in bistable perception.

    “suggesting that frontal cortex encodes prediction errors that mediate endogenous perceptual transitions in bistable perception.”

    Experiment, 2017. Weilnhammer et al., PLoS Computational Biology.

  5. Rhythm perception is conceptualized as an interaction between what is heard ("rhythm") and the brain's anticipatory structuring of music ("meter").

    “rhythm perception is conceptualized as an interaction between what is heard ("rhythm") and the brain's anticipatory structuring of music ("meter").”

    Review, 2014. Vuust & Witek, Frontiers in Psychology.

Perception is a constant calculation.

Your brain compares input against prior expectations. It uses a Bayesian minimization of the error between what it expects and what it actually receives.

You live in a model, not the world.

That may be why your experiences feel consistent. Your brain filters the world to match what it already thinks should happen.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The rhythm of a city street.

You hear the distant beat of traffic and footsteps. Your brain structures these sounds into a predictable meter before you even notice the pattern.

The honest summary

The sources provide a theoretical framework for how the brain uses prediction and error signaling to process sensory input like vision, music, and rhythm.

The sources identify several unresolved questions regarding the specific nature of predictions and the correlates of perceptual experience within the predictive coding framework.
