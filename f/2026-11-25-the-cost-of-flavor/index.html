Is your spice rack linked to biodiversity?

File no. 0333November 25, 2026History

Biodiversity is inextricably linked with spice production.

The health of an ecosystem is tied to how we produce spices. However, the evidence does not show the historical impact of maritime trade or tropical degradation.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The drivers of forest fragmentation in China are shifting from mainly agricultural expansion to urbanisation and infrastructure development.

    “Analyses of temporal trends show that the drivers of forest fragmentation are shifting from mainly agricultural expansion to urbanisation and infrastructure development.”

    Review, 2019. Liu et al., Biological reviews/Biological reviews of the Cambridge Philosophical Society.

  2. Mixed-species plantations have a higher arthropod biodiversity value than previously indicated by bees alone.

    “Together, these results broadly corroborate our previous conclusions on birds and bees but show a higher arthropod biodiversity value of mixed‐species plantations than previously indicated by bees alone.”

    Study, 2019. Wang et al., Diversity and Distributions.

  3. Mixed-species stands of spruce and birch or spruce and Scots pine are linked to increased biodiversity and reduced stand vulnerability to pest and pathogen damage.

    “By synthesizing the available evidence, we identify positive outcomes from mixtures including increased biodiversity, water quality, esthetic and recreational values, as well as reduced stand vulnerability to pest and pathogen damage.”

    Study, 2016. Felton et al., AMBIO. This shows a link, not a cause.

  4. Mixed forests appear to provide a higher output of most ecosystem goods and services, including higher biodiversity and improved risk management.

    “Based on this review, mixed forests appear to provide a higher output of most ecosystem goods and services, including higher biodiversity and improved risk management, soil properties and multiple-use values.”

    Study, 2020. Huuskonen et al., Forest Ecology and Management.

  5. Biodiversity and ecosystem services are inextricably linked with the spice production.

    “Biodiversity and ecosystem services are inextricably linked with the spice production.”

    Review, 2025. Sil et al., PLANT ARCHIVES.

Mixed stands provide more services.

Mixed-species plantations have higher arthropod biodiversity than those focused on single species. These diverse stands provide a higher output of ecosystem goods and services.

The structure of nature matters.

That may be why monocultures are less resilient. When we simplify nature to favor a single crop, we may be reducing the very services the land provides.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A spice jar on a kitchen shelf.

You reach for a simple seasoning to flavor your dinner. You do not see the complex web of life required to sustain that single ingredient.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that monocultures are less biodiverse than mixed-species stands and that biodiversity is linked to spice production, but it lacks specific data on historical trade impacts.

None of the provided sources contain data on historical maritime trade or the specific ecological degradation of tropical biomes.
