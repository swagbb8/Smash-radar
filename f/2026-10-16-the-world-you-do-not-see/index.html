What did you miss in the last hour?

File no. 0100October 16, 2026The human brain

You do not notice large changes in your visual field.

People often fail to notice significant changes in visual scenes, even when those changes happen right in front of their eyes.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Change blindness can occur even in the absence of a visual disruption.

    “In two experiments, we demonstrate that change blindness can occur even in the absence of a visual disruption.”

    Experiment, 2000. Simons et al., Perception.

  2. Subjects seldom noticed the switch [of pictures during a choice procedure], and in a post-test memory task, they also often remembered the manipulated choice as being their own.

    “Surprisingly, the subjects seldom noticed the switch, and in a post-test memory task, they also often remembered the manipulated choice as being their own.”

    Experiment, 2008. Johansson et al., PSYCHOLOGIA.

  3. When changes are sufficiently gradual, the visible change signal does not seem to draw attention, and large changes can go undetected.

    “When changes are sufficiently gradual, the visible change signal does not seem to draw attention, and large changes can go undetected.”

    Experiment, 2000. Simons et al., Perception.

  4. People often fail to notice significant changes in visual scenes, even when these changes happen right in front of their eyes.

    “A large body of literature has shown that observers often fail to notice significant changes in visual scenes, even when these changes happen right in front of their eyes.”

    Experiment, 2009. Brady et al., Communicative & Integrative Biology.

  5. One of the major causes of change blindness for real-world objects is a lack of encoding time or attention to each object.

    “The results reported here suggest that one of the major causes of change blindness for real-world objects is a lack of encoding time or attention to each object”

    Experiment, 2009. Brady et al., Communicative & Integrative Biology.

  6. Increasing attention, the saliency of the changed object and spatial violations significantly reduce CB, specifically when measured using the real-world and film paradigms.

    “Increasing attention, the saliency of the changed object and spatial violations significantly reduce CB, specifically when measured using the real-world and film paradigms”

    Systematic review, 2016. Gibbs et al., Crime Psychology Review.

Your brain lacks the time to encode every object.

A major cause of change blindness is a lack of encoding time or attention to each object in your immediate environment.

The world moves without your consent.

That may be why you miss significant shifts in your surroundings. Your brain fills in the gaps, letting large changes go undetected.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A new chair in a busy office.

You walk into a room where a piece of furniture has been moved. You see the room, but you do not see the change. Your brain treats the space as it was before. It assumes the environment is stable. You do not register the shift because your attention is elsewhere. You move through the day based on a mental map that is slightly out of date. The physical world is changing, but your perception remains fixed on the past version of the room.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that people frequently fail to notice significant changes in scenes, though these failures can be mitigated by increased attention or saliency.

The research identifies various factors like encoding time, saliency, and the speed of change as critical variables that influence whether a change is actually perceived.
