Is your memory a recording or a reconstruction?

File no. 0192October 28, 2026The human brain

You can abolish a stable memory.

Interrupting the process of reconsolidation can remove a memory that was previously stable.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Human declarative memory can be selectively rewritten during reconsolidation.

    “These results demonstrate that human declarative memory can be selectively rewritten during reconsolidation.”

    Experiment, 2013. Chan & LaPaglia, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  2. Interrupting the reconsolidation process can abolish a previously stable memory.

    “Critically, interrupting this reconsolidation process can abolish a previously stable memory.”

    Experiment, 2013. Chan & LaPaglia, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  3. Extinction during reconsolidation prevents the return of defensive reactions and diminishes PFC involvement.

    “These results suggest that extinction during reconsolidation prevents the return of defensive reactions and diminishes PFC involvement.”

    Study, 2013. Schiller et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  4. Videos that were more surprising when interrupted produced more false memories.

    “We found that videos that were more surprising when interrupted produced more false memories.”

    Preprint, not peer reviewed, 2018. Sinclair & Barense, Learning & Memory.

  5. Individuals with PTSD, a history of trauma, or depression are at risk for producing false memories when they are exposed to information that is related to their knowledge base.

    “Our review suggests that individuals with PTSD, a history of trauma, or depression are at risk for producing false memories when they are exposed to information that is related to their knowledge base.”

    Review, 2017. Otgaar et al., Clinical Psychological Science.

  6. Perception and memory are imperfect reconstructions of reality that can be influenced by several factors resulting in false memories.

    “Perception and memory are imperfect reconstructions of reality. These reconstructions are prone to be influenced by several factors, which may result in false memories.”

    Review, 2012. Straube, Behavioral and Brain Functions.

Memory is a reconstruction.

Human memories are not static recordings. They are dynamic reconstructions. They can be rewritten, abolished, or distorted during a process called reconsolidation.

Your history is fluid.

That may be why your past feels different each time you recall it. Your brain actively alters the information during the act of remembering.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A childhood kitchen.

You remember the smell of toast and the color of the cabinets. Each time you think of it, your brain redraws the scene.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that memories are labile reconstructions that can be actively altered or impaired during retrieval and reconsolidation.

The sources do not provide a comprehensive view of all memory stages, as most studies focus specifically on either encoding or retrieval.
