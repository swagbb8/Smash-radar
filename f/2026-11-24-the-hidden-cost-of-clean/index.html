Does your cleaning routine irritate your lungs?

File no. 0328November 24, 2026Health and environment

Your cleaning products cause symptoms without changing your lung function.

Experimental data shows that volatile organic compounds (VOCs) cause dose-related respiratory symptoms, but they do not significantly change lung function or biomarkers of airway inflammation.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. VOC exposures caused dose-related increases in lower respiratory, upper respiratory, and non-respiratory symptoms, with no significant change in lung function.

    “VOC exposures caused dose-related increases in lower respiratory, upper respiratory, and non-respiratory symptoms, with no significant change in lung function (FEV(1);, FVC, or FEF(25-75), nasal lavage cellularity or differential cell counts, induced sputum cellularity or differential cell counts, or biomarkers of airway inflammation, including IL-8, LTB(4), or albumin in nasal lavage or induced sputum samples.”

    Randomized trial, 2000. Pappas et al., International Journal of Occupational and Environmental Health.

  2. A meta-analysis suggests that VOCs have a medium-sized effect on the onset of asthma with an effect size of ~0.37.

    “Our analysis suggests that VOCs have a medium-sized effect on pulmonary diseases, including the onset of asthma (effect size (or Cohen’s d) ~0. 37; 95% confidence interval (CI) = 0. 25–0. 49; n = 23)”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Alford & Kumar, International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

  3. A meta-analysis suggests that VOCs have a medium-sized effect on wheezing with an effect size of ~0.26.

    “and wheezing (effective size ~0. 26; 95% CI = 0. 10–0. 42; n = 10).”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Alford & Kumar, International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

  4. Atopic individuals had significantly reduced FEE(25-75) following exposure to VOCs at 50 mg/m(3).

    “Atopic individuals had significantly reduced FEE(25-75) following exposure to VOCs at 50 mg/m(3), suggesting that these individuals may be more sensitive to the health effects of VOCs.”

    Randomized trial, 2000. Pappas et al., International Journal of Occupational and Environmental Health.

  5. Prolonged exposure to VOCs has been associated with respiratory irritation, neurological effects, and an increased risk of chronic diseases.

    “Prolonged exposure to VOCs has been associated with respiratory irritation, neurological effects, and an increased risk of chronic diseases.”

    Review, 2025. Horvat et al., Toxics.

The evidence on VOCs is mixed.

Research shows VOCs are linked to asthma onset and wheezing. However, these exposures do not always result in measurable changes to lung function or specific inflammation biomarkers. Results vary by age, country, and individual sensitivity.

Your irritation might be a subclinical response.

That may be why you feel a persistent tightness or irritation in your throat and nose. The evidence suggests your body reacts to these chemicals even when standard tests show no damage.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that VOCs are associated with respiratory symptoms and asthma onset, but it also shows that these exposures do not necessarily result in measurable changes to lung function or specific inflammation biomarkers.

The studies show varying results based on country, age, disease type, and individual sensitivity, such as in atopic individuals.
