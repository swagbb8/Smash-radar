What does your biology say about colonial borders?

File no. 0132October 20, 2026History

Colonialism left a genetic mark on southern African mammals.

The two southernmost populations of cape buffalo have significantly lower heterozygosity levels of 0.33% and 0.29% compared to the rest of the population. The evidence regarding how arbitrary borders specifically shaped national genetic landscapes is mixed or contested.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Religion, a history of colonialism, and a history of Communist rule are important in some cases regarding the worldwide patterns of cognitive test performance.

    “Religion, a history of colonialism, and a history of Communist rule are important in some cases.”

    Study, 2003. Meisenberg, Mankind Quarterly.

  2. Events across the past three centuries were the main drivers of the exceptional loss of genetic diversity in the southernmost populations of Cape buffalo, which coincided with the onset of colonialism.

    “detailed investigations of recent demographic history show that events across the past three centuries were the main drivers of the exceptional loss of genetic diversity in the southernmost populations, coincident with the onset of colonialism in the southern extreme of the Cape buffalo range.”

    Study, 2023. Quinn et al., Molecular Ecology.

  3. Genetic data alone can be used to identify historical mixture events, including those caused by European colonialism.

    “We identified events whose dates and participants suggest they describe genetic impacts of the Mongol empire, Arab slave trade, Bantu expansion, first millennium CE migrations in Eastern Europe, and European colonialism”

    Study, 2014. Hellenthal et al., Science (New York, N.Y.). This shows a link, not a cause.

  4. The two southernmost populations of Cape buffalo have significantly lower heterozygosity levels of 0.33% and 0.29% compared to the rest of the population.

    “with the two southernmost populations having significantly lower heterozygosity levels (0. 33% and 0. 29%) on par with that of the domesticated water buffalo (0. 29%).”

    Study, 2023. Quinn et al., Molecular Ecology.

  5. Human-mediated impacts during the colonial period caused massive losses of large mammal abundance in southern Africa.

    “our results add to the growing body of studies suggesting that multiple recent human-mediated impacts during the colonial period caused massive losses of large mammal abundance in southern Africa.”

    Study, 2023. Quinn et al., Molecular Ecology. This shows a link, not a cause.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that colonial-era human impacts caused specific genetic diversity losses in southern African mammals and are detectable in human admixture history, but it does not provide a broad analysis of national border effects.

The sources focus on specific species, regions, or historical events rather than providing a systematic analysis of how arbitrary colonial borders specifically defined the genetic landscapes of modern nations.
