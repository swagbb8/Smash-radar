Does a countdown timer always make you buy?

File no. 0119October 18, 2026Money and power

The countdown timer does not always work.

A limited-time notice does not directly generate higher purchase intentions compared to a non-limited-time notice. The evidence is mixed.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Perceived green washing mediates the negative effect of product scarcity appeals on green product purchase intentions.

    “Perceived green washing mediates the negative effect of product scarcity appeals on green product purchase intentions.”

    Experiment, 2024. Sheng-hong et al., Frontiers in Psychology.

  2. Scarcity and social comparison did not exhibit significant direct or moderating influence on luxury purchase intention.

    “In contrast, scarcity and social comparison did not exhibit significant direct or moderating influence.”

    Experiment, 2025. Abdrabbo et al., Discover Sustainability.

  3. A limited-time availability notice does not directly generate higher purchase intention compared to a non-limited-time notice.

    “The results demonstrate that a limited-time notice does not directly generate higher purchase intention compared to a non-limited-time notice.”

    Experiment, 2022. Broeder & Wentink, The International Review of Retail Distribution and Consumer Research.

  4. A limited-time availability notice causes higher perceived scarcity and subsequently activates higher perceived competition, which, in turn, leads to higher purchase intention for Italian consumers.

    “Specifically, a limited-time availability notice creates higher perceived scarcity and subsequently activates higher perceived competition, which, in turn, leads to higher purchase intention. However, this indirect effect on purchase intention emerged only for the Italian consumers”

    Experiment, 2022. Broeder & Wentink, The International Review of Retail Distribution and Consumer Research.

  5. Time-based scarcity is most effective for high involvement products.

    “Results show that demand-based scarcity is most effective for utilitarian products, supply-based scarcity for experiences, and time-based scarcity for high involvement products.”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Barton et al., Journal of Retailing.

Scarcity is context dependent.

The effect of time-based scarcity depends on the product type, your cultural background, and the market context. It can lead to negative responses for green products or show no influence in luxury markets.

The tactic is not universal.

That may be why you feel a sense of urgency in some stores but feel indifferent to the same pressure in others. The manipulation relies on specific triggers rather than a single rule.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A red clock on a website.

You watch the seconds tick down on a checkout page while considering an eco-friendly soap or a high-end watch.

The honest summary

The effectiveness of scarcity cues is highly dependent on the product type, the consumer's cultural background, and the specific market context.

The research shows that scarcity tactics do not have a universal effect and can even lead to negative consumer responses depending on the product category.
