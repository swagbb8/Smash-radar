The uncomfortable truth

How fast do you judge a stranger?

File no. 0001October 4, 2026Psychology

You judge a person in 100 milliseconds.

First impressions of traits from faces occur in as little as 100 ms. These judgments correlate with those made without time limits.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Inferences of competence based solely on facial appearance predicted the outcomes of U.S. congressional elections better than chance (e.g., 68.8% of the Senate races in 2004).

    “We show that inferences of competence based solely on facial appearance predicted the outcomes of U.S. congressional elections better than chance (e.g., 68.8% of the Senate races in 2004)”

    Study, 2005. Todorov et al., Science.

  2. Once learned, first impressions occur following very brief exposure (100 ms).

    “Crucially, in the final two experiments (Experiments 4 and 5) we show that, once learned, these first impressions occur following very brief exposure (100 ms).”

    Experiment, 2021. Lee et al., Scientific Reports.

  3. Judgments made after a 100-ms exposure to a face correlated highly with judgments made in the absence of time constraints.

    “Judgments made after a 100-ms exposure correlated highly with judgments made in the absence of time constraints, suggesting that this exposure time was sufficient for participants to form an impression.”

    Experiment, 2006. Willis & Todorov, Psychological science.

  4. Trait impressions from voices require longer exposure to develop and stabilize although they are still formed by 400 ms.

    “Unlike faces, however, trait impressions from voices require longer exposure to develop and stabilize although they are still formed by 400 ms.”

    Study, 2023. Mileva & Lavan, Journal of Experimental Psychology General.

  5. When exposure time increased from 100 to 500 ms, participants' judgments became more negative, response times for judgments decreased, and confidence in judgments increased.

    “When exposure time increased from 100 to 500 ms, participants' judgments became more negative, response times for judgments decreased, and confidence in judgments increased.”

    Experiment, 2006. Willis & Todorov, Psychological science.

Your brain processes faces faster than speech.

Trait impressions from faces form in 100 ms. Impressions from voices take longer, stabilizing by 400 ms. Longer exposure to faces can lead to more negative judgments.

Visual bias dictates your reality.

That may be why your first glance at a stranger dictates your trust in them. Your brain makes a decision before you have time to think.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The hallway at your office.

You pass a colleague in the corridor. You decide if they are competent or approachable before they can say a single word to you.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that trait inferences from faces occur rapidly, often within 100 milliseconds, and can influence real-world behaviors like voting.

While impressions are formed rapidly, increasing exposure time can lead to more negative judgments and more differentiated person impressions.
