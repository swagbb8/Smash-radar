What are you missing right now?

File no. 0036October 8, 2026Psychology

You do not see what you are not looking for.

Eighty-three percent of radiologists did not see a gorilla that was 48 times the size of the average nodule. They looked directly at its location.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Eighty-three percent of radiologists did not see a gorilla that was 48 times the size of the average nodule.

    “A gorilla, 48 times the size of the average nodule, was inserted in the last case that was presented. Eighty-three percent of the radiologists did not see the gorilla.”

    Study, 2013. Drew et al., Psychological science.

  2. The majority of radiologists who missed the gorilla looked directly at its location.

    “Eye tracking revealed that the majority of those who missed the gorilla looked directly at its location.”

    Study, 2013. Drew et al., Psychological science.

  3. When attending to black items or white items in a dynamic display, nearly 30% of observers failed to notice a bright red cross move across the display, even though it had a unique color, luminance, shape, and motion trajectory and was visible for 5s.

    “when attending to black items or white items in a dynamic display, nearly 30% of observers failed to notice a bright red cross move across the display, even though it had a unique color, luminance, shape, and motion trajectory and was visible for 5s.”

    Experiment, 2001. Most et al., Psychological Science.

  4. Doubling or even tripling the time when the unexpected object was visible barely affected the likelihood of noticing it.

    “Doubling or even tripling the time when the unexpected object was visible barely affected the likelihood of noticing it and had no impact on how accurately subjects reported its features.”

    Experiment, 2019. Wood & Simons, Royal Society Open Science.

  5. Inattentional blindness significantly increases with the perceptual load of the task.

    “The results reveal that, in line with the perceptual account of the Load Theory, inattentional blindness significantly increases with the perceptual load of the task.”

    Meta-analysis, 2021. Matias et al., Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology.

  6. The most influential factor affecting noticing is a person's own attentional goals.

    “Although some stimulus properties can influence noticing of unexpected objects, the most influential factor affecting noticing is a person's own attentional goals.”

    Experiment, 2005. Most et al., Psychological review.

Your goals dictate your reality.

Inattentional blindness increases with the perceptual load of your task. Your own attentional goals are the most influential factor in what you notice.

Your focus is a filter.

That may be why you miss significant events in your life. When your mind is occupied, it filters out even the most salient information.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A familiar walk.

You walk through a crowded station with your headphones on. You focus on your destination. You pass a person in distress and do not see them.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that inattentional blindness is a robust phenomenon where specific attentional goals and perceptual loads can cause people to miss highly salient, visible events.

The cognitive account of the Load Theory is not clearly supported by the empirical findings analyzed in the meta-analysis.
