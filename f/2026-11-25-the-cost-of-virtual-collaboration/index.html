Who is actually doing the work in your remote team?

File no. 0331November 25, 2026Modern society

You cede your responsibility to virtual assistants.

In online experiments, people show social loafing tendencies when working with virtual assistants and tend to give up their share of the work to the technology.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Perceived playfulness, perceived ease of use, and xenophobia are linked to an individual's social loafing in a virtual team.

    “The results showed that the perceived playfulness, perceived ease of use, and xenophobia were able to predict an individual’s social loafing, but the perceived usefulness is not able to predict it.”

    Study, 2015. Abraham & Trimutiasari, International Journal of Electrical and Computer Engineering (IJECE).

  2. In an online experiment with N = 102, participants showed social loafing tendencies in virtual collaboration with virtual assistants and tended to cede responsibility to the virtual assistant.

    “The results indicate SL tendencies in virtual collaboration with VAs and that participants tend to cede responsibility to the VA.”

    Experiment, 2021. Stieglitz et al., Information Systems Frontiers.

  3. Factors contributing to social loafing in online learning include group size, internet connectivity issues, digital distractions, and types of leaders.

    “The Factors of Online Learning that Contribute to Social Loafing has four (4) emergent themes that were classified: Group size, Internet Connectivity issue, Digital distractions, and Types of Leaders”

    Study, 2023. Sagayno et al., British Journal of Teacher Education and Pedagogy.

  4. Group size is linked to social loafing behaviors.

    “Group size was found to be positively correlated with social loafing behaviors, but no significant relationship was found with task visibility.”

    Study, 2020. Tosuntaş, Journal of Pedagogical Research.

  5. In a study of university language learners, some students did not do their share of the work and engaged in social loafing and free riding.

    “While most students contributed to content and editing of their wiki page as required, some students did not do their share of the work and engaged in social loafing and free riding.”

    Study, 2012. Arnold et al., CALICO Journal.

Group size and digital factors drive loafing.

Social loafing is linked to larger group sizes, internet connectivity issues, and digital distractions. It is also influenced by perceived playfulness and the ease of using a tool.

The friction of remote work creates shadows.

That may be why you feel less accountable in large virtual teams. The presence of digital tools can make it easier to step back from your individual role.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A muted camera in a large meeting.

You sit in your chair. You watch the names of colleagues scroll by. You wait for someone else to speak first because the group is large and the task feels shared by everyone except you.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that social loafing occurs in virtual and online environments, often correlating with larger group sizes and the delegation of responsibility to technology.

The sources do not provide a definitive causal link between the difficulty of monitoring individual contributions and the increase in social loafing.
