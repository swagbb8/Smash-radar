Does quitting social media actually make you happier?

File no. 0004October 4, 2026Social media

Quitting social media can increase loneliness.

Studies show that total abstinence from these platforms can lead to a decline in life satisfaction and an increase in negative affect.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. People who stopped using social media showed a decline in life satisfaction, an increase in negative affect, and an increase in loneliness.

    “Those who abstained from social media use, compared with those in the control group, evidenced a decline in life satisfaction, an increase in negative affect, and an increase in loneliness.”

    Randomized trial, 2019. Vally & D'Souza, Perspectives In Psychiatric Care.

  2. Abstaining from social media had no measurable positive effect on well-being, and some models showed significant deficits in social relatedness and satisfaction with one’s day.

    “Evidence indicated that abstinence from social media had no measurable positive effect on well-being, and some models showed significant deficits in social relatedness and satisfaction with one’s day.”

    Experiment, 2021. Przybylski et al., Journal of Technology in Behavioral Science.

  3. Social media abstinence interventions had no significant effects on positive affect, negative affect, or life satisfaction.

    “The analyses revealed no significant effects of social media abstinence interventions on positive affect, negative affect, or life satisfaction.”

    Meta-analysis, 2025. Lemahieu et al., Scientific reports.

  4. Social media use has been shown to be effective in maintaining social connections and improving older adults' psychosocial outcomes.

    “Social media use has been shown to be effective in maintaining social connections and improving older adults' psychosocial outcomes.”

    Systematic review, 2024. Lei et al., International Psychogeriatrics.

  5. Limiting social media use is an effective way to reduce symptoms of depression.

    “Overall, this meta-analysis suggests that limiting social media use is an effective way to reduce symptoms of depression.”

    Meta-analysis, 2025. May et al., European Journal of Investigation in Health Psychology and Education.

  6. Social media use interventions are effective in improving mental well-being, especially for depression and when using therapy-based interventions.

    “This review provides some evidence that social media use interventions are effective in improving mental well-being, especially for depression and when using therapy-based interventions.”

    Systematic review, 2023. Plackett et al., Journal of Medical Internet Research.

The evidence is mixed.

While limiting use can reduce depression symptoms, complete abstinence often shows no measurable benefit for well-being and may cause deficits in social relatedness.

Connection is a requirement.

That may be why cutting ties entirely feels isolating. Social media can be a tool for maintaining connections and improving psychosocial outcomes for some people.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A quiet room.

You sit alone at a kitchen table. Your phone is in another room. You feel the weight of the silence and the distance from your friends.

The honest summary

The evidence is inconsistent, showing that while some interventions help with depression, total abstinence often fails to improve well-being and may even cause negative outcomes.

Many studies suffer from poor research quality, such as selection bias and small sample sizes, making it difficult to draw firm conclusions.
