Do you perform different versions of yourself online?

File no. 0209October 31, 2026Social media

You actively perform to keep your identities separate.

While social media merges your different worlds, you use tailored performances and privacy management to prevent your identities from collapsing into one.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. To prevent unintentional context collapse, participants frequently reinstated contexts through tailored performances and audience separation.

    “To prevent unintentional context collapse, participants frequently reinstated contexts through tailored performances and audience separation.”

    Study, 2014. Duguay, New Media & Society.

  2. Context collapse is experienced as an event where individuals intentionally redefine their sexual identity across audiences or manage unintentional disclosure.

    “Context collapse was experienced as an event through which individuals intentionally redefined their sexual identity across audiences or managed unintentional disclosure.”

    Study, 2014. Duguay, New Media & Society.

  3. Students engaged in practices such as adjusting privacy settings and self censoring in order to avoid context collapse between their social identities and their classroom identities.

    “Students engaged in practices such as adjusting privacy settings and self censoring in order to avoid context collapse between their social identities and their classroom identities.”

    Study, 2017. Dennen & Burner, Distance Education.

  4. The core topics of professional identity construction cover the blurring of professional stereotypes and reconstruction of multiple professional selves; merging public and private identities; belonging to a group and trusting social media.

    “The core topics of professional identity construction cover the blurring of professional stereotypes and reconstruction of multiple professional selves; merging public and private identities; belonging to a group and trusting social media.”

    Systematic review, 2019. Kasperiūnienė & Žydžiūnaitė, SAGE Open.

  5. Echo chambers not only foster ideological polarization but also serve as spaces for identity reinforcement and cultural belonging.

    “echo chambers not only foster ideological polarization but also serve as spaces for identity reinforcement and cultural belonging.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Ahmmad et al., Societies.

  6. Algorithmic systems structurally amplify ideological homogeneity, reinforcing selective exposure and limiting viewpoint diversity.

The structure of social media forces a merger.

Platforms blur professional stereotypes and merge public and private identities. Algorithms amplify ideological homogeneity, creating echo chambers that reinforce your cultural belonging.

Your online presence is a constant act of management.

That may be why you feel a constant need to monitor who sees what. You are not just sharing your life; you are constructing boundaries to protect your distinct selves.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

Adjusting a setting before a post.

You sit in a quiet room at night. You open an app. You check the privacy settings to ensure your classmates do not see what you share with your friends. You delete a sentence to fit a different audience. You manage your own boundaries in real time.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while context collapse is a structural reality of social media, individuals frequently employ specific strategies to maintain and separate their distinct identities.

The evidence base suffers from geographic bias toward Western contexts and limited longitudinal research.
