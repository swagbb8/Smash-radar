Is your work autonomy an illusion?

File no. 0230November 4, 2026Money and power

Your resistance to algorithmic management may reinforce your own control.

Workers who try to resist algorithmic management may inadvertently contribute to their own control. This happens through recursive dynamics that compress worker agency. The evidence on this is mixed and still developing.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Workers who try to resist algorithmic management may inadvertently contribute to their own control.

    “We find that workers exploit gaps that platforms subsequently close, rendering them unwitting contributors to their own control.”

    Experiment, 2026. Dasgupta et al., Human Relations.

  2. The recursive dynamics of algorithmic management may compress worker agency.

    “recursive dynamics may compress worker agency, exposing the limits of algoactivism and challenging assumptions about its emancipatory potential.”

    Experiment, 2026. Dasgupta et al., Human Relations.

  3. Algorithmic management is linked to various worker-related outcomes through several mediating pathways.

    “highlight main mediating pathways through which the individual dimensions of AM impact various gig worker-related outcomes.”

    Systematic review, 2024. Kadolkar et al., Journal of Organizational Behavior.

  4. Algorithmic management is linked to a tension between promised flexibility and algorithmic control.

    “the tension between promised flexibility and algorithmic control, worker resistance and agency, biases and fairness in algorithmic systems”

    Systematic review, 2026. Chigbu, Frontiers in sociology.

  5. Leader autonomy support is linked to basic needs, well-being, and positive work behaviors.

    “LAS was positively associated with basic needs, well-being, and positive work behaviors, and was negatively associated with distress.”

    Meta-analysis, 2018. Slemp et al., Motivation and Emotion.

  6. Authoritarian leadership and little autonomy are linked to burnout symptoms.

    “the risk factors that moderate this correlation were of structural/organisational type (social support, quality of the working environment, authoritarian leadership, little autonomy or long working days, etc.)”

    Systematic review, 2021. Lozano et al., International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

Platforms absorb your efforts to gain autonomy.

Algorithmic management creates a tension between promised flexibility and actual control. While autonomy support is linked to well-being, these systems can systematically absorb worker efforts to gain agency.

Your agency may be a feedback loop.

That may be why your attempts to work smarter or push back against the system end up refining the very tools that manage you.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A notification on your phone.

You adjust your pace to hit a metric. You think you are optimizing your day. The system records the change and adjusts your next task accordingly.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that while autonomy is a key driver of well-being, algorithmic systems can create complex dynamics where worker efforts to gain agency are systematically absorbed by the platform.

The research on algorithmic management is still developing, with some studies highlighting a gap in global and comparative analysis.
