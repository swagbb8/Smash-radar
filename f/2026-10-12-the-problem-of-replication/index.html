Only 36% of replicated psychology experiments achieved statistical significance.

While 97% of original studies reported statistically significant results, only 36% of the replicated studies achieved the same.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Only 36% of the replicated studies achieved statistical significance, while 97% of the original studies reported statistically significant results.

    “Although statistically significant results were reported in 97% of the original studies, statistical significance was achieved in only 36% of the replicated studies.”

    Experiment, 2016. Johnson et al., Journal of the American Statistical Association.

  2. More than 90% of tests performed in eligible psychology experiments tested negligible effects.

    “The resulting model suggests that more than 90% of tests performed in eligible psychology experiments tested negligible effects”

    Experiment, 2016. Johnson et al., Journal of the American Statistical Association.

  3. Replication effects were half the magnitude of the original effects.

    “Replication effects were half the magnitude of original effects, representing a substantial decline.”

    Experiment, 2015. Collaboration, Science (New York, N.Y.).

  4. Both optimistic and pessimistic conclusions about reproducibility are possible, and neither are yet warranted.

    “Using the Reproducibility Project: Psychology data, both optimistic and pessimistic conclusions about reproducibility are possible, and neither are yet warranted.”

    Study, 2016. Anderson et al., Science.

  5. Correlational tests suggest that replication success was better predicted by the strength of original evidence than by characteristics of the original and replication teams.

    “Correlational tests suggest that replication success was better predicted by the strength of original evidence than by characteristics of the original and replication teams.”

    Experiment, 2015. Collaboration, Science (New York, N.Y.).

Replication effects are often much smaller than first reported.

Replication effects were half the magnitude of the original effects. More than 90% of tests performed in eligible psychology experiments tested negligible effects.

The evidence on reproducibility is currently mixed.

The data shows a substantial decline in replication success, but some researchers argue that neither optimistic nor pessimistic conclusions are yet warranted.

The honest summary

The evidence shows a substantial decline in replication success compared to original findings, with many tests appearing to yield negligible effects.

One source notes that both optimistic and pessimistic conclusions about reproducibility are possible and neither are yet warranted.
