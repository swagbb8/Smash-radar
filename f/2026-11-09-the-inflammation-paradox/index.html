Does your workout actually cause inflammation?

File no. 0258November 9, 2026Health and environment

Overtraining is linked to inflammatory pathways.

When you push your body too far, reactive oxygen and nitrogen species may contribute to overtraining syndrome in skeletal muscle.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. Resistance training demonstrates the highest potential for mitigating systemic CRP in postmenopausal women with overweight or obesity.

    “Based on probabilistic rankings, RT demonstrates the highest potential for mitigating systemic CRP”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Huang et al., Frontiers in immunology.

  2. Combined training appears most effective in attenuating IL-6 and TNF-α in postmenopausal women with overweight or obesity.

    “whereas CT appears most effective in attenuating IL-6 and TNF-α.”

    Meta-analysis, 2026. Huang et al., Frontiers in immunology.

  3. Reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (ROS) and inflammatory pathways are the most likely mechanisms contributing to overtraining syndrome in skeletal muscle.

    “Current data implicates reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (ROS) and inflammatory pathways as the most likely mechanisms contributing to OTS in skeletal muscle.”

    Review, 2020. Cheng et al., Redox Biology.

  4. Exercise programs can reduce levels of CRP, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α.

    “Exercise program could attenuate the CRP, IL-6, IL-8 and TNF-α level.”

    Meta-analysis, 2025. Feng et al., BMC Sports Science Medicine and Rehabilitation.

  5. Exercise demonstrated a beneficial impact on oxidative status, as evidenced by decreased MDA levels and increased GPx activity.

    “Additionally, exercise demonstrated a beneficial impact on oxidative status, as evidenced by decreased MDA levels and increased GPx activity.”

    Meta-analysis, 2025. Feng et al., BMC Sports Science Medicine and Rehabilitation.

  6. Endurance training decreased circulating CRP, IL-6 and TNF-α levels.

    “Endurance training (ET) decreased circulating CRP, IL-6 and TNF-α levels.”

    Systematic review, 2021. Encabo et al., International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health.

Exercise usually lowers inflammation.

Structured programs typically reduce markers like CRP, IL-6, and TNF-α. Endurance training and resistance training show these effects in various populations.

The risk is in the intensity.

That may be why high-intensity training feels different. While exercise generally improves your oxidative status, extreme volume can trigger the very pathways you are trying to fix.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The heavy breathing in a dark gym.

You stand on a rubber mat at 6:00 am. Your muscles burn as you push past your limits. You think you are getting stronger, but your body is struggling to keep up.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that exercise typically reduces inflammatory markers and oxidative stress rather than increasing them, though overtraining syndrome is linked to inflammatory and oxidative stress mechanisms.

The evidence for specific exercise modalities in postmenopausal women has low to very low certainty.
