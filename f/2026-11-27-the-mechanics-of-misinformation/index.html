The uncomfortable truth

Is your brain losing its grip on reality?

File no. 0337November 27, 2026The human brain

Your brain does not lose its truth-sensing hardware.

Misinformation does not degrade the anterior cingulate cortex. This area remains active during dishonest decisions. Evidence on this specific degradation is currently mixed.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Deceptive immoral decisions under reputation risk make activity and functional connectivity between the bilateral ACC and left AI increase.

    “Crucially, deceptive immoral decisions under reputation risk enhanced activity of - and functional connectivity between - the bilateral ACC and left AI, suggesting the need for heightened emotional processing and cognitive control when making immoral decisions under reputation risk.”

    Experiment, 2023. Dupont et al., Communications Biology.

  2. Dishonest decisions are linked to increased activity in the bilateral anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) and anterior insula (AI).

    “Dishonest decisions were associated to increased activity in a cortico-subcortical circuit including the bilateral anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), anterior insula (AI), left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, supplementary motor area, and right caudate.”

    Experiment, 2023. Dupont et al., Communications Biology.

  3. Cognitive load reduces the effectiveness of corrections to misinformation, with a single correction being entirely ineffective under load.

    “cognitive load reduced correction effectiveness, with a single correction entirely ineffective under load.”

    Experiment, 2023. Sanderson et al., Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition.

  4. Low cognitive ability makes the effect of information strain and information overload on misinformation sharing more pronounced.

    “cognitive ability moderated and weakened the effect information strain and information overload have on misinformation sharing in such a way that this effect is more pronounced among those with low cognitive ability.”

    Study, 2022. Apuke et al., Journal of Information Science.

  5. Using an infographic reduces cognitive load, which makes people better able to verify false news.

    “Drawing from processing fluency theory, we propose that an infographic – a visual representation of information – reduces cognitive load, thereby helping people retain and process the necessary information to discern truth from falsehood in health news.”

    Experiment, 2021. Domgaard & Park, Health Education Journal.

  6. Information overload and social media fatigue are linked to sharing misinformation.

    “Our findings suggest that information overload and social media fatigue are strong predictors of misinformation sharing.”

    Study, 2022. Apuke et al., Journal of Information Science.

Cognitive load creates a barrier to truth.

Information overload and social media fatigue make it easier to share false news. High cognitive load can make a single correction entirely ineffective.

Your willpower is being outpaced by volume.

That may be why you find it harder to spot lies when you are tired. The problem is not a broken brain, but an overwhelmed one.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

Scrolling through your feed at 11 pm.

You see a headline that fits your view. You share it instantly. You are tired. Your brain is full. The truth feels like too much work to verify.

The honest summary

The sources show that cognitive load and information overload facilitate the spread and persistence of misinformation, but they do not provide evidence that high-frequency exposure to misinformation degrades the brain's ability to activate the anterior cingulate cortex.

None of the provided sources specifically measure the degradation of the anterior cingulate cortex's error-detection capabilities as a direct result of high-frequency misinformation exposure.
