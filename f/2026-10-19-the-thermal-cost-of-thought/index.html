Does the heat actually make you less smart?

File no. 0126October 19, 2026Health and environment

Heat impairs complex learning more than simple tasks.

While your brain handles basic reading well in the heat, complex tasks like mathematics suffer more. This evidence is mixed due to differences in study populations.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The effect of thermal variations on cognitive performance is relatively stable across a range of temperatures.

    “the effect of thermal variations on cognitive performance follows an extended-U relationship with performance being relatively stable across a range of temperatures.”

    Experiment, 2016. Zhang & Dear, Indoor Air.

  2. Long-term heat exposure is linked to impaired cumulative learning in students, with complex tasks like mathematics more affected than simpler ones like reading.

    “Long-term heat exposure was found to impair students’ cumulative learning, with complex tasks (e.g., mathematics) more affected than simpler ones (e.g., reading).”

    Systematic review, 2025. Vasilakopoulou & Santamouris, PLOS Climate.

  3. Maintaining adequate ventilation and thermal comfort in classrooms could raise an average test score to "commended performance".

    “Based on the results, maintaining adequate ventilation and thermal comfort could raise an average tests score to "commended performance".”

    Study, 2015. Haverinen‐Shaughnessy & Shaughnessy, PLoS ONE.

  4. Simpler cognitive tasks are less susceptible to temperature effects than more complex tasks.

    “Results confirm that simpler cognitive tasks are less susceptible to temperature effects than more complex tasks; the effect of thermal variations on cognitive performance follows an extended-U relationship”

    Experiment, 2016. Zhang & Dear, Indoor Air.

  5. Adaptation via acclimatization and increased air conditioning use showed protective effects against heat-impaired learning.

    “Adaptation via acclimatization and increased air conditioning use showed protective effects.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Vasilakopoulou & Santamouris, PLOS Climate.

Your brain has limits in high temperatures.

Long-term heat exposure is linked to impaired cumulative learning. However, cognitive performance stays relatively stable across many temperatures if you have proper ventilation and thermal comfort.

The environment shapes your mental capacity.

That may be why your focus slips when the air is still. Your ability to process difficult information is not fixed; it is tied to the temperature of your room.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The afternoon slump in a stuffy office.

You sit at your desk. The air is heavy. You try to solve a difficult problem but your mind feels sluggish and slow.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that heat impacts complex learning more than simple tasks, but performance is not uniformly degraded across all temperatures.

The findings are subject to population heterogeneity and methodological differences across the studies reviewed.
