Does fixing corruption actually create equality?

File no. 0179October 26, 2026Money and power

State capture follows wealth concentration, but not vice versa.

Control of corruption and government effectiveness do not have statistically significant effects on wealth inequality.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Control of corruption and government effectiveness do not have statistically significant effects on wealth inequality.

    “Our results reveal that control of corruption and government effectiveness do not have statistically significant effects on wealth inequality.”

    Study, 2023. Kovač & Verbič, South East European Journal of Economics and Business.

  2. Differentials in responsiveness are weaker in the United States compared to other developed democracies.

    “published research suggests that differentials in responsiveness are weaker in the United States compared to other developed democracies.”

    Systematic review, 2021. Elkjær & Klitgaard, Perspectives on Politics.

  3. Political outcomes better reflect the preferences of the rich, but results vary considerably across models and studies.

    “we find that, although this research collectively suggests that political outcomes better reflect the preferences of the rich, results vary considerably across models and studies.”

    Systematic review, 2021. Elkjær & Klitgaard, Perspectives on Politics.

  4. Domestic credit has an unfavourable effect on wealth inequality as measured by the Gini coefficient for wealth.

    “the findings indicate an unfavourable effect of domestic credit on wealth inequality as measured by the Gini coefficient for wealth.”

    Study, 2023. Kovač & Verbič, South East European Journal of Economics and Business.

  5. The greater the share of wealth the 1% hold, the more state capture we should expect.

    “The greater the share of wealth the 1% hold, the more state capture we should expect.”

    Study, 2023. Korman, International Journal of Development Issues.

The direction of power is one way.

High wealth concentration is linked to state capture. However, the quality of a government does not necessarily dictate how wealth is distributed among people.

The system is not broken.

That may be why fixing institutional corruption does not automatically result in a more equal distribution of wealth. The mechanisms of concentration operate independently of government effectiveness.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence shows a link between high wealth concentration and state capture in some regions, but it also shows that institutional quality does not necessarily dictate wealth distribution.

The findings are geographically and methodologically diverse, with some studies showing specific regional links while others find no significant effect on wealth inequality.
