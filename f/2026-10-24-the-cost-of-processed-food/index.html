Is your healthy snack linked to mortality?

File no. 0161October 24, 2026Health and environment

Ultra-processed food is linked to higher mortality.

High consumption of ultra-processed foods is linked to an increased risk of all-cause mortality.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. High consumption of ultra-processed foods is linked to an increased risk of all-cause mortality in five studies with a risk ratio (RR) 1·25, 95 % CI 1·14, 1·37; P < 0·00001.

    “highest UPF consumption was found to be associated with increased risk of all-cause mortality in five studies (risk ratio (RR) 1·25, 95 % CI 1·14, 1·37; P < 0·00001)”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Pagliai et al., British Journal Of Nutrition. This shows a link, not a cause.

  2. High consumption of ultra-processed foods is linked to an increased risk of irritable bowel syndrome, overall cancer, postmenopausal breast cancer, gestational obesity, adolescent asthma and wheezing, and frailty.

    “high UPFs consumption was obviously associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality, overall cardiovascular diseases, coronary heart diseases, cerebrovascular diseases, hypertension, metabolic syndrome, overweight and obesity, depression, irritable bowel syndrome, overall cancer, postmenopausal breast cancer, gestational obesity, adolescent asthma and wheezing, and frailty.”

    Systematic review, 2020. Chen et al., Nutrition Journal.

  3. High consumption of ultra-processed foods is linked to an increased risk of cerebrovascular disease in two studies with a risk ratio (RR) 1·34, 95 % CI 1·07, 1·68; P = 0·01.

    “cerebrovascular disease in two studies (RR 1·34, 95 % CI 1·07, 1·68; P = 0·01)”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Pagliai et al., British Journal Of Nutrition.

  4. High consumption of ultra-processed foods is linked to an increased risk of depression in two studies with a risk ratio (RR) 1·20, 95 % CI 1·03, 1·40; P = 0·02.

    “depression in two studies (RR 1·20, 95 % CI 1·03, 1·40; P = 0·02)”

    Meta-analysis, 2020. Pagliai et al., British Journal Of Nutrition.

  5. Greater exposure to ultra-processed food was associated with a higher risk of adverse health outcomes, especially cardiometabolic, common mental disorder, and mortality outcomes.

    “Greater exposure to ultra-processed food was associated with a higher risk of adverse health outcomes, especially cardiometabolic, common mental disorder, and mortality outcomes.”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Lane et al., BMJ.

The link to chronic disease.

High consumption of ultra-processed foods is linked to risks of cancer, irritable bowel syndrome, and cerebrovascular disease.

The honest summary

The evidence consistently links high consumption of ultra-processed foods to a wide range of adverse health outcomes including mortality, cardiovascular disease, and mental health disorders.

Most of the evidence is derived from observational studies, and large-scale prospective designed studies are still needed to confirm causal pathways.
