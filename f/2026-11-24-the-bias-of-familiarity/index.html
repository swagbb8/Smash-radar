Your brain is being hijacked

Do you like them because they are familiar?

File no. 0326November 24, 2026Human relationships

Social associations are distinct from simple repeated exposure.

Social association can modify perception and alter expectancies automatically. This is not the same as simply seeing something many times.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Social association does not reflect stimulus familiarity per se, that it can modify perception, that it can both occur automatically and alter expectancies.

    “Evidence using this procedure suggests that social association does not reflect stimulus familiarity per se, that it can modify perception, that it can both occur automatically and alter expectancies”

    Study, 2015. Humphreys & Sui, Journal of Cognitive Psychology.

  2. Judgements of inefficient actions were biased towards the efficient prediction.

    “Judgements of inefficient actions were biased towards the efficient prediction (straight trajectories upward to avoid the obstruction, arched trajectories downward towards the target).”

    Experiment, 2018. Hudson et al., Proceedings of the Royal Society B Biological Sciences.

  3. Investors do exhibit familiarity bias when choosing between different companies to invest in.

    “The results of this exploratory study indicate that investors do exhibit familiarity bias when choosing between different companies to invest in.”

    Observational study, 2017. Vries et al., Acta Commercii.

  4. Familiarity with novel foods and curiosity both led to higher acceptance.

    “Familiarity with novel foods and curiosity both led to higher acceptance.”

    Systematic review, 2024. Monaco et al., Appetite. This shows a link, not a cause.

Familiarity influences your choices.

Investors show a familiarity bias when choosing companies. Familiarity with novel foods is linked to higher acceptance. However, these influences are not always synonymous with repeated exposure.

Your perception may be biased.

That may be why you favor certain choices. Your brain may prioritize what is familiar over what is optimal, automatically modifying how you see the world.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The office chair.

You sit in the same chair every morning. You choose the same brand of coffee. These habits shape how you perceive your daily routine.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that familiarity and social associations influence perception and decision-making, but they are not always synonymous with simple repeated exposure.

The sources do not provide direct evidence on the 'mere exposure' effect specifically regarding the development of trust or intimacy in human relationships.
