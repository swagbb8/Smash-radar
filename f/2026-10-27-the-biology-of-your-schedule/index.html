Is your schedule fighting your biology?

File no. 0185October 27, 2026History

Your daily schedule may conflict with your biology.

Major dietary transitions and environmental factors like late-night work are linked to changes in sleep architecture and metabolic health.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Major dietary transitions plausibly altered sleep duration, architecture, circadian timing, neuroendocrine regulation, and the temporal alignment between feeding behavior and biological rhythms.

    “how each major dietary transition plausibly altered sleep duration, architecture, circadian timing, neuroendocrine regulation, and the temporal alignment between feeding behavior and biological rhythms.”

    Review, 2026. Bragazzi et al., Nutrients.

  2. Metabolic health and sleep integrity depend on when food is consumed in relation to biological rhythms shaped over deep evolutionary time.

    “the emerging framework of "evolutionary chrononutrition" proposes that metabolic health and sleep integrity depend not only on what humans eat, but critically on when food is consumed in relation to endogenous circadian architecture shaped across deep evolutionary time.”

    Review, 2026. Bragazzi et al., Nutrients.

  3. Circadian rhythm misalignments caused by environmental factors like late-night work and skipping breakfast are linked to negative impacts on endocrine and reproductive functions.

    “Circadian rhythm misalignments due to environmental factors, including late-night work and skipping breakfast, negatively impact endocrine and reproductive functions.”

    Review, 2025. Ono et al., Reproductive medicine and biology. This shows a link, not a cause.

Biological rhythms are shaped over deep evolutionary time.

When you eat or work outside these rhythms, it can alter your neuroendocrine regulation and the timing of your internal biological processes.

Your habits may be misaligning your internal systems.

That may be why late-night work and specific feeding behaviors are linked to negative impacts on your endocrine and reproductive functions.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.
