Can a machine see your dreams?

File no. 0009October 5, 2026The future

Machines can reconstruct your dreams from brain activity.

Generative models can identify links between functional magnetic resonance imaging patterns and verbal reports of visual imagery during sleep.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Machine-learning models can predict the contents of visual imagery during the sleep-onset period by discovering links between human functional magnetic resonance imaging patterns and verbal reports.

    “Here we present a neural decoding approach in which machine-learning models predict the contents of visual imagery during the sleep-onset period, given measured brain activity, by discovering links between human functional magnetic resonance imaging patterns and verbal reports”

    Study, 2013. Horikawa et al., Science.

  2. A brain decoding technique combined with deep neural network representations can reconstruct illusory percepts as images from brain activity.

    “Here, we leverage a brain decoding technique combined with deep neural network (DNN) representations to reconstruct illusory percepts as images from brain activity.”

    Study, 2023. Cheng et al., Science Advances.

  3. The DBDM model achieved Inception, CLIP and SwAV distances of 0.611, 0.225 and 0.405, respectively, when reconstructing semantic details of original images from fMRI patterns.

    “In particular, the best performance is achieved by DBDM in reconstructing the semantic details of the original image; the Inception, CLIP and SwAV distances are 0. 611, 0. 225 and 0. 405, respectively.”

    Experiment, 2023. Meng & Yang, Bioengineering.

  4. A two-stage framework called Brain-Diffuser can reconstruct images that capture low-level properties and overall layout from fMRI signals.

    “In the first stage, starting from fMRI signals, we reconstruct images that capture low-level properties and overall layout using a VDVAE (Very Deep Variational Autoencoder) model.”

    Study, 2023. Özçelik & VanRullen, Scientific Reports.

  5. The NeuralDiffuser model provides detailed cues in the form of gradients to achieve both semantic coherence and detail fidelity when reconstructing visual stimuli.

    “This extension of the bottom-up process for diffusion models achieves both semantic coherence and detail fidelity when reconstructing visual stimuli.”

    Experiment, 2025. Li et al., IEEE Transactions on Image Processing.

Neural networks decode your internal visuals.

Deep neural networks and diffusion models can translate fMRI signals into images. They reconstruct low-level layouts, semantic details, and even illusory percepts.

Your internal world is becoming data.

That may be why the line between your private thoughts and machine-readable data is thinning. Your subjective experiences are becoming accessible to decoding.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A quiet moment before sleep.

You lie in bed and see a fleeting image of a forest or a house. The machine sees the patterns your brain makes to create that image.

The honest summary

Multiple studies demonstrate that diffusion models and deep neural networks can successfully decode fMRI signals into visual reconstructions ranging from low-level layouts to complex semantic details.

The research focuses on reconstructing visual stimuli and experiences rather than real-time, high-fidelity visual streaming.
