Does your bank account change your biology?

File no. 0291November 16, 2026Money and power

Low-income white individuals show more physical weathering in skeletal samples.

The evidence shows that physical markers of stress and aging are more pronounced in low-socioeconomic-status white individuals than in bipoc individuals.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Low-socioeconomic-status white individuals have significantly more antemortem tooth loss than either BIPOC or high-SES white individuals.

    “We find some evidence for increased AMTL in BIPOC individuals, but significantly more AMTL in low-socioeconomic-status white individuals than either BIPOC or high-SES white individuals.”

    Study, 2023. Walkup et al., Forensic Science International Synergy.

  2. Poverty experienced during childhood is linked to chronic disease severity in late life.

    “Poverty experienced during childhood—but not during young adulthood or older adulthood—predicted chronic disease severity in late life.”

    Observational study, 2017. Do, The Scholars Repository - LLU (Loma Linda University).

  3. Black individuals have a higher risk of elevated stress markers than their White counterparts when looking at the relationship between child poverty and allostatic load.

    “Ethnicity moderates the child poverty and AL relationship such that Black individuals have higher risk of elevated stress markers than their White counterparts.”

    Observational study, 2017. Do, The Scholars Repository - LLU (Loma Linda University).

Poverty leaves a physical mark on the body.

Chronic stress from poverty is linked to markers like allostatic load and cortisol levels. The specific biological outcomes vary by ethnicity and life stage.

The body remembers economic history.

That may be why your current physical health is tied to the environment of your past. Poverty is not just a lack of money. It is a biological state.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The weight of a long shift.

You stand on a concrete floor for eight hours. Your teeth ache. Your joints feel heavy. You are tired in a way that sleep does not fix.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that poverty and chronic stress are linked to physiological markers like allostatic load, cortisol levels, and dental wear, but the specific biological outcomes vary by ethnicity and life stage.

Several key findings are based on observational studies that show associations rather than direct causation.
