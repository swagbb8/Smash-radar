What actually predicts your behavior?

File no. 0257November 9, 2026Human relationships

The evidence does not compare shared values to shared lifestyles.

The research focuses on commitment readiness, affective reactions, and stability factors rather than comparing shared values to shared lifestyles. The evidence on this specific comparison is mixed or contested.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence goes against the popular idea.

What the research found

  1. Intentions based on anticipated affective reactions are significantly stronger predictors of behavior than other factors like self-efficacy or past behavior.

    “the only significant moderator of the intention-behavior relationship was the extent to which intentions were based on anticipated affective reactions (intentions more strongly based on anticipated affective reactions were significantly stronger predictors of behavior).”

    Experiment, 2015. Conner et al., Health Psychology.

  2. The amount of time a person thinks is right to be in a committed relationship predicts their commitment level, maintenance processes, and actions toward ending a relationship.

    “readiness (a) predicts commitment, maintenance processes, and actions toward ending a relationship;”

    Study, 2019. Agnew et al., Social Psychological and Personality Science.

  3. People who report both higher commitment and higher readiness are more likely to enact maintenance behaviors and least likely to enact leave behavior.

    “those reporting both higher commitment and higher readiness being more likely to enact maintenance behaviors and least likely to enact leave behavior.”

    Study, 2019. Agnew et al., Social Psychological and Personality Science.

  4. Children in foster care who achieved early stability across every level of risk had a 22% probability of behavioral problems, compared to 36% among unstable children.

    “Among low-risk children, the probability of behavioral problems among early stabilizers was 22%, compared to 36% among unstable children, showing a 63% increase in behavior problems due to instability alone.”

    Observational study, 2007. Rubin et al., PEDIATRICS.

  5. Low levels of Agreeableness and Conscientiousness are associated with counterproductive behaviors.

    “Low levels of Agreeableness and Conscientiousness areassociated with counterproductive behaviors.”

    Meta-analysis, 2010. Șulea et al., Psihologia Resurselor Umane.
