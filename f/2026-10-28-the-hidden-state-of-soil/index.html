What the research found

  1. The Southeast Cerrado was the most biotically stable and irreplaceable region for wild edible plants compared with other areas across the neotropics.

    “The Southeast Cerrado was the most biotically stable and irreplaceable region for the WEPs compared with other areas across the neotropics.”

    Study, 2015. Oliveira et al., American Journal of Botany.

  2. Incorporating agricultural expansion data into the spatial prioritization process can reduce the spatial correlation between areas under high impact of agriculture and priority areas for conservation.

    “incorporating agricultural expansion data into the spatial prioritization process can significantly alleviate conservation conflicts, by reducing spatial correlation between the areas under high impact of agriculture and the priority areas for conservation.”

    Study, 2014. Dobrovolski, Frontiers of Biogeography.

  3. In the South American Chaco, 68% of carbon emissions from land-use changes came from forest-to-grazing-land conversions.

    “The majority of these emissions came from forest‐to‐grazing‐land conversions (68%),”

    Study, 2016. Baumann et al., Global Change Biology.

  4. Transition zones between agricultural lands and páramos experience the highest pressure from anthropogenic activities.

    “Results indicate that transition zones between agricultural lands and páramos experience the highest pressure from anthropogenic activities.”

    Study, 2026. González‐Orozco, Discover Conservation.

  5. Human activities, including industrial and agricultural expansion, have significantly disrupted natural river morphology and ecological patterns.

    “However, human activities, including hydraulic engineering, land use changes, industrial and agricultural expansion, and overfishing, have significantly disrupted natural river morphology and ecological patterns.”

    Review, 2026. Zhang et al., Environmental monitoring and assessment.
