The future is closer than you think

Can the soil keep producing forever?

File no. 0305November 19, 2026History

The Green Revolution linked to permanent soil health degradation.

The push for higher grain yields in India is linked to the loss of soil flora and fauna. It is also linked to systematic damages to the soil, groundwater, and ecosystem.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. The Green Revolution in India is linked to the loss of soil flora and fauna and ecological unbalance.

    “The country witnessed the benefits Green Revolution Technologies (GRTs) in boosting of grain yield and the after effect of GRTs during past few decades in terms of deterioration of soil fertility and land degradation, loss of soil flora and fauna, genetic erosion, ecological unbalance”

    Review, 2018. Maitra et al., Journal of Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry.

  2. The Green Revolution in India is linked to yield plateauing and associated insecurity in the livelihood of the farmers.

    “The country witnessed the benefits Green Revolution Technologies (GRTs) in boosting of grain yield and the after effect of GRTs during past few decades in terms of deterioration of soil fertility and land degradation, loss of soil flora and fauna, genetic erosion, ecological unbalance, yield plateauing and associated insecurity in livelihood of the farmers.”

    Review, 2018. Maitra et al., Journal of Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry.

  3. The Green Revolution in India is linked to the deterioration of soil fertility and land degradation.

    “The country witnessed the benefits Green Revolution Technologies (GRTs) in boosting of grain yield and the after effect of GRTs during past few decades in terms of deterioration of soil fertility and land degradation”

    Review, 2018. Maitra et al., Journal of Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry.

  4. The use of urea as a nitrogen-rich fertilizer in India is linked to global warming.

    “As urea, a nitrogen-rich fertilizer is used much more than the recommended 4-to-1 ratio to potassium; it is contributing to the global warming.”

    Study, 2015. Rahman, Asian Journal of Water Environment and Pollution.

  5. The Green Revolution in India is linked to systematic damages to the soil, groundwater, and ecosystem.

    “The extent of systematic damages caused in the process of Green Revolution to the soil, groundwater, and ecosystem needs to be quantified.”

    Study, 2015. Rahman, Asian Journal of Water Environment and Pollution. This shows a link, not a cause.

  6. The Green Revolution in India is linked to the degradation of soil health and increasingly larger shares of public spending on input subsidies.

    “Both continents have two challenges in common: degradation of soil health and increasingly larger shares of public spending on input subsidies.”

    Study, 2013. Rashid et al., Agricultural Economics.

Yields plateau while soil health declines.

The Green Revolution is linked to yield plateauing and insecurity for farmers. It is also linked to a larger share of public spending on input subsidies as soil fertility deteriorates.

The cost of your food is hidden in the earth.

That may be why the food you eat relies on an increasingly fragile system. The land is losing its ability to sustain itself without constant intervention.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The grain in your pantry.

You see a full bowl of rice or wheat. You do not see the depleted ground or the chemical inputs required to keep it there.

The honest summary

The sources provide evidence that Green Revolution Technologies are linked to significant environmental and soil degradation in India and other regions.

The sources do not provide a specific quantification of the systematic damages caused to the soil or groundwater.
