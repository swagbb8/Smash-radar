Can history be deleted?

File no. 0168October 24, 2026Modern society

The right to be forgotten is a legal obligation.

It is not primarily an ethical principle.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The Right to Be Forgotten is linked to the rights of self-determination, to delete, and to identity and individuality in the context of gender transition.

    “it discusses in gender transition the relation between "the right to self-determination", "the right to delete", and "the right to identity and individuality".”

    Study, 2021. Correia et al., Health Care Analysis.

  2. The Right to Be Forgotten is predominantly interpreted as a legal obligation rather than an ethical principle.

    “The findings indicate that RTBF is predominantly interpreted as a legal obligation rather than an ethical principle.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Taşdelen, Denetişim.

  3. The Right to Be Forgotten creates a conflict between immutable ledgers and data deletion requirements in blockchain systems.

    “The most frequently identified ethical challenge concerns the conflict between immutable ledgers and data deletion requirements.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Taşdelen, Denetişim.

  4. Google and other search engines now delist links to web pages that contain information about an individual that is inadequate, irrelevant, no longer relevant, or excessive.

    “Google and other search engines now delist links to web pages that contain “inadequate, irrelevant or no longer relevant, or excessive” information about that individual.”

    Experiment, 2016. Xue et al., Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies.

  5. The United States resists the adoption of the Right to Be Forgotten due to strong protections for free speech.

    “jurisdictions like the United States resisting its adoption due to strong protections for free speech.”

    Study, 2022. Mbah, World Journal of Advanced Research and Reviews. This shows a link, not a cause.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that the Right to Be Forgotten is a complex legal and ethical framework that balances individual privacy against data permanence and public interest.

The sources provide a broad overview of legal and ethical tensions but do not provide specific data on the extent of 'systemic gaps' in the historical record.
