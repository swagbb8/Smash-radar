The uncomfortable truth

Do you actually choose your next move?

File no. 0296November 17, 2026The human brain

The readiness potential is not your conscious intent.

Neural activity shows planning and anticipation, but it does not correlate with your subjective feeling of being ready to act.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. The feeling of readiness is linked to beta-band amplitude, but not with the readiness potential.

    “We found that the feeling of readiness reliably correlates with the beta-band amplitude, but not with the readiness potential.”

    Experiment, 2023. Parés-Pujolràs et al., Neuroscience of Consciousness.

  2. The readiness potential reflects planning and anticipation for a forthcoming action rather than freedom from external constraint.

    “We found that RP amplitudes grew with learning, suggesting that this neural activity reflects planning and anticipation for the forthcoming action, rather than freedom from external constraint.”

    Experiment, 2020. Travers et al., Cognitive Neuroscience.

  3. Adults with ADHD showed significantly diminished RP peaks and also decreased negativity in preparation of the movement at frontal locations compared with healthy controls.

    “Compared with healthy controls, ADHD patients showed significantly diminished RP peaks and also decreased negativity in preparation of the movement at frontal locations.”

    Study, 2013. Seo et al., Journal of Attention Disorders.

  4. Participants with ACLR demonstrated greater motor planning and response inhibition during a choice reaction time task.

    “The ACLR group demonstrated greater motor planning and response inhibition during a choice reaction time task.”

    Study, 2022. Sherman et al., Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise.

Your brain plans before you feel ready.

The readiness potential reflects neural planning for a forthcoming action. It is a marker of anticipation rather than a direct indicator of your conscious choice to move.

Your sense of agency may be an illusion.

That may be why you feel like you decide to act in the moment, when your brain has already begun the mechanical process of preparation.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

You reach for your coffee.

You sit at the kitchen table in the morning. Your hand moves toward the mug. You feel like you chose that exact second to reach, but your brain began the plan before the feeling arrived.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that the readiness potential is a marker of neural planning and anticipation rather than a direct indicator of conscious intent or the subjective feeling of being ready to act.

The research does not definitively prove that the brain initiates actions before conscious intent, as one study found no correlation between the readiness potential and the subjective feeling of readiness.
