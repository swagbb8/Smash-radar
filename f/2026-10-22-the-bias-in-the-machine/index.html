The uncomfortable truth

Who is actually deciding if you are qualified?

File no. 0146October 22, 2026Technology

Technical limitations are not being put into practice.

There is a disconnect between the documentation of AI risks and the governance frameworks meant to manage them.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Technical studies rarely engage with HCAI design principles, while governance-oriented work rarely operationalizes the technical limitations documented in empirical literature.

    “Technical studies rarely engage with HCAI design principles governance-oriented work rarely operationalizes the technical limitations that the empirical literature has documented in detail.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Moutafail & Belkhoutout, Springer Link (Chiba Institute of Technology).

  2. Incomplete specifications in design protocols and weak AI design exploited for prediction are linked to data bias and algorithmic bias in healthcare studies.

    “the majority of the studies in healthcare suffer from data bias and algorithmic bias due to incomplete specifications mentioned in the design protocol and weak AI design exploited for prediction.”

    Systematic review, 2023. Kumar et al., Multimedia Tools and Applications.

  3. AI-recruitment systems may fail to achieve unbiased decisions about applicants because they can replicate and amplify human biases.

    “There are risks of encoding biases in the datasets and algorithms of AI which lead AIRS to replicate and amplify human biases.”

    Study, 2021. Soleimani et al., International Journal of Knowledge Management.

  4. Knowledge sharing between HR managers and AI developers is linked to mitigating biases in AI-recruitment systems by informing data labeling and understanding job functions.

    “The findings show that knowledge sharing can help to mitigate biases in AIRS by informing data labeling, understanding job functions, and improving the machine learning model.”

    Study, 2021. Soleimani et al., International Journal of Knowledge Management.

  5. AI-driven recruitment systems are linked to significant concerns regarding algorithmic bias, fairness, transparency, and accountability.

    “While AI-driven recruitment systems improve efficiency and decision-making, they also introduce significant concerns regarding algorithmic bias, fairness, transparency, and accountability.”

    Systematic review, 2026. Rakshitha M & VijayKumar.

Algorithms replicate and amplify human biases.

AI-recruitment systems can mirror human prejudices. Incomplete design protocols and weak specifications lead to data bias and algorithmic bias.

The tools are built to predict, not to be fair.

That may be why systems designed for efficiency end up perpetuating historical inequalities. The risk is known, but the solution is not operationalized.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

You submit a resume for a job you want.

You click send. A machine scans your history. It looks for patterns in data that humans left behind years ago. You wait for a response that may never come.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that AI-recruitment systems can replicate human biases and suffer from design-related flaws, but it highlights a gap between identifying these risks and operationalizing technical solutions.

The sources focus heavily on identifying risks and theoretical frameworks rather than providing specific data on the exact proxies used for protected classes.
