Does the light in your home affect your weight?

File no. 0026October 8, 2026Health and environment

Light at night is linked to a 14% increased risk of obesity.

Exposure to light during the night is associated with higher body weight in the highest exposure groups.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Exposure to light at night is linked to a 14% increased risk of obesity in the highest exposure group compared to the lowest.

    “A significant 14% increased risk of obesity was observed in the highest LAN exposure group compared to the lowest (OR = 1. 14, 95% CI: 1. 07-1. 22; I² = 92. 5%, P 0. 05).”

    Meta-analysis, 2025. Tang et al., BMC Public Health.

  2. Blue-enriched white light with a color temperature greater than 5000 Kelvin improves sleepiness in night-shift workers.

    “Further, the blue-enriched white light with a color temperature greater than 5000 Kelvin was effective in improving sleepiness of night-shift workers.”

    Meta-analysis, 2022. Wu et al., Healthcare.

  3. Light at night is linked to an increased risk of depression.

    “Our findings suggest that both outdoor and indoor ALAN exposures are associated with increased risk of depression.”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Chen et al., Environmental Health and Preventive Medicine.

  4. Sleep interventions, including light therapy, improve sleep or reduce sleep disturbance among rotating night shift workers.

    “Conclusion Sleep interventions were effective in promoting sleep or reducing sleep disturbance among rotating night shift workers.”

    Meta-analysis, 2023. Jeon et al., Frontiers in Public Health.

Light exposure affects your metabolism and mental health.

Research links light at night to increased risks of obesity and depression. While evidence for depression is inconsistent, the link to metabolic issues is established.

Your environment shapes your physical health.

The lights in your home may be influencing your weight and mental state. That may be why your body feels different at night.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The glow of a screen in a dark room.

You sit on the couch late at night. The only light comes from the phone in your hand or the television across the room.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that light at night is associated with metabolic issues like obesity and several mental health disorders, while specific lighting interventions can improve sleepiness in shift workers.

The evidence for depression is inconsistent, with one meta-analysis failing to reach statistical significance.
