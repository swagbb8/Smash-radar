Can you tell who is real?

File no. 0081October 14, 2026Technology

AI faces are rated as more trustworthy than real faces.

Studies show that synthetic faces are indistinguishable from human ones and earn higher trust ratings. You are more likely to believe a person who does not exist.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. AI-synthesized faces are indistinguishable from real faces and are rated as more trustworthy than real faces.

    “Our evaluation of the photorealism of AI-synthesized faces indicates that synthesis engines have passed through the uncanny valley and are capable of creating faces that are indistinguishable-and more trustworthy-than real faces.”

    Study, 2022. Nightingale & Farid, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America.

  2. People's accuracy in detecting deepfakes is 53.16% [42.12, 64.64], k = 18 for images, which is below the chance level of 35%.

    “for images, 53. 16% [42. 12, 64. 64], k = 18; for text, 52. 00% [37. 42, 65. 88], k = 15; and for video, 57. 31% [47. 80, 66. 57], k = 26. Odds ratios were 0. 64 [0. 52, 0. 79], k = 62, indicating 39% detection accuracy, below chance (audio 45%, image 35%, text 40%, video 40%).”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Diel et al., Computers in Human Behavior Reports.

  3. Synthesized human deepfake detection is at chance for different modalities.

    “Synthesized human deepfake detection is at chance for different modalities.”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Diel et al., Computers in Human Behavior Reports.

  4. Manipulations designed to disrupt visual processing of faces hinder human participants' performance while mostly not affecting the model's performance.

    “We find that manipulations designed to disrupt visual processing of faces hinder human participants' performance while mostly not affecting the model's performance, suggesting a role for specialized cognitive capacities in explaining human deepfake detection performance.”

    Experiment, 2021. Groh et al., Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

  5. Detection accuracy varies widely, with some studies showing humans outperforming AI models and others indicating the opposite.

    “Detection accuracy varies widely, with some studies showing humans outperforming AI models and others indicating the opposite.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Somoray et al., Human Behavior and Emerging Technologies.

  6. Strategies like feedback training, AI support, and deepfake caricaturization improved detection performance above chance levels (65.14% [55.21, 74.46], k = 15), especially for video stimuli.

    “Moreover, d' values show no significant difference from chance. However, strategies like feedback training, AI support, and deepfake caricaturization improved detection performance above chance levels (65. 14% [55. 21, 74. 46], k = 15), especially for video stimuli.”

    Meta-analysis, 2024. Diel et al., Computers in Human Behavior Reports.

Your brain is currently unable to detect these fakes.

Human accuracy in detecting deepfakes is currently at chance levels. While certain interventions can improve this, your natural ability to spot synthetic faces is limited.

Your sense of trust is being bypassed.

That may be why you feel a connection to digital personas. If a fake face is more trustworthy than a real one, your intuition is no longer a reliable filter.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The honest summary

The evidence confirms that AI faces can be more trustworthy than real ones and that humans currently struggle to detect them at rates better than chance.

Detection performance is influenced by a complex interplay of factors including person-level cognitive abilities and stimuli-level factors like deepfake quality.
