Who told the guards to be cruel?

File no. 0041October 8, 2026History

The guards were told how to act.

Experimenters gave the guards precise instructions on how to treat the prisoners and encouraged them to adopt tough roles.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. The experimenters directly encouraged the guards to adopt roles and act tough.

    “experimenters directly encouraged them to adopt roles and act tough in a manner consistent with tenets of identity leadership.”

    Experiment, 2019. Haslam et al., American Psychologist.

  2. The guards received precise instructions regarding the treatment of the prisoners.

    “the fact that the guards received precise instructions regarding the treatment of the prisoners”

    Experiment, 2019. Le Texier, The American psychologist.

  3. The SPE drew on a prison experiment conducted by students in one of Zimbardo's classes 3 months earlier.

    “the extent to which the SPE drew on a prison experiment devised and conducted by students in one of Zimbardo's classes 3 months earlier”

    Experiment, 2019. Le Texier, The American psychologist.

  4. The participants were selected as the most stable mentally and least involved in antisocial behaviours.

    “The participants were selected as the most stable mentally and least involved in antisocial behaviours of those who had applied to take part.”

    Experiment, 2019. McDermott, UEL Research Repository (University of East London).

  5. The college student prisoners were not representative of prison populations in terms of ethnicity, educational attainment and socioeconomic status.

    “The college student prisoners were not representative of prison populations in terms of ethnicity, educational attainment and socioeconomic status.”

    Experiment, 2019. McDermott, UEL Research Repository (University of East London).

It was not a spontaneous behavior.

The study drew on a prison experiment conducted by students three months earlier. The behavior was a result of coaching and prior work.

The power was manufactured.

That may be why the results look like a study on human nature when they might actually be a study on how people follow orders.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A supervisor giving a direct order.

You stand in a hallway and watch someone tell you exactly how to behave in your job. You do it because they told you to.

The honest summary

The evidence indicates that the Stanford Prison Experiment was heavily influenced by external instructions and prior student work rather than being a purely organic demonstration of role conformity.

While the sources highlight significant flaws and coaching, they also note that the interpersonal power dynamics in the simulation successfully mimicked those of a real prison.

Who is giving you your instructions?
