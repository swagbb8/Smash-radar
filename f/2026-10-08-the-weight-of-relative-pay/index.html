The uncomfortable truth

Why does your neighbor's salary matter to you?

File no. 0038October 8, 2026Money and power

Knowing you earn less can increase your effort.

When workers see their peers earn more, they provide significantly more labor effort. This effect is often moderated by where you stand in your own company's hierarchy.

Established. Several studies combined, or official data. The evidence is mixed.

What the research found

  1. Piece-rate workers shown their peer earnings standing provide significantly more labor effort.

    “Piece-rate workers shown their peer earnings standing provide significantly more labor effort.”

    Experiment, 2025. Huet-Vaughn, Experimental Economics.

  2. The effect of employee standing in the external pay hierarchy depended on where one stood in the internal pay hierarchy.

    “Additionally, within-level interactions indicated that the effect of employee standing in the external pay hierarchy depended on where one stood in the internal pay hierarchy”

    Study, 2006. Trevor & Wazeter, Journal of Applied Psychology.

  3. Differences in pay rank matter more than differences in pay levels.

    “Differences in pay rank matter more than differences in pay levels.”

    Study, 2012. Card et al., American Economic Review.

  4. Underpaid CEOs will engage in acquisition activity as a self-interested means of attempting to realign their pay with that of their peers.

    “Specifically, we propose that CEO s with negative relative pay standing status (underpaid relative to comparison CEO s) will engage in acquisition activity, as a self‐interested means of attempting to realign their pay with that of their peers.”

    Study, 2014. Seo et al., Strategic Management Journal.

  5. Workers with salaries below the median for their pay unit and occupation report lower pay and job satisfaction and a significant increase in the likelihood of looking for a new job.

    “Workers with salaries below the median for their pay unit and occupation report lower pay and job satisfaction and a significant increase in the likelihood of looking for a new job.”

    Study, 2012. Card et al., American Economic Review.

  6. Having a lower income than the reference group is associated with poorer wellbeing.

    “Having a lower income than the reference group is associated with poorer wellbeing.”

    Systematic review, 2025. Croak et al., PloS one.

Comparison shapes your work and your health.

Earning below the median for your occupation is linked to lower job satisfaction and poorer wellbeing. It also triggers strategic behaviors, like looking for a new job or attempting to realign pay through acquisitions.

Your pay is a relative measure.

That may be why your sense of worth is tied to your neighbors. Your internal rank might determine how much the external hierarchy affects your drive.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

The coffee break.

You sit in the breakroom and look at your phone. You see a post from a friend in the same industry who just got a promotion.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that relative pay comparisons significantly impact subjective wellbeing and job satisfaction for those earning below the median, but these comparisons can also trigger increased labor effort or strategic business behaviors.

The impact of relative pay comparisons is often moderated by an individual's specific rank within their internal organization's hierarchy.

Is your effort your own, or a reaction to them?
