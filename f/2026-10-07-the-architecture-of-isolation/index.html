The dark side of modern life

Why are you talking to your screen instead of your coworkers?

File no. 0016October 7, 2026Modern society

Open offices link to social withdrawal.

Open office architecture appears to trigger a natural human response to withdraw from coworkers. Instead of face-to-face interaction, people interact over email and instant messages.

Supported. At least one solid peer-reviewed study. The evidence backs this.

What the research found

  1. Open architecture appeared to trigger a natural human response to socially withdraw from officemates and interact instead over email and IM.

    “In short, rather than prompting increasingly vibrant face-to-face collaboration, open architecture appeared to trigger a natural human response to socially withdraw from officemates and interact instead over email and IM.”

    Study, 2018. Bernstein & Turban, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B Biological Sciences. This shows a link, not a cause.

  2. The best-performing offices implemented active and biophilic design, prioritized overall ergonomics and different spaces designed to support a variety of work-related activities.

    “The best-performing offices implemented active and biophilic design, prioritized overall ergonomics and different spaces designed to support a variety of work-related activities.”

    Study, 2020. Cândido et al., Facilities.

  3. Workplaces can be successfully designed to facilitate the seemingly conflicting requirements of supporting both collaboration and concentration.

    “It considers how workplaces can besuccessfully designed to facilitate the seemingly conflicting requirements of supporting both collaboration and concentration”

    Study, 2015. Walsh, Arrow - TU Dublin (Technological University Dublin).

The architecture of your workspace matters.

Research shows that open designs are linked to a decrease in personal interaction. Workplaces can be designed to balance collaboration and concentration, but open plans often lead to electronic communication.

Your digital presence is a defense.

That may be why you reach for your keyboard instead of looking up. The physical space you work in might be driving you to isolate yourself behind a screen.

This part is our reading of the evidence, not a finding.

A message instead of a nod.

You sit at your desk. A coworker walks past. You do not look up. You type a message to them instead of speaking aloud. You stay in your chair. The screen stays lit.

The honest summary

The evidence shows that open office architectures are linked to a decrease in face-to-face interaction and an increase in electronic interaction.

The research is limited by a lack of direct empirical research on how human interaction patterns change as a result of these architectural changes.
