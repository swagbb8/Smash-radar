The newest files, each in two sentences.

  1. where does the scroll end?

    Social media addiction is formalized as entrapment in a design built through intentional variable-ratio reinforcement.

    Supported. The sources indicate that infinite scrolling is a deliberate design choice that creates difficult-to-break loops and shares clinical markers with other addictions.

  2. Does your interaction actually stop the hate?

    Offensive messages are often only reviewed by human moderators if a user files a complaint.

    Established. The sources describe the mechanisms and failures of online hate speech management rather than providing evidence that active engagement reduces hate speech.

  3. Is your kindness a biological defense?

    A multilevel biological threat response to social situations can increase risk for viral infections and inflammation-related disease conditions when sustained.

    Supported. The evidence shows that parental altruism is associated with lower anxiety and higher trust, but it also establishes that humans have a biological system for responding to social threats that can have physical health consequences.

  4. Is your home's warmth costing you your health?

    This global burden of premature mortality is a consequence of household air pollution from solid fuels. The evidence is mixed regarding the specific impact of rustic wood smoke on cardiovascular risk.

    Established. The evidence confirms that household air pollution from solid fuels is a major driver of global mortality and developmental issues, but it does not specifically isolate 'rustic' wood smoke from fireplaces as a primary cause of cardiovascular risk.

  5. Does your screen change how you feel time?

    Engaging with interactive content changes how you experience the passage of hours. This finding is currently supported but not definitively proven as a universal rule.

    Established. The evidence shows that interactive media can increase time perception and flow, but it does not specifically confirm that high-frequency consumption distorts neural processing speed.

  6. Why does choosing feel so heavy?

    When you perceive digital personalization as a strategy to control you, the cognitive cost of choosing increases. This happens because your persuasion knowledge activates.

    Supported. The evidence confirms that decision fatigue is a real phenomenon driven by cognitive costs, persuasion knowledge, and perceived responsibility in digital environments.

  7. Why is it so hard to choose your future over your present?

    Present bias for real consumption is stronger than for real effort. This makes it harder to choose long-term goals over immediate rewards.

    Established. The evidence confirms that temporal discounting and present bias contribute to impulsive and habitual behaviors, but it does not specifically address long-term planning for climate change.

  8. Is your brain losing its grip on reality?

    Misinformation does not degrade the anterior cingulate cortex. This area remains active during dishonest decisions. Evidence on this specific degradation is currently mixed.

    Supported. The sources show that cognitive load and information overload facilitate the spread and persistence of misinformation, but they do not provide evidence that high-frequency exposure to misinformation degrades the brain's ability to activate the anterior cingulate cortex.

  9. Can a machine know how you feel?

    Affective computing can automatically identify your emotional states by analyzing neural and cardiac dynamics. This technology is capable of recognizing and forecasting your feelings.

    Established. The evidence confirms that wearable technology can be used to recognize and forecast emotions, but it also highlights significant hurdles regarding data reliability and ethical privacy implications.

  10. Does every noise affect a child's brain?

    Evidence links aircraft noise to lower reading performance, but some road and railway noise show no effect on attention or executive function.

    Established. The evidence suggests a link between noise and cognitive performance in children, but results are inconsistent and often of low or moderate quality.

  11. Does being nice make you less happy?

    The data shows that being agreeable is linked to better relationships and positive affect, rather than lower life satisfaction. The evidence on its impact on overall well-being remains mixed.

    Established. The evidence does not support the idea that high agreeableness leads to lower life satisfaction; instead, it is linked to higher marital satisfaction and positive affect.

  12. Is your spice rack linked to biodiversity?

    The health of an ecosystem is tied to how we produce spices. However, the evidence does not show the historical impact of maritime trade or tropical degradation.

    Supported. The evidence confirms that monocultures are less biodiverse than mixed-species stands and that biodiversity is linked to spice production, but it lacks specific data on historical trade impacts.

  13. Are you actually who you think you are?

    Personality traits are both stable and changeable throughout your life span. Targeted interventions can produce measurable changes in your traits.

    Established. The evidence shows that personality is not fixed and can be significantly altered through targeted interventions, especially for those with specific goals.

  14. Who is actually doing the work in your remote team?

    In online experiments, people show social loafing tendencies when working with virtual assistants and tend to give up their share of the work to the technology.

    Supported. The evidence confirms that social loafing occurs in virtual and online environments, often correlating with larger group sizes and the delegation of responsibility to technology.

  15. Does your smoothie actually lower your Glycemic Index?

    While smoothies require more meals to maintain a low Glycemic Load, the process of nutrient extraction in specific fruits like raspberries and passionfruit can actually reduce their Glycemic Index.

    Supported. The evidence contradicts the proposed angle by showing that nutrient extraction can lower the Glycemic Index of certain fruits, even though smoothies are associated with higher meal counts to maintain a low Glycemic Load.

  16. Is your face hiding your thoughts?

    Current research on emotion recognition and facial motor circuits provides no data on whether surveillance causes you to subconsciously alter your expressions. The evidence is mixed and inconclusive.

    Supported. The sources discuss emotion recognition technology, facial motor circuits, and medical biomarkers, but none address the psychological impact of surveillance on facial expressions.

  17. Does your cleaning routine irritate your lungs?

    Experimental data shows that volatile organic compounds (VOCs) cause dose-related respiratory symptoms, but they do not significantly change lung function or biomarkers of airway inflammation.

    Supported. The evidence confirms that VOCs are associated with respiratory symptoms and asthma onset, but it also shows that these exposures do not necessarily result in measurable changes to lung function or specific inflammation biomarkers.

  18. Who decided what your medicine does?

    Business interests have influenced the development of the field of anthropology. They also shape the types of pharmaceutical effects that are made and remade for society.

    Supported. The evidence confirms that commercial interests influence anthropological development and pharmaceutical production, but it lacks specific data on the funding of early biological studies for labor exploitation.

  19. Do you like them because they are familiar?

    Social association can modify perception and alter expectancies automatically. This is not the same as simply seeing something many times.

    Established. The evidence shows that familiarity and social associations influence perception and decision-making, but they are not always synonymous with simple repeated exposure.

  20. Does your past have more power than your present?

    The secondary flow of attention to past events generated by remembering processes is larger than the primary attention flow to the current event.

    Supported. The evidence shows that digital environments create a tension between reduced individual memory retention and increased cascading flows of attention to historical data.

  21. Does your city's hum change your brain?

    Research suggests noise exposure is linked to amyloid-β deposition and tau hyperphosphorylation in the hippocampus and cortex. This evidence is mixed, involving both rat experiments and human observations.

    Established. The evidence suggests that chronic noise exposure is associated with cognitive decline and specific neuropathological changes, though it also impacts cardiovascular health and children's verbal performance.

  22. Does your convenient meal have a hidden cost?

    Research consistently links these foods to metabolic dysfunction and systemic inflammation across all life stages. There is no evidence of any beneficial health outcomes.

    Established. The evidence consistently links high ultra-processed food consumption to metabolic issues and inflammation, though many studies are observational and require further research to confirm causality.

  23. Does your phone drain your brain when it is silent?

    Even if you do not touch the device, it occupies your mental resources. The evidence on this is mixed, however, as some larger studies find no significant effect.

    Established. The evidence is mixed, with some studies showing a 'brain drain' effect on working memory and cognitive capacity while others find no statistically significant impact.

  24. Is your sleep actually impairing your brain?

    People with narcolepsy type 1 and idiopathic hypersomnia show large impairments in attention, with effect sizes of -0.90 and -0.97 respectively.

    Established. The evidence shows that while chronic hypersomnolence is linked to significant cognitive deficits, strategic napping can be beneficial for performance provided it avoids the period of sleep inertia.

  25. Who is your leader really serving?

    Research links dark triad traits to profit manipulation and bullying rather than direct promotion rates. The evidence on how these traits impact corporate structures is mixed.

    Established. The evidence shows that dark triad traits are associated with opportunistic behaviors like profit manipulation and bullying, and can be beneficial for internationalization, but it does not directly measure promotion rates.

  26. Does your brain actually need the hippocampus to find your way?

    Evidence suggests that the hippocampus might not be required for the storage or recovery of a spatial layout experienced long before an injury.

    Established. The sources do not provide evidence that modern cities atrophy the hippocampus; instead, they suggest the hippocampus might not be required for long-term spatial storage and highlight other brain structures involved in navigation.

  27. Is your probiotic helping the wrong bacteria?

    The most popular probiotic, Lactobacillus, can harbor genes that protect bacteria from antibiotics. These genes can then move from the probiotic to harmful bacteria in your body.

    Supported. The evidence shows that Lactobacillus probiotics can harbor and transfer antibiotic resistance genes to pathogens, particularly in laboratory settings.

  28. Does doing good make it easier to do wrong?

    When you do something good, you may feel entitled to act poorly later. This effect is stronger when people are watching you. The evidence for this is mixed and some studies have failed to replicate it.

    Established. The evidence shows that while moral licensing exists and is influenced by social observation and concrete thinking, its effects are inconsistent across different studies and replication attempts.

  29. Does your diet change your internal biology?

    Western diets reshape the specific types of bacteria in your gut without necessarily reducing the total variety of species present.

    Supported. The evidence shows that industrialization and Western diets significantly reshape the specific types of microbes present in the human gut, but it does not confirm a systematic reduction in overall microbial diversity.

  30. Who actually decides how your city grows?

    The evidence shows that elite capture influences urban planning, welfare distribution, and oral health policy, but it does not specifically address pharmaceutical profits.

    Established. The sources confirm that elite capture is a recurring mechanism that undermines policy intent in various sectors like urban planning, welfare, and oral health, but they do not specifically address the pharmaceutical profit motive.

  31. Is your screen eroding your ability to read a room?

    Technology is being used to help people implement real-time social interaction.

    Established. The evidence confirms that digital communication lacks real-time non-verbal cues which can hinder emotional understanding, but it also shows that technology is being developed to simulate or facilitate these interactions.

  32. Who knows your daily routine better than you?

    Smart home technology can effortlessly collect data on your presence and the time you spend on specific activities. The evidence on this is mixed regarding long-term clinical effects.

    Supported. The evidence shows that smart home technology enables granular behavioral tracking and prediction, though its direct impact on clinical outcomes remains unproven and user awareness is not a strong driver of security behavior.
