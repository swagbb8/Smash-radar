  2. Is the bot actually being sincere?

    Established. The sources show that human-computer interaction can improve learning and cooperation, but they do not provide evidence that it desensitizes humans to emotional nuances.

  4. Who is the park actually for?

    The priority for children's hang-out spots around schools is determined more by safety and surveillance than by access to nature.

    Established. The evidence shows that urban green spaces provide diverse ecological and health benefits, but their practical use by certain populations is heavily influenced by surveillance and safety factors.

  5. Does your brain choose survival over you?

    There is no data linking the brain's survival instincts to the suppression of your personal identity.

    Supported. The provided sources do not contain information regarding the brain's prioritization of survival over personal identity.

  6. Did your house design decide your role?

    Archaeological evidence shows men and women shared domestic spaces and performed various tasks interchangeably.

    Established. The sources provide mixed evidence, with some highlighting the potential for domestic spaces to reinforce gender norms while others provide historical evidence of shared domestic spaces.

  7. Is your personality actually yours?

    Active engagement in daily routines designed to meet cultural goals creates specific neural patterns in the brain. These patterns form the basis of your sense of self.

    Established. The evidence shows that the self is constructed through social, cultural, and relational processes, including caregiver communication and cultural neural patterning.

  8. Does your diet dictate how you treat people?

    Differences in gut microbiome profiles are linked to prosocial behavior in rats. This evidence is currently emerging.

    Established. The sources establish a link between gut microbiota, stress, and social behavior in animal models, but much of the human data is currently limited to pubertal timing and general cognitive health.

  9. Can a computer predict your next move?

    The use of these tools can make it difficult for law enforcement to provide an account of their decisions in important ways.

    Established. The sources confirm that predictive modeling is used for governance and behavior analysis, but emphasize that these systems are mediated by human judgment and lack rigorous independent validation.

  10. Was the weekend actually invented for your rest?

  11. Does your solitude leave a physical mark?

    Studies on animal models show that isolation and stress lead to structural changes in the hippocampus. Human data on the prefrontal cortex remains unconfirmed.

    Established. The evidence shows that isolation and stress can cause structural changes and atrophy in the hippocampus of animal models, but human data on specific brain regions like the prefrontal cortex is missing.

  12. Does your search for the perfect partner create the conflict you want to avoid?

    While you seek depth, this specific expectation correlates with friction in the relationship. This finding is based on observational studies and does not establish a causal link.

    Supported. The evidence shows that romantic idealism and mutual emotional support are associated with positive relationship qualities, but specific idealistic beliefs about meeting all needs are linked to conflict.

  13. Does school food actually cause metabolic issues?

    While children often consume low fiber and high calories, there is a lack of clinical data to confirm exactly how specific fiber-to-calorie ratios in school meals affect insulin resistance.

    Supported. The sources confirm that children often have low fiber intake and that school programs lack plant-based policies, but they do not provide specific data on the fiber-to-calorie ratios of government-subsidized meals.

  14. Do you love what you made, or just the effort you spent?

    Supported. The evidence confirms that effort and friction are linked to psychological ownership in creative tasks, but it does not address whether this leads to overvaluing poorly made physical goods.

  15. Does predictive policing actually create racial bias?

    A randomized trial found no significant differences in the proportion of arrests by racial-ethnic group during these deployments.

    Established. The evidence is mixed, showing that while runaway feedback loops are a theoretical and modeled risk, empirical data from a randomized trial did not show significant racial bias in arrests.

  16. Do you need a partner who is just like you?

    Supported. The evidence suggests that similarity in status or personality is not the primary driver of marital satisfaction, and in some cases, differences are more relevant.

  17. Is your work autonomy an illusion?

    Established. The sources confirm that IoT environments can track and predict behavior, but they highlight significant vulnerabilities in current privacy protections and a lack of implemented solutions.

  19. Whose values are you actually living out?

    People may develop their self-perception based on algorithmically amplified feedback rather than their own values.

    Supported. The evidence shows that algorithmic feedback loops and notifications are linked to neurological changes, identity fragmentation, and significant cognitive costs like lost focus time.

    Research shows these systems are designed to maximize your screen time. These findings are currently supported by evidence.

    Established. The evidence confirms that social media algorithms target reward centers and that addiction involves neural sensitization to 'wanting' rather than 'liking,' but specific neuroimaging for social media remains limited.

  22. Does your room stay dark enough for your genes?

  23. Are you trapped in a filter bubble?

    Established. The evidence is mixed, with some studies finding no proof of filter bubbles and others identifying specific platforms that correlate with higher diversity.

  24. Does discrimination always leave a physical trace?

    Established. The evidence shows a complex and inconsistent relationship between discrimination and allostatic load, influenced by demographic groups, specific types of discrimination, and individual coping mechanisms.

    The evidence is mixed. While high-status areas offer environmental benefits like cooler temperatures, they are linked to specific behavioral risks and heightened physiological reactivity in certain groups.

    Established. The evidence shows that affluence is associated with both specific behavioral risks and distinct physiological and environmental benefits, rather than a uniform psychological cost.

  29. Does your body remember every moment of pain?

    Epidural analgesia significantly reduces the stress response and pain syndrome during natural delivery.

    Supported. The evidence focuses on clinical and physiological responses to specific stressors like labor and social tests rather than historical labor structures.

    Established. The evidence is mixed, showing that while multitasking can impair cognitive outcomes and increase susceptibility to interference, it is also associated with improved task-switching abilities in certain contexts.
