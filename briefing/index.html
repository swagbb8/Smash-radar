  1. Who knows your daily routine better than you?

    Supported. The evidence shows that smart home technology enables granular behavioral tracking and prediction, though its direct impact on clinical outcomes remains unproven and user awareness is not a strong driver of security behavior.

  2. Is your craving a choice or a physical rewiring?

    Chronic substance use creates maladaptive neuroplasticity and brain inflammation that alter your reward and decision-making circuitry.

    Established. The evidence shows that substance use causes structural and functional changes in the brain's reward and executive systems through maladaptive neuroplasticity and inflammation.

  3. Does talking actually solve your problems?

    Certain facets of alexithymia are linked to lower reports of rumination and social sharing.

    Supported. The sources do not provide evidence that talking about problems reinforces negative neural pathways; instead, they show varied emotional processing and regulation strategies.

  4. Is your flow state just a distraction?

    The experience is not just high arousal. It depends on a balance between your skill and the task challenge.

    Supported. The evidence shows that flow is a complex state involving dopaminergic function and reward structures, but it also requires specific cognitive balances and sensory-motor simulation.

  5. Is your feed actually showing you everything?

    While some studies show personalization can match human editorial variety, internet customization effectively isolates individuals from diverse opinions.

    Established. The evidence is mixed, with some studies confirming the existence of filter bubbles and others finding that the effects on news diversity may be exaggerated or manageable.

  7. Does low-fat dairy really cause weight gain?

    Established. The sources do not provide evidence that low-fat dairy products specifically cause weight gain; instead, they suggest that a low-fat diet with high fiber and protein is a supported strategy for weight management.

  8. Does social rejection actually hurt like a burn?

    A statistical analysis of 244 participants failed to support the claim that social rejection operates on the same pain matrix as physical pain.

    Established. The evidence suggests that social pain is more complex than a simple literal activation of the physical pain matrix, as studies failed to find a consistent overlap with nociceptive stimuli.

    Supported. The sources provide evidence that Green Revolution Technologies are linked to significant environmental and soil degradation in India and other regions.

    Established. The evidence shows that strict and harsh parenting styles are linked to internalizing symptoms, but it also highlights that permissive parenting and parental dissimilarity contribute to these problems.

    Established. The evidence does not support a universal link between homeownership and improved health, as the effects are inconsistent across different demographics and socioeconomic conditions.
